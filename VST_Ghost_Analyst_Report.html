2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Retail Electricity (~50%): TXU Energy and other retail brands serving residential and commercial customers in deregulated Texas market. Provides stable, margin-accretive revenue.
Wholesale Generation (~50%): 41 GW fleet selling into wholesale power markets. Nuclear (Comanche Peak), natural gas (largest U.S. fleet), solar, and battery storage. Revenue varies with power prices and demand.

Vistra’s integrated model — owning both generation assets and retail customers — is a natural hedge. When wholesale power prices rise, generation profits increase but retail margins compress. When prices fall, the opposite occurs. This vertical integration smooths earnings volatility and provides structural advantages over pure generators or pure retailers.

The Comanche Peak nuclear plant is Vistra’s crown jewel for the AI power thesis. At 2.4 GW, it provides enough carbon-free baseload to power multiple hyperscaler data centers simultaneously. While CEG has the larger nuclear fleet (21 reactors), Vistra’s nuclear assets combined with the largest natural gas fleet in the U.S. provide a diversified power supply that data center operators increasingly demand. The gas fleet also benefits from the Iran conflict’s impact on natural gas pricing.
