1Investment Thesis
  • A high-quality compounder at a 52-week low — a valuation reset, not a broken business. Netflix trades at $72.88, down 46% from its split-adjusted high of $134 and sitting at its 52-week low. But the business is executing: revenue grew 16% YoY in Q1 to $12.25B, operating margins are 32%, free cash flow guidance was raised to $12.5B, and the company authorized a combined $31.8B in buybacks — more than its entire 2026 content budget. This is a multiple compression driven by sentiment and M&A overhang, not deteriorating fundamentals.
  • The market is pricing Netflix like a slow-growth utility — at 19x forward and ~26x free cash flow. At a forward P/E of 19x and roughly 26x 2026 free cash flow, Netflix is valued in line with a mature, low-growth business. Yet it is guiding to 12-14% revenue growth with expanding margins. Businesses growing at that rate with these margins have historically traded at 30-42x earnings. The gap between the valuation and the fundamentals is the opportunity — and the analyst consensus agrees, with a $112 target sitting 53% above the current price.
  • The ad tier is an underappreciated second engine, and the M&A overhang is lifting. The advertising business reached 250M+ monthly active viewers and 4,000+ advertisers (up 70% YoY), with ad revenue on track to double to $3B in 2026. Meanwhile, the two overhangs that compressed the multiple — the failed Warner Bros bid (which netted a $2.8B termination fee) and the missed Roku acquisition — are now resolved. With M&A uncertainty clearing and a clean quarter due July 16, the catalyst for re-rating is in sight.

    4Price Action & The Valuation Disconnect

    Netflix peaked near $134 (split-adjusted) in mid-2025 and has declined 46% to a 52-week low of $72.88 through a series of sentiment-driven, not fundamentals-driven, events. The decline accelerated recently: down ~20% in the past three months alone. The 10:1 stock split in November 2025 (chart adjusted) coincided with the start of the M&A saga that has weighed on the multiple ever since.

    The revenue chart tells the real story: uninterrupted growth from $10.25B to $12.25B per quarter. The business never stopped executing. What changed was the multiple — Netflix re-rated from a high-growth disruptor (35-40x) to a mature business (19x) as investors demanded proof that growth could continue without aggressive M&A. The disconnect between a 16%-growing business and a 19x forward multiple is the core of the BUY thesis.

    5Anatomy of the Decline: Why It Fell (and Why It's Sentiment)

    Understanding the decline is essential to the thesis, because the engine's discipline requires distinguishing a valuation reset from structural impairment. Netflix's decline is the former. It unfolded in distinct, identifiable phases:

    Phase 1 (Apr 16): Q1 beat, light guide. Revenue and EPS beat, but Q2 guidance ($12.57B rev, $0.78 EPS) came in just below consensus, and management declined to raise full-year guidance after the beat. Co-founder Reed Hastings simultaneously stepped down from the board. The stock fell ~10%. Phase 2 (Feb-Jun): the M&A overhang. Netflix bid ~$83B for Warner Bros, was outbid by Paramount Skydance, declined to match, and collected a $2.8B termination fee. Then on June 16 it missed out on Roku (Fox bought it for $22B). Two failed acquisitions created a 'strategic direction' concern and an 'acquisition hangover discount.'

    Critically, none of these are fundamental problems. Revenue kept growing 16%. Margins kept expanding. FCF guidance was raised. The market simply re-rated the multiple downward on (a) the end of hyper-growth expectations and (b) uncertainty about capital allocation after two failed deals. Per our framework's decision tree, a decline driven by valuation reset and sentiment — with intact fundamentals — supports a BUY, distinct from a structural/competitive decline which would warrant a HOLD.

    The resolution path is clear and known: a clean quarter with no acquisition rumors and no guidance miss. That quarter is likely Q2, reporting July 16. The overhangs are now resolved (both deals are dead), removing the source of the discount.

    6Business Model & Competitive Position

    Netflix is the clear global leader in streaming with ~300M paid memberships and only ~5% of global TV viewing time — a long runway. Its moat is a virtuous cycle: the largest subscriber base funds the largest content budget (~$20B/year), which drives engagement, which attracts subscribers. No competitor matches this scale. Disney+, Max, and Paramount+ are all subscale and, in several cases, still unprofitable in streaming. Netflix is the only streamer that has definitively translated scale into profit.

    The advertising tier is the underappreciated second engine. At 250M+ MAUs and 4,000+ advertisers (up 70% YoY), ad revenue is on track to double to $3B in 2026. This monetizes the password-sharing crackdown cohort and price-sensitive users while opening a high-margin revenue stream entirely separate from subscription growth. Live sports (World Baseball Classic, NFL) and gaming further diversify engagement. The thesis does not require subscriber hyper-growth — it requires monetizing the existing 300M base better, which the ad tier does directly.

    8Management & Capital Structure

    Co-CEOs Ted Sarandos and Greg Peters lead operations, with Jay Hoag now chairman following Reed Hastings' departure from the board. The leadership transition created near-term sentiment noise but does not change the operating trajectory — the co-CEO structure has been in place and executing for over a year. The decision to walk away from both Warner Bros (at the required price) and Roku reflects capital discipline that the market should ultimately reward, even if it created short-term 'strategic direction' anxiety.

    The balance sheet is a weapon. Netflix authorized a combined $31.8B in share buybacks — exceeding its entire ~$20B annual content budget — and is generating $12.5B in free cash flow. With the stock at a 52-week low, every dollar of buyback is highly accretive. Insider activity is balanced (disposed:acquired ratio ~1.05, far healthier than the momentum names), and the ~$130M of recent insider selling is routine scheduled executive compensation, not a signal of concern.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    We would move to STRONG BUY if: (1) Q2 on July 16 delivers a clean beat with no guidance cut, confirming the overhang is the only thing holding the stock down; (2) the ad tier revenue trajectory accelerates beyond the $3B path; or (3) the stock holds the $72 level and begins re-rating as M&A fears fully dissipate.

    We would move to HOLD if: management announces another large, dilutive acquisition (reviving the capital-allocation concern), revenue growth decelerates below 12%, or operating margins guide below 30%. The thesis rests on Netflix being a disciplined, growing, cash-generative compounder; a return to aggressive empire-building M&A would undermine the core argument. The falling-knife technical means patience and scaling in are warranted — but at 19x forward / 26x FCF with the entire Street above the price, the risk/reward is firmly favorable.

    14Rating & Conclusion

    Netflix is the highest-quality business we have initiated this cycle trading at its most attractive valuation in years. Revenue is growing 16%, operating margins are 32% and expanding, free cash flow guidance was raised to $12.5B, the balance sheet is pristine (Altman Z 9.4, Piotroski 8/9), and management is buying back $31.8B of stock at a 52-week low. The ad tier is a genuine second engine scaling toward $3B. This is not a broken business.

    The decline is a valuation reset plus an M&A overhang — both of which are resolving. The failed Warner Bros and Roku deals are dead, removing the 'acquisition hangover discount,' and a clean Q2 print on July 16 is the known catalyst for re-rating. At 19x forward and 26x free cash flow, the market is pricing Netflix like a no-growth utility; the analyst consensus ($112, 53% above the price) and our own work say that is a mistake.

    BUY — conviction 76/100, target $105 (+44%). A high-quality compounder at a 52-week low, 53% below consensus, with resolving overhangs and a clear July 16 catalyst. The weak technical (falling knife) argues for scaling in rather than a single entry, but this is the rare setup where fundamentals and Street sentiment both point firmly higher while the price sits at its lows. The opposite of the momentum names — here, the discount is the opportunity.
