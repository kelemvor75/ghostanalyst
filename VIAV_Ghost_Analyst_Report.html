1Investment Thesis
  • The re-rating is the trade that already happened. VIAV has gone from a ~$9 cyclical laggard to a $40–55 AI-infrastructure name — a 4–6x move — on a genuine data-center and defense tailwind. At ~32x FY27E and ~44x FY26E non-GAAP EPS, with GAAP earnings sitting near zero, the entire “cheap cyclical → secular winner” gap is now priced inside the multiple. The easy money was the re-rating; from here the stock has to out-execute an already-optimistic consensus.
  • Roughly half the headline growth is bought, not organic. NSE revenue rose 54.4% year-over-year in the March quarter — but ~$54M of that came from the Spirent acquisition. Ex-Spirent, organic NSE growth was closer to the high-20s%. Consensus already models the step-down to ~19% total growth in FY27 once Spirent anniversaries; a “growth-optics” air-pocket looms in early FY27 even if the underlying business stays healthy.
  • The people closest to the story are de-risking into strength. Management issued ~$575M of equity at $45 to retire a 7% term loan it was not forced to touch (VIAVI held ~$499M of cash). Insiders are net sellers 16:2 with zero open-market purchases, and the options market is paying ~100% implied volatility with net-long puts. All of this is rational — and none of it is the behavior of people who think the currency is cheap.
  • The tailwind is real; so is the cyclicality. The 400G → 800G → 1.6T optical and Ultra-Ethernet upgrade cycle and the aerospace/defense PNT build-out are genuine multi-year drivers, and VIAVI holds real product leadership (1.6T TestCenter, first-to-market Ultra Ethernet Transport validation). But test-and-measurement is violently cyclical: as recently as FY23, revenue fell 21.5% and the NSE segment cratered when network-equipment and semiconductor R&D budgets pulled back. This business has done it before.
  • Fairly valued today; a wide, cycle-dependent range. Our $44 base case is ~fair value. The bull case ($66) approaches the Street if the AI-capex super-cycle persists; the bear case ($27) reflects a digestion-driven earnings miss plus multiple compression. That distribution — not the point estimate — is the message. We respect the tape but will not chase a name 4–6x off its lows into a Strong-Buy consensus whose $64 average target was struck 20–30% above today’s price.

    • The differentiated call. The market is treating VIAV as a linear AI-infrastructure winner and extrapolating a 40%+ growth rate that is partly acquired onto a business with a documented history of sharp down-cycles. We think the more useful question is not direction but durability and price: the re-rating has already delivered the outsized return, valuation is now full on non-GAAP and meaningless on GAAP, and the marginal signals from management, insiders, and the options tape all lean cautious. That combination lands us at HOLD — constructive on the franchise, unwilling to pay up after the move.

    4Price Action & The Re-Rating

    VIAVI is one of 2026’s cleaner “left-for-dead cyclical re-rates as an AI beneficiary” stories. The stock troughed in the single digits, then compounded through a sequence of catalysts: the October 2025 close of the Spirent test-asset acquisition, a strong December quarter, a decisive Q3 beat-and-raise on April 29 (+42.8% revenue), and index-buying tied to the June 22 promotion into the S&P MidCap 400. It peaked near $55 on May 1.

    Two things then broke the ascent. First, on May 19 the company priced a ~$575M equity offering at $45 — a discount to the ~$50 pre-deal price — to repay its term loan; secondaries into a vertical move reliably cap momentum, and this one did. Second, the broader high-multiple complex cooled. The result is a stock that is −26% from its early-May closing peak (−32% from the $60.43 intraday high on April 30), ~17% below its 50-day moving average, yet still ~36% above its 200-day. In plain terms: the multi-month uptrend is intact, but the short-term momentum has been distributed and is now repairing. For a momentum-oriented investor, the tape is no longer confirming an entry — it is asking for either a lower price or a fresh re-acceleration above the 50-day before re-engaging.

    5The AI-Datacenter Test Franchise & the ‘Bought Growth’ Question

    The franchise. VIAVI’s Network & Service Enablement (NSE) segment — ~79% of revenue — sells the test, monitoring, and validation gear that network-equipment makers, chip vendors, hyperscalers, and carriers use to build and run high-speed infrastructure. This is where the AI story lives: every generation of data-center interconnect (400G, now 800G, next 1.6T) must be validated in the lab and in production, and each step up in speed increases test complexity and test intensity. VIAVI has genuine leadership here — a 1.6T TestCenter platform, the industry’s first Ultra Ethernet Transport validation solution for AI fabrics, and a widening set of AI-fabric and optical tools. The October 2025 acquisition of Spirent’s high-speed-ethernet, network-security, and channel-emulation lines (a DOJ-mandated divestiture from Keysight’s purchase of Spirent) bolted a premium HSE/security franchise directly onto that platform.

    The ‘bought growth’ question — the crux of our call. The March quarter’s +54.4% NSE growth is the number bulls anchor to. But Spirent contributed $54.2M of NSE revenue that quarter. Strip it out and organic NSE growth was roughly in the high-20s% — still very good, and clearly AI- and defense-driven, but not 54%. This matters for two reasons. First, the market’s ~44x FY26 / ~32x FY27 multiple implicitly treats the blended 40%+ rate as the run-rate. Second, consensus itself models the truth: FY27 revenue growth decelerates to ~19% as the Spirent contribution laps. When that anniversary hits (the December 2026 quarter), reported growth mechanically steps down — and if AI-capex is digesting at the same moment, an otherwise-healthy business could print a scary-looking deceleration.

    The cyclicality the AI narrative papers over. It is easy to forget, three quarters into an up-cycle, that this is a deeply cyclical business. In FY23, VIAVI revenue fell 21.5% year-over-year and NSE collapsed when network-equipment and semiconductor R&D spend pulled back; the FY24 trough carried negative GAAP operating income. Lab-and-production test is early-cycle demand — among the first line items cut when customers tighten. We are not forecasting a downturn; AI-fabric build-out is real and, for now, accelerating. We are pointing out that a stock priced for durable secular growth is underwriting away a risk this specific company has realized before, and recently.

    Net: The AI-datacenter test franchise is real, well-positioned, and genuinely growing organically in the high-20s%. But the headline 50%+ growth overstates the sustainable rate, the multiple prices durability the cycle has not yet demonstrated, and the Spirent anniversary sets up an optical air-pocket in early FY27. Bullish on the business; unwilling to pay for perfection.

    The two-segment structure is a deliberate barbell. NSE is the growth and cyclicality engine — lower-margin, capex-sensitive, and now the home of the Spirent HSE/security assets and the Inertial Labs position-navigation-timing (PNT) business that serves GPS-denied aerospace and defense applications. OSP is the ballast: a high-margin (~38% operating margin), cash-generative franchise built on optical coatings, currency anti-counterfeiting pigments, and 3D-sensing filters used in consumer devices. OSP is lumpier (currency-redesign timing, consumer-electronics cycles) but structurally very profitable, and it underwrites the model when NSE turns down.

    Geographic mix is well-balanced — roughly Americas ~$0.4B, Asia-Pacific ~$0.4B, EMEA ~$0.3B on a trailing basis — which diversifies end-market risk but also exposes VIAVI to tariff and export-control cross-currents in the test-and-measurement supply chain.

    Competition. VIAVI competes primarily with Keysight Technologies (the larger, higher-margin, broader T&M platform — and, post-Spirent, an even more direct rival at the high end), EXFO, and Anritsu in various niches, alongside in-house test tools at the largest customers. The Spirent HSE deal is best read as VIAVI buying its way to relevance in the fastest-growing (AI-fabric/ethernet/security) corner of the market before Keysight consolidated it. It is strategically sound; it also raises integration and customer-overlap execution risk that the current multiple leaves little room to fumble.

    7Financial Model, Valuation & the DCF Caveat

    Basis. GAAP earnings are near zero — the March quarter’s $0.03 GAAP EPS versus $0.27 non-GAAP tells the story — because acquisition-driven intangible amortization, stock compensation, and restructuring dominate the ~$61M GAAP-to-non-GAAP bridge. A GAAP P/E is therefore not meaningful; the whole valuation rests on non-GAAP forward estimates and the assumption that the amortization is genuinely ‘non-economic.’ We anchor on FY27E non-GAAP EPS of $1.29 (consensus), one year out, past the noisiest integration quarters but before the FY28 numbers require heroic assumptions.

    The DCF caveat — and we hold a bullish DCF to the same standard as a bearish one. Our data engine’s discounted-cash-flow output for VIAVI is effectively $1 — useless, and correctly so. DCF is reliable for stable, mature free-cash-flow generators; it is not for a cyclical serial-acquirer with a 0.5% trailing FCF yield, an ~85x trailing EV/EBITDA, and cash flows swinging on integration timing and working capital. We therefore do not use DCF as an anchor in either direction — not to manufacture downside, and not to justify a target. The multiple-based scenario grid above is the honest frame, and it says the same thing the DCF caveat implies: there is no deep margin of safety here at ~32x FY27E.
    8Management, Capital Allocation & the Equity Raise

    Leadership. President & CEO Oleg Khaykin and CFO Ilan Daskal have run the deliberate transformation of the old JDS Uniphase into a focused test-and-measurement plus optical business — shrinking, refocusing, and then, over the past 18 months, acquiring into the AI-fabric and defense-navigation growth verticals (Inertial Labs for PNT; the ~$425M Spirent HSE/security/channel-emulation deal for NSE).

    The capital-structure judo — underappreciated, and a point in management’s favor. VIAVI funded the Spirent deal with a $600M term loan in October 2025 (~7% cost). It then prepaid $150M in the March quarter and, in May, issued ~$575M of equity at $45 to retire the remaining $450M. Read cynically, that is dilution near a peak. Read correctly, it is opportunistic balance-sheet management: the company used an inflated equity currency — the stock had 4–6x’d — to extinguish expensive debt it was not forced to touch (it held ~$499M of cash), leaving it roughly net-cash on financial debt and free to redeploy. Combined with U.S. NOLs that hold the blended non-GAAP tax rate in the mid-teens, the financial engineering here is genuinely good. It is also, tellingly, an admission by management that the currency was rich — which is part of why we are not chasing.

    Insider activity. The signal cuts the other way at the personal level: insiders disposed versus acquired 16:2, with zero open-market purchases, including CEO stock sales in early May framed as post-run profit-taking (largely pre-scheduled 10b5-1 plans). None of this is alarming on its own after a move like this — but the complete absence of insider buying at these levels is worth naming, and it is consistent with the corporate-level decision to sell equity.

    11Analyst Sentiment

    Sell-side sentiment is decisively bullish and was reset sharply higher after the Q3 beat. The consensus rating is Strong Buy — roughly 7 Buy · 1 Hold · 0 Sell among recent covering analysts — with an average 12-month target around $64 (recent covering-analyst range ~$60–70; the full range across all analysts spans a stale $24 low to a $70 high). The magnitude of the target revisions is the tell: Susquehanna moved from $25 to $65, Stifel from $35 to $60, B. Riley from $53 to $63, with Needham at $68, Rosenblatt at $70, and UBS the notable holdout at $60 with a Neutral rating.

    Two caveats keep us from simply importing the Street’s number. First, most of these targets were struck in late April–June when the stock traded ~$48–55; it is now ~$41, well below where the bullish case was underwritten, and the targets have not all been refreshed for the dilution. Second, the consensus $64 implies roughly 50x FY27E or ~35x FY28E non-GAAP EPS — a multiple that prices durable secular growth into a business we view as cyclically-exposed and partly inorganically-grown. Our $44 base sits below the Street specifically because we apply a cyclical-appropriate ~34x to FY27E rather than extrapolating the acquisition-boosted growth rate. Where the Street sees a linear compounder, we see a good franchise at a full price after a large move — a difference of multiple philosophy, not of facts.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    What moves us to BUY:

    • A valuation reset toward $30–35 (~24–27x FY27E) that restores a real margin of safety and re-skews the risk/reward upward without any deterioration in the franchise.
    • A confirmed technical re-acceleration — a decisive reclaim of the 50-day on volume — that says the distribution is complete and the primary uptrend has resumed.
    • Evidence that organic NSE growth is sustaining above ~25% after the Spirent anniversary, i.e., the secular-winner thesis is real rather than acquisition-flattered.
    • Insiders shifting from selling to net buying at current levels.

    What moves us to SELL:

    • Early signals of AI-capex digestion (hyperscaler capex guidance cuts, NEM/chip R&D softening) hitting VIAVI’s early-cycle lab/production demand.
    • A guide-down or a materially negative organic-growth print as Spirent laps, revealing the underlying rate is weaker than the headline implied.
    • The multiple holding above ~40x FY26E while growth decelerates — the classic late-cycle setup where price and fundamentals diverge.
    14Rating & Conclusion

    HOLD, $44 target (~fair value). VIAVI is a genuinely good business at a genuinely full price. The AI-datacenter test franchise is well-positioned and growing organically in the high-20s%; the Spirent deal was strategically smart and financially well-executed; the balance sheet is now clean; and A&D/PNT adds a second secular leg. We are not bearish on the company.

    We are, however, unwilling to chase it. The 4–6x re-rating has already delivered the outsized return that the AI thesis was worth; the stock now trades at ~44x FY26 / ~32x FY27 non-GAAP with near-zero GAAP earnings; roughly half the headline growth is acquired and sets up a deceleration optic in early FY27; and the marginal signals — management issuing equity at the top, insiders selling 16:2 with zero buys, ~100% options IV skewed to puts — all point the same cautious direction. This is a cyclical business wearing a secular multiple, and the people closest to it are trimming into strength.

    For a momentum-oriented investor, the actionable message is timing, not thesis: the primary uptrend is intact but the short-term momentum has been distributed — the stock has broken below its 50-day and now sits ~26% off its early-May peak — and the risk/reward at ~$41 is balanced. The ~5% pullback since Friday improves the entry at the margin (base-case upside is now ~+8% rather than ~+2%), but it clears neither of our re-engagement triggers. We would re-engage on either a valuation reset toward $30–35 or a confirmed re-acceleration above the 50-day — and we would turn cautious quickly on the first hard evidence that the AI-capex cycle is digesting. Until one of those tips the balance, the disciplined call on a name that has run this far is to wait.

    Bottom line: A high-quality AI-infrastructure and defense-test franchise, fairly valued after a spectacular run. Respect the tape, name the cyclicality and the full multiple plainly, and require a better price or a fresh signal before paying up. HOLD.
