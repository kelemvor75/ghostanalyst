1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

GEV has been volatile — ranging from $300 to $900+ since the GE spinoff in April 2024. The stock currently trades at $873, near its highs, after surging on the Q4 earnings beat and the Rothschild upgrade. Revenue acceleration from $8B to $11B in four quarters reflects the power infrastructure buildout. The FY26E EPS dip (-16%) reflects lapping of one-time items in Q4 FY25, not fundamental weakness — FY27E growth of +54% is the real trajectory.

The electrification thesis is straightforward: AI data centers are consuming electricity at unprecedented rates. Every new hyperscaler facility needs gas turbines, grid connections, transformers, and switchgear. GE Vernova builds all of these. The $240B market cap reflects the market’s recognition that this is not a traditional industrial company — it’s an AI infrastructure play disguised as a power equipment manufacturer.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Power (~45%): Gas turbines (HA class — world’s most efficient), steam turbines, nuclear services, and aeroderivative turbines. Growing as data centers drive gas peaker and baseload demand.
Wind (~25%): Onshore and offshore wind turbines. Historically challenged (losses, quality issues) but improving under new management post-spinoff. Selective bidding strategy prioritizing margins over volume.
Electrification (~30%): Grid equipment, transformers, high-voltage switchgear, and power conversion. The fastest-growing segment as grid modernization accelerates to support AI power demand.

6  |  ANALYST SENTIMENT

33 analysts cover GEV: 6 Strong Buy, 23 Buy, 4 Hold, 0 Sell — 88% buy-side consensus. Consensus target of $840 (median $849, high $1,087). Stock at $873 trades slightly above consensus. Rothschild & Co reversed from Sell to Buy on March 9 — a dramatic reversal. Wells Fargo (Overweight, Mar 31), Morgan Stanley (Overweight, Mar 23), RBC (Outperform), and Evercore (Outperform) all maintain. Citigroup Neutral is the holdout. FY27E EPS $22.74 (+54%) is the key growth year.

The electrification supercycle driving GE Vernova’s growth is not speculative — it’s physics. U.S. electricity demand grew less than 1% annually for the past decade. The U.S. Department of Energy now projects demand growth of 3-5% annually through 2035, driven by AI data centers, electric vehicle charging, industrial reshoring, and building electrification. This 3-5x acceleration in demand growth requires massive investment in power generation and grid infrastructure — exactly what GE Vernova builds.

The Q4 FY2025 EPS of $13.28 includes one-time items that inflate the FY2025 base, making the FY2026E comparison appear negative (-16%). Stripping out the one-time items, the underlying business is growing 20-25% annually. FY2027E EPS of $22.74 (+54%) represents the clean growth trajectory as the gas turbine and grid equipment backlogs convert to revenue. Investors should focus on FY2027E, not the optically challenging FY2026 comparison.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from BUY if:

Gas turbine orders decline 20%+ for two quarters. Would signal the power generation buildout is peaking.
Wind segment losses widen after improvement trend. Would indicate the turnaround strategy is failing.
Grid equipment delivery times extend beyond 3 years. Would signal capacity constraints that competitors could exploit.
Electricity demand growth falls below projections. Would undermine the core electrification thesis.
