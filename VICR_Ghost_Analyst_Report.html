1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

VICR has surged from $30 to $158 over the past 12 months — a 427% move as the market discovered GPU power delivery as a critical AI infrastructure bottleneck. The stock is volatile, with 30-40% swings in both directions driven by lumpy quarterly revenue that makes trend analysis difficult from any single quarter.

The Q3 FY2025 revenue spike to $410M (vs $90-140M in surrounding quarters) was likely a large GPU power module deployment for a hyperscaler. This kind of lumpiness is normal for a $7B company selling high-value modules in large batches — but it makes traditional quarterly analysis misleading. The right framework is trailing twelve months and forward estimates, not individual quarters.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Advanced Products (~70%): High-density power conversion modules using Factorized Power Architecture (FPA). Includes Point-of-Load (PoL) converters, bus converters, and current multipliers for GPU, CPU, and AI accelerator power delivery. The growth engine.
Brick Products (~30%): Traditional DC-DC power converters for industrial, defense, and telecom applications. Mature, stable, providing a cash flow base.

Vicor’s Factorized Power Architecture is a genuinely differentiated technology. Traditional Voltage Regulator Modules (VRMs) lose significant energy as heat when delivering 700W+ to a GPU. Vicor’s approach factorizes the power conversion into separate regulation and transformation stages, achieving 97%+ efficiency at power densities that competitors cannot match. This isn’t marketing — it’s physics. When a GPU draws 700W, even a 2% efficiency improvement saves 14W of waste heat per chip, multiplied across thousands of GPUs in a data center.

The competitive moat is protected by over 500 patents and the practical reality that qualifying a power module for a GPU platform takes 12-18 months. Once Vicor’s modules are designed into an NVIDIA or AMD reference architecture, they’re locked in for the product lifecycle. The 55% gross margin reflects this pricing power — customers pay for performance, not commodity conversion.
