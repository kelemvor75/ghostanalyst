1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

TSM peaked at $400+ in late 2025 before pulling back to $338 — a 15% decline driven by Iran conflict geopolitical risk and the broader tech selloff. The revenue trajectory tells the real story: $26.4B → $29.4B → $30.6B → $32.6B in four quarters, each setting new records. Q1 2026 estimates of ~$27.5B reflect seasonal dip but the full-year trajectory remains strongly upward.

The Taiwan geopolitical risk is the elephant in the room and the primary reason TSM trades at a discount to its quality. The Iran conflict has amplified this risk by demonstrating how quickly regional conflicts can disrupt global supply chains. However, TSMC’s Arizona fab, Japan fab, and European investments are gradually diversifying production away from Taiwan — reducing (though not eliminating) the geographic concentration risk.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Smartphone (~35%): Apple A-series and M-series chips, Qualcomm Snapdragon, MediaTek. Seasonal but large. iPhone 17 cycle in H2 2026 is a tailwind.
HPC / AI (~35%, fastest growing): NVIDIA GPUs (Blackwell, Vera Rubin), AMD MI450, Google TPU, custom ASICs for hyperscalers. Growing 3x company average. N3E and N2 nodes are sold out through 2027.
IoT / Auto / Other (~30%): Automotive chips, industrial IoT, consumer electronics. Mature but provides diversification and downside protection.

TSMC’s moat is the most formidable in the semiconductor industry. Manufacturing chips at the 3nm and 2nm nodes requires $20-30B fabs, decades of process knowledge, and yield expertise that no competitor can replicate quickly. Samsung Foundry is the only alternative for leading-edge nodes, and it trails TSMC in yield and customer trust. Intel Foundry is attempting to compete but is 2-3 years behind. The result is that TSMC can sustain 57-59% gross margins on a commodity-like service — because it’s not really a commodity when there’s only one supplier that can deliver at scale.

The AI demand vector is structural, not cyclical. Every new AI model requires more compute, which requires more GPUs, which requires more TSMC wafer capacity. The company has responded by accelerating N2 development and expanding CoWoS advanced packaging capacity (the technology that stacks HBM on GPU dies) — both of which are directly tied to AI chip production.

TSM’s options chain is the most put-heavy in our coverage at 8.4:1 (159,108 puts vs 19,038 calls). This is NOT bearish sentiment — it’s Taiwan geopolitical hedging. Institutions that own $870B of TSMC stock are required by risk mandates to hedge against a tail-risk scenario in the Taiwan Strait. The $175 strike alone holds 17,202 puts and the $250 strike holds 34,367 puts. Call activity is concentrated at $190-$250, reflecting modest upside speculation. The options market prices in a ~15% probability of a severe geopolitical event — if that doesn’t materialize, the stock re-rates higher.

9  |  WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from BUY if:

Revenue growth decelerates below 15% for two quarters. Would signal AI chip demand is peaking.
Samsung Foundry achieves yield parity at N2. Would break TSMC’s monopoly on leading-edge nodes.
Geopolitical escalation in the Taiwan Strait. Would fundamentally alter the risk profile — severity depends on scope.
Gross margins fall below 50%. Would indicate pricing pressure or Arizona cost headwinds are structural.
10  |  PRICE TARGET & CONCLUSION
Price Target: $410  |  Rating: BUY

Our $410 target represents 21% upside from $337.95, derived from 22x FY2027E EPS of $17.96 plus a premium for monopoly positioning in AI chip manufacturing.

TSMC is the most important company in the global technology supply chain. Over 90% of advanced semiconductors are manufactured in its fabs. Every AI GPU, every smartphone processor, every leading-edge chip flows through TSMC. At 19x FY27E with 24% growth, it’s the cheapest way to own the AI infrastructure buildout. The Taiwan geopolitical discount is the reason it’s cheap — and the Arizona/Japan/EU diversification is gradually reducing that discount.

Our conviction score of 83/100 places TSM firmly in the top quartile. The 8.4:1 put/call ratio in options tells the story: institutions are massively long but heavily hedged against tail risk. If the geopolitical premium fades — through continued diversification or de-escalation — the stock re-rates toward $400+. Earnings on April 17 is the near-term catalyst.
