1Investment Thesis
  • A category-defining defense-drone franchise on sale. AeroVironment is the dominant Western supplier of tactical small UAS and loitering munitions (Switchblade, Puma, JUMP 20), now scaled into a full-spectrum defense prime via the $4.1B BlueHalo acquisition. The stock trades ~58% below its 52-week high and roughly 47% off recent peaks — a derating driven by a single contract failure, not by erosion of the core franchise.
  • The sell-off is contract-specific, the demand backdrop is record-high. The collapse of the $1.7B SCAR/BADGER Space Force program (stop-work → termination → $151M impairment) drove a ~40% three-stage decline and a securities class action. But funded backlog hit a record $1.1B, unfunded backlog is $3.0B, book-to-bill ran 1.60, and YTD awards reached $4.6B — including a $186M Switchblade task order. Demand for the core products has never been higher.
  • Valuation reflects the overhang, not the trajectory. At ~$176, AVAV trades around 44x FY26E EPS midpoint but only ~4.4x FY27E and ~3.6x FY28E revenue against a Street that models a path to ~$2.5B revenue and $5+ EPS by FY28. Consensus is Buy with a $326 target (+85%). The risk/reward hinges on whether the SCAR loss is a one-time event in a secular grower (our base case) or the first crack in post-merger execution.

    4Price Action & Revenue Trajectory

    The three red markers trace the SCAR program's staged collapse — the stop-work order (Jan 20), the Space Force reopening the program (Mar 2), and Q3 results with the contract termination and impairment (Mar 10). The stock has not recovered, and the recent class-action filings have added a fresh overhang.

    Quarterly revenue (blue bars = post-BlueHalo close, ~May 2025) shows the acquisition more than doubling the top line — Q3 FY26 revenue of $408M was +143% year-over-year as reported, though only ~6% on a pro-forma basis, underscoring that the growth is acquisition-led and that organic execution is what the market is now scrutinizing.

    5Recent Developments & Catalyst Analysis

    The SCAR collapse (Jan–Mar 2026) is the defining recent event. AeroVironment's BlueHalo unit held a $1.7B contract to supply BADGER phased-array antenna systems to the U.S. Space Force's SCAR program. A January stop-work order, a March reassessment, and ultimately a termination for convenience led to a $151.3M goodwill impairment in the Space segment and a GAAP operating loss of $179M in fiscal Q3. Management intends to commercialize the phased-array BADGER technology and may recompete for SCAR under a revised structure.

    Securities class action. Multiple firms have filed suit (lead case Norrell v. AeroVironment, E.D. Va.), alleging the company overstated SCAR's security and growth potential during the June 25, 2025–March 10, 2026 class period. The lead-plaintiff deadline is July 27, 2026. This is an active overhang, though such suits are common after sharp defense-contractor drawdowns and typically take years to resolve.

    The offsetting positive — core demand is accelerating. Fiscal Q3 featured a $186M U.S. Army Switchblade task order and a $97M missile-related award; year-to-date awards reached $4.6B. AeroVironment is scaling manufacturing aggressively, including a new 140,000-sq-ft Salt Lake City facility with ~$2B of annual production potential. Management reaffirmed expectations for a record Q4 as delayed orders convert.

    Catalyst setup: The single most important near-term catalyst is fiscal Q4 / full-year FY2026 results (expected late June 2026) — the "record Q4" management guided to. A clean print that confirms organic momentum and backlog conversion would directly rebut the execution-risk narrative; a second miss would deepen it.
    6Business Model & Competitive Position

    AeroVironment designs, builds, and sustains autonomous and robotic systems for the U.S. Department of Defense, allied militaries, and government agencies. Revenue is a mix of one-time hardware sales and longer-lived services (training, spares, sustainment, software). Post-BlueHalo, the company reports two principal segments:

    SegmentWhat it does~% of Rev
    Autonomous Systems (AxS)Switchblade loitering munitions, Puma / Raven / JUMP 20 / P550 UAS, Titan counter-UAS — the historical core franchise~68%
    Space, Cyber & Directed Energy (SCDE)Space systems (incl. the former SCAR/BADGER work), directed energy, cyber/EW — largely the acquired BlueHalo capabilities~32%

    Competitive moat. AeroVironment holds an entrenched position in hand-launched tactical drones and is the Western standard-bearer for loitering munitions, a category validated and accelerated by the war in Ukraine. Its programs of record, security clearances, and DoD relationships create high switching costs. The competitive pressure is twofold: legacy primes (Lockheed Martin, Textron) moving down into tactical systems, and well-funded "Silicon Valley" defense startups (e.g., Anduril) moving up — making execution and scale the key battlegrounds, precisely where BlueHalo integration matters.

    Peer set (per data provider): KTOS, ESLT, BWXT, CW, TXT, plus broader A&D primes.

    8Management & Capital Structure

    Leadership in transition. Chairman, President & CEO Wahid Nawabi has led since 2016 and is the architect of the company's shift from niche drone-maker to acquisitive defense prime. The finance and operations bench is turning over at a sensitive moment: long-time CFO Kevin McDonnell is retiring (advisory through July 2026), succeeded by Sean Woodward (EVP & CFO, effective May 1, 2026; 22+ years in defense finance, incl. prior AV tenure), and Dr. Robert Smith joined as EVP & COO (April 2026) to oversee manufacturing scale-up. New financial and operational stewardship is a double-edged factor — fresh execution focus, but transition risk while integrating BlueHalo.

    13What Would Change Our Mind
    Bearish triggers: (1) a second consecutive quarter that misses guidance, signaling the SCAR failure was symptomatic of broader integration problems rather than an isolated event; (2) a further write-down of acquired BlueHalo goodwill; (3) evidence of organic (pro-forma) revenue declining rather than growing; (4) a litigation development implicating broader disclosure issues; (5) loss or stagnation of a flagship Switchblade/UAS program of record.
    Bullish confirmation: (1) a record Q4 with organic growth and margin recovery; (2) tangible progress recompeting SCAR or commercializing BADGER; (3) continued large loitering-munition awards; (4) demonstrated manufacturing scale-up (Salt Lake City) converting backlog to revenue; (5) settlement or dismissal of the litigation on immaterial terms.
    14Price Target & Conclusion

    We initiate AeroVironment at BUY with a 12-month base-case target of $326 (+85%), in line with Street consensus. The thesis is straightforward: a dominant, secularly-advantaged defense-drone franchise is trading at a deep discount because of a single contract failure and the litigation it spawned — not because the demand for its core products has weakened. With record backlog, accelerating loitering-munition orders, and a manufacturing scale-up underway, the setup is a quality franchise on sale.

    The honest counterweight is execution risk. The bull case requires a freshly-reconstituted management team to convert backlog through a newly-integrated, scaling manufacturing base — and the SCAR episode is a real reminder that post-merger execution can disappoint. We size this as a conviction-on-the-business, patience-on-the-entry position: the technical setup (Layer 2 = 38, 71% volatility, −15% momentum) and the open litigation argue for scaling in rather than chasing, with the late-June FY2026 print as the catalyst that should resolve much of the debate.

    Bottom line: BUY, $326 target. A best-in-class defense-drone franchise de-rated on a contained, contract-specific failure. High fundamental conviction, weak near-term technicals — accumulate into weakness ahead of the record-Q4 catalyst.
