1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

META peaked at $740 in February before pulling back 22% to $574 amid the Iran conflict market selloff and rotation out of mega-cap tech. The revenue trajectory remains strong: $42.3B → $47.5B → $51.2B → $59.9B over four quarters, with Q4’s $59.9B representing a 24% YoY acceleration. The pullback has widened the discount to consensus from 15% to 48% — the largest gap since the 2022 trough.

The selloff is macro-driven, not Meta-specific. The Iran conflict has compressed multiples across all mega-cap tech as rates reprice higher on oil inflation pass-through. Meta’s advertising business is actually relatively insulated from the conflict — advertisers shift budgets to digital channels during uncertainty because the ROI is measurable and adjustable in real time. The $574 price represents a better entry point than anything available since mid-2024.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Family of Apps Advertising (~97%): Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger, and Threads. The advertising engine serves 3.35B+ daily active people across all platforms. Revenue is driven by ad impressions × price per impression, both of which are growing. AI-powered Advantage+ ad tools and Reels monetization are the current growth vectors. Instagram and WhatsApp are the strongest growers.
Reality Labs (~3%): Quest VR/MR headsets, Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses, and metaverse development. Running at ~$4-5B annual losses. This is the long-term bet that the market is skeptical about but management is committed to. The Ray-Ban Meta glasses are the first product in this segment showing genuine consumer traction.

Meta’s competitive moat is the combination of 3.35 billion daily users and the world’s most sophisticated AI-driven advertising platform. No other company on earth has this reach. Google has search intent (higher purchase intent per query) but Meta has attention volume and the ability to create demand through visual, social, and video content. The shift to AI-powered ad targeting through Advantage+ has measurably improved advertiser ROI, which drives pricing power — Meta can charge more per impression because each impression converts better.

The Llama open-source AI strategy is the most underappreciated asset. By making Llama the default AI model for developers and enterprises, Meta creates an ecosystem that reinforces its infrastructure investments. Every developer building on Llama is indirectly validating Meta’s AI architecture. The open-source approach also means Meta doesn’t need to win the AI API revenue battle against OpenAI/Anthropic — it wins by making AI a commodity that drives more engagement on its platforms, which drives more ad revenue at 82% margins.

9  |  WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from BUY if:

Revenue growth decelerates below 10% for two consecutive quarters. Would signal the advertising market is saturating or macro is overwhelming the AI efficiency gains.
Operating margins fall below 33% (2023 trough level). Would indicate AI capex and Reality Labs are overwhelming the ad business’s profitability.
Reality Labs losses exceed $20B annually with no consumer product traction. Would signal Zuckerberg’s metaverse bet is consuming shareholder value.
Regulatory action forces structural changes to ad targeting or data collection. Would undermine the core advertising moat.
10  |  PRICE TARGET & CONCLUSION
Price Target: $750  |  Rating: BUY

Our $750 target represents 31% upside from $574.46, derived from 20x FY2027E EPS of $34.37 plus a premium for the AI-powered advertising flywheel and Llama ecosystem optionality. Consensus is $848.

Meta Platforms is the most compelling risk/reward in mega-cap tech. The combination of 82% gross margins, 41% operating margins, 26% EPS growth, $50B+ in annual FCF, and a 16x forward multiple creates a profile that no other $1T+ company can match. The 22% pullback from $740 to $574 has widened the gap to analyst consensus to 48% — the kind of dislocation that typically resolves upward for companies with this quality of earnings.

Our conviction score of 70/100 is lower than our mid-cap AI infrastructure names because META’s volatility and momentum are lower by construction (it’s a $1.45T stock — it moves slower). But the qualitative case is among the strongest in our coverage. The AI capex is not speculative — it directly improves the advertising engine that generates $200B in revenue at 82% margins. When the macro cloud lifts, META re-rates toward $750-$850. The April 30 earnings report is the near-term catalyst.
