1Investment Thesis
  • The pioneer of semiconductor-based ultrasound — a real, growing franchise. Butterfly replaced the piezoelectric crystals in traditional ultrasound with a proprietary silicon chip ("Ultrasound-on-Chip"), enabling a whole-body handheld probe (the iQ+/iQ3) that plugs into a phone or tablet. It is a genuine point-of-care leader, with revenue growing to $98M in 2025 (+19%) on a ~50/50 hardware/software mix, FY26 guidance of $117–121M, and — critically — cash burn collapsing from −$187M (2022) to just −$16M (2025), approaching breakeven with a healthy balance sheet ($138M cash, ~$20M debt).
  • Today's catalyst: a potentially transformative licensing model ("BFLY Embedded"). Butterfly spiked +31% after Midjourney unveiled "Midjourney Medical" and the Midjourney Scanner — a full-body ultrasonic computational-tomography device built on Butterfly's licensed Ultrasound-on-Chip technology. The prototype uses 40 Butterfly imaging modules per system (future versions substantially more) under a co-development agreement worth up to $74M. TD Cowen framed this as validating Butterfly's chip as a licensable infrastructure platform — opening licensing fees, chip sales, and revenue-sharing well beyond handheld point-of-care ultrasound. It reframes Butterfly from a device maker into a potential semiconductor-IP platform.
  • But the price has run ahead of the proof — hence HOLD. The story is exciting and the burn trajectory is genuinely improving, but: the stock at ~$7.46 trades ~27% above the $5.42 analyst consensus (those targets largely predate today's news, but the gap is stark), it remains GAAP-unprofitable with a ~$1.95B cap on ~$98M of revenue (~19x sales), the Midjourney revenue is early-stage (a prototype; the $74M is "expected" over time), and today's move was amplified by a ~13% short interest squeeze. Real catalyst, real franchise — but a one-day spike to above all targets is not an entry. HOLD.

    5The Midjourney Catalyst & AI Strategy

    Today's move: a marquee AI partner validates Butterfly's chip as licensable infrastructure. At a San Francisco launch event, Midjourney — the AI image-generation company — unveiled a new healthcare division, Midjourney Medical, and the Midjourney Scanner: a full-body ultrasonic computational-tomography device that generates high-resolution 3D body scans in about a minute. Crucially, it is built on Butterfly's licensed Ultrasound-on-Chip technology — the current prototype incorporating 40 Butterfly imaging modules per system, with future generations expected to use substantially more, under a co-development agreement carrying up to $74M in expected payments. The stock rose ~31%.

    Why it matters — "BFLY Embedded" reframes the business. TD Cowen's analyst noted the unveiling highlights the underappreciated value of Butterfly's chip platform beyond handheld ultrasound. If Butterfly's silicon can power entirely new imaging machines built by partners, it becomes a licensable semiconductor-IP platform — generating licensing fees, chip sales, and revenue-sharing — a fundamentally higher-margin, more scalable model than selling probes one at a time. This is the optionality the market is repricing today: a small medical-device company potentially becoming "the chip inside" a new category of imaging hardware.

    The broader AI strategy is consistent with this. Butterfly has been building an AI/software ecosystem: integrating third-party AI (e.g., DESKi's HeartFocus for guided cardiac echo so less-trained clinicians can capture complete views), Compass AI for device/workflow connectivity, and educational tools (ScanLab). The thesis is an open platform — hardware + cloud software + third-party AI + now licensed chips — rather than a closed device. The Midjourney deal is the most dramatic proof point yet that the chip itself has standalone platform value.

    Competitive position — a differentiated leader in a real but niche category. Butterfly's moat is its proprietary semiconductor ultrasound chip, which uniquely enables a single low-cost whole-body handheld probe — a genuine technological edge that competitors (GE, Philips, Fujifilm/Sonosite, and fellow disruptor Exo) have struggled to match at the same price/form-factor. The point-of-care ultrasound market is large and growing (especially as AI lowers the skill barrier for less-trained users), and Butterfly is a recognized leader. The risks: it competes against vastly larger medical-imaging incumbents, the handheld market is still developing, and the company remains sub-scale and unprofitable. The Midjourney licensing model, if it scales, would be a powerful new moat — but it is early. Founder Jonathan Rothberg's semiconductor pedigree underpins the platform's credibility, and the company runs remarkably lean — roughly 190 employees generating ~$98M of revenue.

    14Rating & Conclusion

    We initiate Butterfly Network at HOLD with a base-case target of $5.42 (the current analyst consensus, which predates today's catalyst and is likely to rise). To give the story its due: Butterfly is a genuine innovator — the pioneer of semiconductor-based "Ultrasound-on-Chip" handheld imaging, a real and growing franchise ($98M revenue, +19%) with a dramatically improving financial profile (cash burn collapsed from −$187M to −$16M, a clean balance sheet, near breakeven). And today's Midjourney catalyst is genuinely exciting: a marquee AI partner building a full-body scanner on Butterfly's licensed chip validates a potentially transformative "BFLY Embedded" licensing model — reframing the company from a device maker into a possible semiconductor-IP platform with licensing fees, chip sales, and revenue-sharing. For an EM/point-of-care audience, the clinical relevance and the platform logic both resonate.

    But our rating is on the stock at today's price, and there discipline wins. After a +31% one-day spike, BFLY trades ~$7.46 — ~27% above the $5.42 analyst consensus (stale or not, a stark gap), at ~19x sales, while still GAAP-unprofitable, with today's move amplified by a ~13% short squeeze and concentrated call buying. The Midjourney revenue is at the prototype/co-development stage — real and promising, but early, with the $74M "expected" over time rather than booked. The bull case (a scaling licensing platform) is legitimate but unproven; the asymmetry after a squeeze-driven vertical move — limited near-term upside to fundamentals, real downside if the spike unwinds — argues for patience. We would turn constructive on evidence the licensing model is generating real revenue, on upward target revisions confirming the re-rating, or on a pullback toward fundamentals. For now: a genuinely exciting catalyst on a stock that has run ahead of the proof — HOLD.

    Bottom line: HOLD, $5.42 base-case target (below the spiked price; likely to be revised up). A real semiconductor-ultrasound leader with collapsing cash burn and a genuinely transformative new catalyst — Midjourney building a full-body scanner on Butterfly's licensed chip, validating the "BFLY Embedded" licensing model — but the stock spiked +31% today to ~27% above (pre-news) analyst targets, on a ~13% short squeeze, still unprofitable, with the licensing revenue early-stage. Respect the catalyst and the improving franchise; the price has run ahead of the proof. Watch for real licensing revenue and target revisions; revisit on confirmation or a pullback, not on the squeeze.
