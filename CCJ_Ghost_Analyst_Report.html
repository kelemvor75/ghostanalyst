Investment Thesis
The Western World's Nuclear Fuel Monopoly. Cameco is one of the world's largest uranium producers, controlling ~18% of global production. With nuclear energy experiencing a renaissance driven by AI data center power demand and decarbonization mandates, Cameco sits at the center of a structural supply deficit.
AI Power Demand Is the New Catalyst. Every major hyperscaler — Microsoft, Google, Amazon — has announced nuclear power partnerships or SMR investments. The electricity required for AI data centers is driving utilities back to nuclear, creating unprecedented demand for uranium fuel.
Supply Deficit Is Structural. Years of underinvestment in uranium mining have created a supply gap that cannot be quickly closed. New mines take 10-15 years to develop. Cameco's existing production capacity gives it pricing power that grows with each passing year of deficit.

1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

CCJ has rallied 30% from $84 in October to $109 today, tracking the broader nuclear renaissance narrative. The Iran conflict has added an energy security premium. Revenue has been lumpy — uranium delivery timing creates quarter-to-quarter volatility — but the trend is solidly upward.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL
Uranium Production: McArthur River/Key Lake (Saskatchewan) — world's largest high-grade uranium mine. Cigar Lake — another tier-1 asset. Production costs among the lowest globally.
Westinghouse (49% stake): Nuclear fuel services, reactor technology, and plant maintenance. Provides downstream exposure to the nuclear value chain beyond raw uranium.
Long-Term Contracts: Cameco sells uranium under long-term contracts at prices above spot, providing revenue visibility. Contract book extends through the 2030s.
