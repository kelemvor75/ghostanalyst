1Investment Thesis
  • The real story isn't the recovery — it's that the market has already paid for a recovery, an AI-power transformation, and now a $7B acquisition, all at once, before any of the three is proven. onsemi is a genuinely improving business: Q1 2026 marked its first year-over-year revenue growth in three years, ending a brutal two-year decline from an $8.3B peak. Gross margin has expanded for three straight quarters, the AI data center segment more than doubled year-over-year, and automotive grew 5% — its first annual gain after seven consecutive quarters of decline. But the stock has nearly tripled off its $44.56 low to ~$119, and at ~27x forward earnings, ~39x EV/EBITDA, and 7.8x sales, the market is no longer pricing a cyclical power-semi company. It is pricing a flawless execution of three separate, unproven bets simultaneously — and that is a demanding thing to underwrite at this price.
  • Disentangling the three layers is the whole analysis: a cyclical bounce (mean-reverts), a structural AI/SiC transformation (compounds), and a dilutive M&A pivot (unproven) — and they should not be valued the same way. Most coverage blurs these together into one bullish 'onsemi is back' narrative. They are different in kind. Layer one is the cyclical trough recovery — real, but the easy money (the move off $44) is already made, and cyclical recoveries mean-revert rather than compound. Layer two is the structural transformation: onsemi as the only broad-based U.S. power-semiconductor supplier 'from grid to processor,' with SiC content gains in 900V EVs and a GaN/SiC AI-data-center funnel — this is the part that could justify a premium, but AI data center is still a small slice of a ~$6B revenue base. Layer three is the brand-new $7B all-stock Synaptics acquisition (announced June 25), which the market greeted with an 8% drop — a dilutive, integration-heavy bet on edge-AI sensing that adds execution risk precisely when the multiple leaves no room for error.
  • A great company at a price that already assumes greatness — we initiate at HOLD, not because the business is weak, but because the risk/reward at ~27x forward with a fresh dilutive deal is balanced at best. The forward earnings math is the crux: the stock is ~27x FY2026 EPS but only ~17x FY2027 and ~13x FY2028 consensus — so if onsemi delivers the recovery and the AI ramp, today's price is reasonable on out-year numbers. But that is precisely the bet, and the analyst community is violently split: consensus sits at ~$99 (below the current price), with bears at $62 and fresh 'AI-charged' bulls at $140-150. When the official consensus is 17% below the price and the dispersion is that wide, the market itself is telling you this is contested. We see a high-quality franchise executing a real turnaround — but at a price that has run ahead of the proof. HOLD, with a clear path to BUY on a pullback or execution confirmation.

    4Price Action & The Three-Layer Re-Rating

    The chart tells the story of a violent re-rating. onsemi bottomed near $44.56 as the semiconductor down-cycle, an automotive inventory correction, and an EV demand air-pocket compressed revenue and crushed margins. From that trough it has nearly tripled to ~$119 — a +143% move over the past year — driven by three sequential catalysts: the November 2025 $6B buyback authorization, the May 2026 Q1 print that confirmed the cyclical inflection, and accelerating AI-data-center commentary. The two recent red markers matter: the $1.5B convertible note offering (May) and, most importantly, the $7B all-stock Synaptics acquisition (June 25), which the market greeted with an 8% drop.

    The revenue chart shows why the stock's tripling deserves scrutiny: quarterly revenue has been essentially flat around $1.5B for five quarters — this is the bottom of an 'L-shaped' cycle, not a V-shaped surge. The bull case is that this flat base is the launchpad for a margin-led earnings recovery (gross margin expanding from the low-30s toward a 53% target) plus an AI-data-center growth vector. But the stock has re-rated ~3x while revenue went sideways — meaning the entire move is multiple expansion on future earnings, not current results. That is the central risk: the price has front-run the fundamentals.

    5The Genius Question: Cyclical Bounce or Structural Compounder?

    The single most important question for onsemi — and the one most coverage glosses over — is what kind of re-rating this is. A cyclical recovery and a structural transformation look identical for a few quarters but are worth completely different multiples. Conflating them is how investors overpay at cycle tops.

    The cyclical layer (mean-reverts — do NOT pay a premium for it). Most of onsemi's near-term earnings recovery is simply the semiconductor cycle turning. Automotive and industrial demand is normalizing after a brutal inventory correction; utilization is rising; margins are recovering off a trough. This is real and drives the FY26-FY27 EPS snapback from $2.33 toward $4.30. But cyclical earnings recoveries are not worth premium multiples — they revert. Paying ~27x forward for the cyclical component alone would be a classic top-of-cycle error: you would be capitalizing peak-trajectory earnings at a peak multiple. If onsemi is only a recovering cyclical, fair value is closer to the bear/consensus range ($62-99), not $119+.
    The structural layer (compounds — this is what could justify the price). The bull case rests on onsemi being more than a cyclical: the only broad-based U.S. power-semiconductor supplier spanning 'grid to processor,' with three secular drivers. (1) AI data center power — doubled YoY, guided to double again in 2026, as GaN (GaNEXUS) and SiC content rises across the 800V DC power tree with 'all the leading hyperscalers.' (2) SiC in 900V EVs — content gains even as unit volumes stay soft (China auto revenue grew despite a 6% drop in passenger-vehicle sales — a content-over-units story). (3) The Treo analog platform and GaN funnel ($1.5B+). If these compound, onsemi grows structurally faster than the cycle and the premium is justified.

    Our honest assessment: both layers are real, but the structural layer is not yet big enough to carry a ~$46B valuation on its own. AI data center, even doubling, is a small fraction of a ~$6B revenue base — the math doesn't yet support the multiple without the cyclical recovery also delivering. So the current price effectively requires both the cyclical snapback (which mean-reverts) and the structural ramp (which is early) to play out together, flawlessly. That can happen — but it is a high bar, and it is why we think the easy money is made and the risk/reward is now balanced. The differentiated insight is not 'onsemi is recovering' (consensus) — it is that the market is paying a structural-compounder multiple for a business that is still mostly a recovering cyclical, and the burden of proof has shifted entirely to execution.

    6Business Model, Segments & The Synaptics Pivot

    onsemi operates three segments. The Power Solutions Group (~$2.8B, the core) makes power discretes, modules, SiC, and GaN for automotive, industrial, and increasingly AI data center power. The Analog & Mixed-Signal Group covers power management and signal-chain analog. The Intelligent Sensing Group (~$0.9B) makes image sensors for automotive ADAS and industrial vision — a genuine franchise (onsemi is a leader in automotive image sensors). The strategic thrust is a 'system-level' approach: combining silicon, silicon carbide, SiC JFETs, and GaN across a customer's full power-conversion stack, which can create stickiness that a single-device competitor (the AOSL-tier suppliers) cannot match.

    The $7B Synaptics deal is the new swing factor — and the market's skepticism is warranted. On June 25, onsemi announced an all-stock acquisition of Synaptics (~$7B), adding edge-AI, IoT connectivity, and human-interface sensing. The strategic logic is 'intelligent sensing + power for the AI edge.' But the market dropped the stock 8%, for sound reasons: (1) it is all-stock, meaning dilution at a moment when onsemi's own shares are richly valued — issuing expensive equity to buy growth; (2) Synaptics has its own challenged end-markets and execution history; (3) it layers a major integration onto a company already juggling a cyclical recovery and a margin-recovery program; and (4) it signals onsemi may see organic growth as insufficient to justify the multiple. We view the deal as raising risk, not reducing it, until management proves the integration math.

    7Financial Model, Valuation & The DCF Caveat

    Our $118 target implies the stock is roughly fairly valued at current levels — we do not see a compelling margin of safety in either direction, which is the definition of a HOLD. The valuation hinges entirely on the forward earnings trajectory, so the framework must be forward-looking. First, the DCF caveat.

    Why the DCF (~$22) is the wrong anchor — and why we'd ignore a bullish DCF too. A standard DCF returns ~$22, but onsemi is a cyclical company at the bottom of its earnings cycle: trailing free cash flow and margins are trough-depressed, and a DCF built on them mechanically extrapolates trough conditions forever, badly understating a normalized business. This is the same reason we do not anchor to DCF for any deep cyclical — and to be intellectually honest, the principle cuts both ways: if a DCF on peak-cycle cash flows returned a number above the price, we would discount that too, because capitalizing peak earnings is just as misleading as capitalizing trough earnings. For cyclicals, normalized mid-cycle earnings power and forward multiples are the right lens, not DCF.

    On forward earnings, the picture is genuinely two-sided. The stock is ~27x FY2026 EPS ($3.10) — rich. But it falls to ~17x FY2027 ($4.30) and ~13x FY2028 ($5.77) on consensus — reasonable-to-cheap if those numbers are hit. The entire debate is whether to underwrite the out-year recovery at today's price. The sensitivity grid frames it on normalized FY2027 power:

    8Management, Capital Structure & The Dilution Question

    CEO Hassane El-Khoury has executed a credible multi-year transformation — exiting non-core/low-margin business ('product pruning'), investing heavily in SiC and GaN capacity, and driving a manufacturing/cost overhaul that is now showing up in three straight quarters of gross-margin expansion. CFO Thad Trent has paired this with aggressive capital return. The strategy is sound; the question is whether the recent capital-allocation moves are accretive or value-destructive at today's share price.

    The capital structure has gotten complicated, and not entirely in shareholders' favor. onsemi has a $6B buyback authorization and returned a record $1.42B in FY2025 — genuinely shareholder-friendly. But the recent moves cut both ways: the $1.5B 0% convertible notes (due 2031, conversion ~$161, with hedges/warrants at $211 to blunt dilution) are cheap financing but add convertible overhang; and the $7B all-stock Synaptics deal issues equity at a rich valuation to fund an acquisition — the opposite of buying back undervalued stock. Insider activity reinforces caution: the CFO sold 90,000 shares across April at $80-100, and the gate flagged six insider sales (~$27M) with zero buys. None of this is damning on its own, but the pattern — issuing expensive equity for M&A while insiders sell into strength — argues against chasing the stock here.

    11Analyst Sentiment

    The sell-side dispersion on onsemi is among the widest we have seen — and that dispersion is itself the signal. The consensus target is ~$99 (median ~$108, high $120, low $62), which means the official consensus sits roughly 17% BELOW the current price. Yet a wave of post-Q1 'AI-charged' target hikes pushed individual firms well above that: Mizuho $150, Wells Fargo $140, 24/7 Wall St $141, with bulls citing the AI-data-center doubling and SiC recovery. On the other side, Barclays initiated at $75 (auto/China exposure), GuruFocus's model implies ~$60, and several firms hold at $100 with explicit valuation concerns — Baird flagged ongoing share losses from product pruning.

    The honest read: when consensus is below the price and the range spans $62 to $150, the market is telling you it genuinely doesn't know. This is not a clean bull setup (where the whole Street sits above the price and is raising targets) nor a clean bear setup. It is a violently contested name where the gap between the recovering-cyclical bears and the structural-AI-compounder bulls is enormous — and the stock currently trades closer to the bull camp than the consensus. That asymmetry — price above consensus, with credible bears at $62 — is exactly why we initiate at HOLD rather than chase. The burden of proof sits with the bulls, and the next two to three quarters of execution (and the Synaptics close) will settle it.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    We would move to BUY if: (1) the stock pulls back toward the low-$90s (consensus) or below, restoring a margin of safety to a high-quality franchise; (2) AI data center revenue grows from a 'small but doubling' slice into a genuine double-digit-percent share of revenue, validating the structural thesis; (3) gross margin convincingly crosses into the mid-40s en route to the 53% target; or (4) the Synaptics integration demonstrably proves accretive with disciplined share-count management.

    We would move to SELL if the cyclical recovery stalls (auto/industrial orders roll over), AI-data-center growth decelerates or proves lower-margin than hoped, or the Synaptics deal destroys value through dilution and integration missteps — any of which, at ~27x forward earnings, would trigger a sharp de-rating toward the $60-75 bear targets. The thesis is balanced today precisely because both the bull and bear paths are credible; a decisive move on execution in either direction would break the tie.

    14Rating & Conclusion

    onsemi is a high-quality, U.S.-based power-and-analog semiconductor franchise that has executed a credible turnaround: the cyclical trough is behind it, gross margins are recovering, automotive has inflected positive, and a genuine AI-data-center growth vector is emerging. As a business, it is doing the right things, and management deserves credit. This is not a bearish report on the company.

    It is a cautious report on the stock at this price. onsemi has nearly tripled off its low to ~$119, where it trades at ~27x forward earnings, ~39x EV/EBITDA, and 7.8x sales — a multiple that prices in a flawless simultaneous execution of three separate bets: a cyclical recovery (which mean-reverts), a structural AI/SiC transformation (which is real but still early and small), and a freshly-announced $7B all-stock Synaptics acquisition (which the market dropped the stock 8% on, and which adds dilution and integration risk). The official analyst consensus sits ~17% below the current price, with credible bears at $62 — when consensus is below the price and the range is that wide, the market is signaling genuine uncertainty, and the burden of proof has shifted entirely to execution.

    HOLD — conviction 52/100, target $118 (roughly fair value). The genius insight here is not that onsemi is recovering — that is consensus. It is that the market is paying a structural-compounder multiple for a business that, by revenue mix, is still mostly a recovering cyclical, and has just added dilutive M&A risk on top. The easy money — the cyclical bounce off $44 — has been made. From here, the risk/reward is balanced: the bull case ($150) requires everything to go right, while the bear case ($62-75) requires only that the recovery prove ordinary. We initiate at HOLD with genuine respect for the franchise and a clear, specific path to BUY — a pullback toward the low-$90s, or hard proof that the AI-data-center ramp and Synaptics integration justify the premium. Own the business at the right price; this is not yet that price.
