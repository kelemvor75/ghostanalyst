1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

NOW has declined 60.8% from its split-adjusted 52-week high of $211 to $83 — the most severe drawdown in the company’s history as a public company. The decline was not a single event but a five-phase cascade that began in July 2025 — seven months before the Iran conflict. Phase 1 (Jul–Nov 2025, -23%): valuation normalization from 50-60x forward earnings — a bubble multiple even for premium SaaS. Phase 2 (Dec 2025, -3%): Bloomberg reported a $7B Armis cybersecurity acquisition, viewed as expensive; an analyst downgraded citing AI disruption risk to multiples. Phase 3 (Jan 2026, -19%): Q4 earnings beat estimates but the market was ‘lukewarm’ — investors were no longer rewarding operational excellence alone.

Phase 4 (Feb–Mar 2026, -10%): Iran conflict added macro pressure while corporations began pausing new software seat expansions in favor of piloting AI agents. Phase 5 (Apr 2026, -27%): Anthropic’s Claude Cowork plugin launch triggered the ‘SaaSpocalypse’ — enterprise software stocks lost nearly $1 trillion in market value. UBS downgraded from Buy to Neutral on April 10, cutting the target from $170 to $100. The stock is below its 50-day ($108) and 200-day ($158) moving averages and making new 52-week lows. The trend is unambiguously bearish.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
IT Service Management (ITSM, ~40%): The core platform — incident, problem, change, and asset management for IT departments. Installed in virtually every Fortune 500. The ‘land’ product. This is the segment most directly threatened by AI agents that could resolve IT tickets autonomously.
IT Operations + HR + Other Workflows (~45%): Cloud management, employee onboarding, security operations, and cross-functional workflows. The ‘expand’ product driving net expansion rates above 125%. Less vulnerable to AI disruption because these workflows involve complex organizational logic.
Now Assist AI + Security (~15%): AI-powered workflow automation and security operations. ServiceNow’s own AI agent platform. This is the company’s answer to the disruption narrative — but the market hasn’t decided if it’s sufficient.

ServiceNow’s moat is workflow ownership — once deployed, switching costs are measured in years and millions. The 95%+ renewal rate and 125%+ net expansion rate are among the strongest in enterprise software. But the AI disruption question is whether AI agents can build new workflows outside ServiceNow’s platform entirely, making the switching cost argument irrelevant for new deployments even if existing customers don’t churn.

The bull case is that ServiceNow becomes the orchestration layer for AI agents — the platform where enterprises deploy, govern, and monitor AI workflows. Now Assist and the new Context Engine are designed for exactly this. The bear case is that Anthropic, OpenAI, and Microsoft build general-purpose AI agents that bypass ServiceNow entirely — why buy a $500K/year ITSM platform when an AI agent can resolve tickets through direct API calls? UBS’s Karl Keirstead heard the bear case directly from CIOs: enterprises are actively budgeting less for traditional software and more for AI infrastructure. The truth is probably between these extremes, but the market is pricing the bear case.

10  |  PRICE TARGET & CONCLUSION
Price Target: $105  |  Rating: HOLD

Our $105 target represents 27% upside from $83.00, derived from 21x FY2027E EPS of $5.02 — a multiple that reflects the genuine uncertainty about AI agent disruption.

ServiceNow is a HOLD, not a BUY, because the stock is in a confirmed downtrend driven by a structural thesis (AI agent disruption of SaaS), not just a cyclical selloff. The fundamentals are strong — 21% revenue growth, 77% gross margins, 95%+ renewal rates, fortress balance sheet. But strong fundamentals in a stock falling 60% means the market is pricing in a future where those fundamentals deteriorate. The April 22 earnings report is the most important data point in ServiceNow’s history as a public company — it will reveal whether enterprises are actually cutting software spend for AI, or whether the narrative has overrun reality.

We are not bears on ServiceNow. We believe the business is far more durable than the stock price implies. But buying a stock making new 52-week lows below both moving averages, with the primary analyst downgrade citing a structural (not cyclical) thesis, and with earnings two weeks away that could confirm or deny the bear case — that’s a HOLD until we have data. If Q1 bookings are strong, we upgrade. If they confirm the SaaSpocalypse, we may downgrade further. Conviction score of 55/100 reflects genuine uncertainty.
