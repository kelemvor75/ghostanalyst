1Investment Thesis

Cerebras builds the largest computer chip ever made and the fastest AI inference in the world — genuinely differentiated technology that Nvidia cannot easily copy. Yet at $206, down ~47% from its $386 debut-day high, the stock is a case study in the gap between a remarkable engineering company and a remarkable stock. We initiate at HOLD, 12-month target $210 (roughly flat). This is not a bearish call on the company; it is a disciplined call on the entry. After the halving the shares are still ~53x forward sales with a −30% operating margin, the celebrated “diversification” is really a rotation of customer concentration onto a cash-burning counterparty, growth is bottlenecked by data-center construction the company does not fully control, and roughly 5x the IPO float unlocks into the fall. Extraordinary company; wrong moment.

  • The concentration didn't diversify — it rotated. 2025 revenue was ~86% two UAE-linked customers (MBZUAI 62%, G42 24%). The $24.6B backlog that looks like de-risking is ~80% OpenAI — a company running historic losses against ~$1.4T of commitments it has not shown it can fund, plus an option on ~33M Cerebras shares. The market prices the backlog as high-quality contracted revenue; it is really an option on one hyperscaler's solvency.
  • The bottleneck is physical — and it cuts both ways. CEO Andrew Feldman: “Demand is not the constraint. Supply is not the constraint. The constraint is data centers.” Bear: growth is gated by slow, capital-intensive construction, and the margin hit (Q1 47% → FY26 38–41%) comes from renting chips back from G42 because Cerebras is capacity-starved. Bull: every megawatt it lights up converts to high-margin cloud revenue (already +167% YoY), so the compression is a timing artifact that inflects when its own capacity opens. It is an infrastructure-execution bet dressed as a chip story.
  • Still priced for perfection, even after halving. ~86x trailing / ~53x FY26E / ~30x FY27E sales against a −30% operating margin. The IPO priced at ~90–100x sales, so the decline is a valuation reset, not a broken thesis — but at $206 there is no evidence the reset is complete.
  • A 5×-float supply wall is coming. The phased lockup frees ~60M shares by the Q2 print (~August) and ~171M — about 5x the entire IPO — by the ~Oct/Nov main expiry. Early holders own stock bought between $0.85 and $107. For a momentum discipline especially, that is a supply overhang to respect, not to front-run.
  • The technology and the demand are real — this is a “when,” not an “if.” Wafer-Scale Engine 3 (4 trillion transistors, ~57x a Blackwell die), the fastest inference available, a $24.6B backlog, ~$3.3B cash, and OpenAI / AWS / IBM / Meta distribution. We expect to want to own it — at a better multiple, after the supply clears and margins inflect. Today is not that day.

    4Price Action & The Re-Rating

    The debut was spectacular and the correction was swift. Priced at $185 on May 13, 2026 — the largest US semiconductor IPO in history, ~$6.4B raised, reportedly 20x oversubscribed — CBRS opened at $350, touched $386 intraday, and closed Day 1 at $311, a 68% pop that valued a company with $510M of trailing revenue at roughly $67–95B, or ~90–130x sales. From there it was largely one direction.

    A wave of Buy-rated brokerage initiations after the June 8 quiet-period expiry briefly arrested the slide near $260. But the June 23 Q1 report — a clear revenue beat (+94%) overshadowed by gross-margin guidance (Q1 47% → FY26 38–41%) and ~69% growth deceleration — sent shares down another ~10–20%, and a 7.6% same-day drop in the SOX semiconductor index amplified it. At $206 the stock is ~47% off its high and ~11% above the IPO price. We read this as a valuation reset, not a broken thesis — but with the multiple still ~53x forward sales and a supply wall ahead, we do not assume the reset is finished.

    5Two Things the Bulls Are Underweighting

    Two things the bulls are underweighting — both defensible from Cerebras's own disclosures.

    1. The concentration illusion. The bull narrative treats the OpenAI and AWS deals as diversification away from the old G42 / MBZUAI concentration (~86% of 2025 revenue). Look closer. Cerebras swapped a sovereign-linked concentration (the UAE) for a single-counterparty concentration on OpenAI: the $24.6B backlog everyone cites as de-risking is ~80% OpenAI. And OpenAI is not an ordinary customer — it is burning cash at a historic rate against an estimated ~$1.4T of compute commitments it has no demonstrated ability to fund, and it holds an option to buy ~33M Cerebras shares at nominal value (a claw on the very upside it is meant to create). The backlog is being priced as contracted, high-quality revenue; it is closer to an option on one hyperscaler's solvency and follow-through.

    2. The bottleneck is physical, and that is the tell. The single most revealing disclosure of the quarter was CEO Andrew Feldman's: “Demand is not the constraint. Supply is not the constraint. The constraint is data centers.” It cuts both ways, which is exactly why the stock is a genuine judgment call rather than a slam-dunk in either direction. The bear read: growth is gated by capital-intensive, slow, partly-out-of-their-control construction, and the margin compression is the symptom — Cerebras is so capacity-starved it is renting its own chips back from G42 at a margin penalty to serve OpenAI. The bull read: if demand and chips truly are not the constraint, then every megawatt of data center Cerebras lights up converts directly into high-margin cloud revenue (already +167% YoY) — the 47% → 38–41% compression is a timing artifact that inflects the moment its own capacity comes online. The stock is a bet on how fast Cerebras can pour concrete and secure power.

    The synthesis. At ~53x forward sales with a −30% operating margin, the market pays a premium price for a company whose growth is throttled by construction and whose “diversification” is a rotation into a cash-burning counterparty. The technology is real and the demand is real — this is not a fad. But entry is the whole game here: you would be buying a downtrending, richly-valued, concentration-heavy name into a 5x-float supply wall, with margins getting worse before they get better.

    A “when,” not an “if.” We fully expect to own Cerebras at some point — the wafer-scale moat and inference-speed lead are the real thing, and the backlog says demand is enormous. The HOLD is about timing and price, not about the company. Wait for the margin inflection (own data centers online), the lockup to clear, and either a cheaper multiple or a trend reversal.
    6Business Model, Segments & Competition

    Cerebras sells two things: systems (the CS-3, built around the Wafer-Scale Engine 3, the largest commercial chip ever — 4 trillion transistors on a single TSMC 5nm wafer, ~900,000 AI cores) and a fast-growing cloud / inference service (Cerebras Cloud, +167% YoY in Q1). The core bet: for AI inference, speed is the product — “fast AI is more valuable than slow AI” — and wafer-scale delivers per-token latency that Nvidia clusters struggle to match. The revenue model is shifting from lumpy hardware sales (historically to G42 / MBZUAI) toward recurring cloud and inference (OpenAI 750MW, AWS), distributed through AWS and Microsoft marketplaces, IBM watsonx, Hugging Face and others.

    Competition is the structural vulnerability. Nvidia dominates with Blackwell/Hopper, a ~75% gross margin, and the CUDA software moat; AMD and Intel bring vast resources and installed bases; Groq and SambaNova compete directly on inference speed; and the hyperscalers build their own silicon (Google TPU, AWS Trainium/Inferentia, Meta MTIA). Cerebras's edge is a genuinely different architecture and inference latency; its exposure is ecosystem depth, structurally lower margins, and the risk that model-efficiency gains (DeepSeek-style) erode the premium the market pays for raw speed.

    7Financial Model, Valuation & The DCF Caveat

    Valuation basis, stated plainly. There are no earnings to value, and there will not be until ~2027 — consensus has Cerebras losing money in 2026 (~−$0.89) before turning profitable in 2027 (~$0.96) and scaling in 2028 (~$4.68). So we value on EV / forward sales, cross-checked against that path to profitability. At $206, CBRS trades at ~86x trailing / ~53x FY26E / ~30x FY27E sales — expensive on any lens, even after the halving.

    The DCF caveat — flagged in both directions. A mechanistic DCF returns a negative fair value (roughly −$1,700). That is meaningless, and we say so: the company is intentionally pre-profit, pouring cash into data-center capex (−$393M FCF), so any model that extrapolates current free cash flow spits out a nonsense negative number. It is a caution about DCF mechanics on pre-profit hyper-growth names, not a valuation. Equally, we flag the aggressive bull models: a widely-cited narrative reaches a ~$415 fair value by assuming 55% revenue growth for five years and a wholesale profitability transformation — a heroic, unproven trajectory no fabless semiconductor newcomer has delivered. We flag that exactly as hard as the negative DCF. The defensible answer sits in between, which is why we anchor on a forward-sales scenario grid.

    8Management, Capital & Capital Allocation

    Cerebras is founder-led by deeply technical operators: CEO Andrew Feldman (who holds ~$1.9B of stock) and CTO Sean Lie (~$1B) — alignment via large equity stakes is a genuine strength. The May IPO raised ~$6.4B gross (the largest US semiconductor IPO ever), a significant portion of it secondary — existing holders, including UAE-linked investors, sold roughly $5B. Post-IPO the balance sheet is a fortress: ~$3.3B cash against ~$0.3B debt, so funding the data-center buildout is not a going-concern question. That is a real positive.

    Two capital-structure overhangs matter, though. First, OpenAI holds an option on ~33M shares at nominal value — a dilution claw on the upside. Second, the phased lockup releases ~171M shares (~5x the IPO) by ~Oct/Nov 2026, at which point early investors with cost bases between $0.85 and $107 can sell. Capital allocation is entirely reinvestment (data centers, R&D), which is appropriate for the stage but means no buyback or dividend cushion and continued cash burn.

    10Options & Positioning

    Call-heavy, but braced for violence. In the near-dated $150–280 band, call open interest (~40K) outweighs puts (~19K), a put/call ratio near 0.46, with the largest single OI at the $220 call — an upside magnet just above spot. But the more telling number is implied volatility: ATM IV runs ~110–120% (wings 130–155%), roughly 3–4x a megacap like Meta. The market is pricing enormous two-way uncertainty into a halved, pre-profit IPO. Downside hedges cluster at the $150–180 puts.

    Dealer gamma is mildly net-positive but tiny in magnitude, so it offers little stabilization — CBRS remains a high-beta, headline-driven tape. Net read: speculative dip-buyers are leaning long through calls, but ~110% IV plus a downtrend says this is a trader's stock, not an investor's entry — another argument to wait rather than chase. Note that Q2 earnings (~August) coincide with lockup releases: a compound volatility event.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    What would move us toward Buy: (1) gross margin inflecting back toward or above the high-40s as own data centers come online — proving the compression was temporary; (2) genuine customer diversification beyond OpenAI (a second large anchor that is neither OpenAI nor UAE-linked); (3) the lockup clearing without a sustained break, showing the supply is absorbed; (4) a technical base / trend reversal (reclaiming the 50- and 200-day averages) — for a momentum discipline, that alone would matter; (5) a cheaper multiple (a further de-rate toward ~25–30x FY27 sales with the story intact).

    What would move us toward Sell: (1) an OpenAI slowdown, renegotiation, or funding stumble that impairs the backlog; (2) gross margin failing to inflect (the rent-back drag proving structural); (3) the lockup triggering a sustained, supply-driven breakdown; (4) revenue decelerating faster than the ~69% guide as data-center constraints bite. Any two of these would tip the risk/reward negative.

    14Rating & Conclusion

    HOLD, 12-month target $210 (roughly flat). Cerebras is one of the most genuinely differentiated hardware companies to go public in years — the largest chip ever built, the fastest AI inference, a $24.6B backlog, marquee customers, and a fortress balance sheet. We are not bearish on the company. We are disciplined on the stock.

    At $206 — still ~53x forward sales with a −30% operating margin — the shares price much of the story to perfection even after halving; the celebrated “diversification” is a rotation of concentration onto a cash-burning OpenAI; growth is gated by data-center construction the company does not fully control; and ~5x the IPO float unlocks into the fall. For a momentum-oriented investor, buying a downtrending stock below both moving averages ahead of a supply wall is stepping in front of the tape. This is a “when,” not an “if” name: we expect to want to own it once margins inflect, the lockup clears, and either the multiple or the trend improves. Today the risk/reward is balanced-to-negative (bear ~$120, bull ~$300), and the honest call is to wait. This is a view, not a guarantee — if the margin inflection and OpenAI ramp arrive early, the bull case at ~$300 is real, and we would upgrade.
