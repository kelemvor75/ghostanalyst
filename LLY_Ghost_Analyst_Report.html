1Investment Thesis
  • The undisputed leader of the largest growth market in pharma history — at a premium price that the growth still arguably justifies. Eli Lilly is the dominant force in GLP-1/incretin therapy, the fastest-growing drug class ever. Mounjaro (diabetes) and Zepbound (obesity), both tirzepatide, generated ~$36B in combined 2025 sales and together surpassed Merck's Keytruda as the world's best-selling medicine. Zepbound holds 71% of new U.S. obesity prescriptions, and tirzepatide is widely regarded as clinically superior to Novo's semaglutide on efficacy and tolerability. 2026 guidance calls for revenue of $80-83B (a 25% midpoint increase that beat consensus) and non-GAAP EPS of $33.50-35.00. This is a ~$1T company still compounding at 25%.
  • A pipeline catalyst and a policy tailwind could each independently re-accelerate an already-soaring business. Two near-term catalysts stand out. First, orforglipron (branded Foundayo) — Lilly's once-daily oral GLP-1 pill — has an expected FDA decision in Q2 2026 via a national priority voucher. A pill is far easier to manufacture, ship, and scale than weekly injections, and could reset the market by reaching patients who refuse needles. Second, Medicare coverage of obesity drugs begins by at least July 2026 (a Trump drug-pricing deal), which CEO David Ricks says opens access to 40 million new beneficiaries — 'quite expansive to volume.' Either could extend the growth runway materially.
  • The valuation is rich and a DCF screams caution — so this is a 'quality at a demanding price,' not a bargain. At ~25x forward earnings, ~15x sales, and ~32x EV/EBITDA, Lilly is priced for continued excellence, and a standard DCF returns roughly $487 — less than half the price — a genuine valuation warning we do not dismiss. But for a business growing 25% with 49% operating margins, 32% ROIC, a fortress balance sheet, a pill catalyst, and a Medicare tailwind, a ~1.5 PEG is defensible, and the analyst consensus ($1,264) sits ~13% above the price with even the low target at the current level. We rate BUY — the premium is real, but so is the growth that earns it; we simply size conviction with eyes open to the valuation risk.

    4Price Action & The Leadership Premium

    Lilly has climbed toward $1,113, near its 52-week high of $1,183, roughly doubling off its low as the GLP-1 thesis compounded. The path was driven by fundamentals: the Q2 2025 Zepbound surge, the February 2026 guidance calling for 25% growth (which beat consensus), and the April 2026 Q1 report with continued orforglipron Phase 3 success. The lone visible drawdown catalyst was HSBC's March downgrade (target cut to $850 on valuation concerns) — a reminder that not everyone is comfortable at this multiple.

    The revenue trajectory is the cleanest expression of the thesis: quarterly revenue climbed from $13.5B to $19.3B over five quarters — extraordinary for a company of this scale. This is not a turnaround or a hope; it is a mega-cap growing like a mid-cap, driven by genuine clinical superiority and unprecedented end-market demand. Notably, the stock has recently lagged the S&P despite this strength, suggesting some of the GLP-1 enthusiasm has cooled into a more show-me posture ahead of the orforglipron decision — potentially a constructive setup if the pill is approved.

    5The GLP-1 Franchise & The Oral Catalyst

    The investment case rests on the most dramatic therapeutic and commercial story in modern pharma. GLP-1 receptor agonists, originally for type 2 diabetes, have become blockbuster obesity treatments — and the addressable market is, in CEO Ricks' word, 'gigantic' (20-25 million patients currently on these medicines, against a far larger eligible population).

    Lilly is winning the injectable war and is positioned to win the oral one. Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) is a dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist widely viewed as superior to Novo's semaglutide on both weight loss (~20% at high doses) and tolerability. Zepbound holds 71% of new U.S. obesity scripts. The next battleground is oral therapy: orforglipron (Foundayo), Lilly's once-daily pill, has an expected Q2 2026 FDA decision via a national priority voucher. Pills are dramatically easier to manufacture and distribute than cold-chain injectables, and could expand the market to needle-averse patients and supply-constrained geographies. Lilly has positive Phase 3 data across diabetes, obesity, and maintenance (the ATTAIN-MAINTAIN switch study).
    A $50B+ manufacturing moat backs the growth. Since 2020, Lilly has committed more than $50 billion to U.S. manufacturing — including a $6.5B Texas API plant, a $4.5B Indiana 'foundry,' and a $1.2B Puerto Rico facility, much of it tied specifically to the orforglipron oral pipeline. In a market where demand has chronically outstripped supply, manufacturing capacity is a genuine competitive weapon, not just capex. This is a structural advantage Novo and would-be entrants cannot replicate quickly.

    The Medicare catalyst compounds this: coverage of obesity drugs begins by at least July 2026 under a drug-pricing agreement, opening access to ~40 million beneficiaries who were previously uncovered — a step-change in the insured population for a company that already can barely make enough product. Together, the oral launch and Medicare access represent two independent, near-term volume accelerants on top of an already-25%-growing base.

    6Business Model, Segments & Competition

    Lilly's revenue is overwhelmingly product-driven — FY2025 product revenue of ~$61.0B versus ~$4.2B in collaboration and other revenue — and increasingly concentrated in cardiometabolic health, where GLP-1 products drove roughly 65% of Q1 2026 revenue. Geographically, the U.S. dominates at ~$43.5B, with Europe ~$11.6B, and smaller contributions from Japan (~$2.1B), China (~$2.0B), and other markets (~$6.1B). Beyond GLP-1, Lilly has real franchises in oncology (Verzenio, Jaypirca), immunology (Taltz), and Alzheimer's (Kisunla) — diversification that the GLP-1 spotlight tends to obscure.

    The competitive dynamic is essentially a duopoly with Novo Nordisk, and Lilly is winning. While Lilly guides to 25% growth, Novo warned of sales and profit declining 5-13% in 2026 as semaglutide pricing falls — a stark divergence that has driven the 'LLY premium vs NVO value-reset' trade. Novo is fighting back (higher-dose Wegovy, CagriSema, oral semaglutide, amycretin in Phase 3), so the war is not over. But Lilly's combination of a superior molecule, a manufacturing lead, and the orforglipron oral edge has put it firmly ahead.

    7Financial Model, Valuation & The DCF Question

    We must address the valuation head-on, because it is the crux of the bear case. At ~$1,113, Lilly trades at ~25x forward earnings, ~15x sales, and ~32x EV/EBITDA — a clear premium. And a standard discounted-cash-flow model returns roughly $487, less than half the current price. We do not wave this away.

    How to read the DCF flag honestly. A DCF of ~$487 reflects that Lilly's current free-cash-flow yield is low (~1.3%) because the company is investing massively in manufacturing and R&D ahead of the revenue — capex and pipeline spend that depress near-term FCF but build the future franchise. A DCF anchored to today's depressed FCF therefore understates a business whose cash generation is set to inflect sharply as the $50B manufacturing build matures and operating leverage kicks in on 25% revenue growth. The DCF is a genuine valuation warning — it says there is no margin of safety and the stock is priced for execution — but it is not the right single anchor for a company in a heavy-investment, high-growth phase. The forward-earnings and PEG lenses are more appropriate here.

    On those lenses, the picture is more defensible: ~25x forward earnings for 25% growth is a ~1.0-1.5 PEG, reasonable for a franchise of this quality, and the multiple compresses to ~17x on FY2030 consensus EPS ($63.53) — i.e., the growth de-rates the stock over time if it simply delivers. Our $1,260 target aligns with consensus and implies ~25x a normalized forward EPS. The sensitivity grid frames the range.

    8Management, Capital & Pipeline Depth

    Lilly is led by CEO David Ricks, whose tenure has overseen one of the great value-creation runs in pharma history through disciplined R&D focus and the tirzepatide franchise. Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and confident: a growing dividend ($6.92/year, recently declared at $1.73/quarter) plus ongoing buybacks (~$708M in a single recent quarter), funded by prodigious cash generation. The balance sheet carries $43B of debt against $5B cash, but with 49% operating margins and an Altman Z of 9.3, leverage is comfortably serviced — the debt funds the manufacturing build, not financial fragility.

    The pipeline is the answer to the patent-cliff bear case. The legitimate long-term risk is that tirzepatide loses exclusivity in the early 2030s. Lilly's response is aggressive reinvestment: orforglipron (oral, near-term), retatrutide (a next-gen triple agonist showing strong obesity data), plus a wave of business development (announced acquisitions of Orna, Centessa, Kelonia, Ajax, Curevo, and others) to broaden beyond GLP-1. The bet is that the cash flowing from tirzepatide funds the next generation before the cliff arrives. Execution is not guaranteed, but the war chest and R&D engine are formidable, and management is clearly playing for the post-tirzepatide era already.

    11Analyst Sentiment

    Wall Street is solidly bullish, though with a healthy minority of caution that befits the valuation. The consensus target is ~$1,264 (median $1,270, high $1,500 from Citigroup, low $830 from Berenberg), implying ~13% upside, and crucially even the low end of the *current* target range (~$1,119) sits right at the price — the bears see limited downside. The rating distribution is strongly positive: roughly 24-26 Buy/Strong Buy, ~5 Hold, and ~2 Sell across ~30 analysts.

    The honest read: a bullish Street, but the valuation debate is live. The bull majority points to the GLP-1 dominance, the orforglipron catalyst, and 25% growth. But the dissent is real and reasoned — HSBC cut its target to $850 in March on valuation, Berenberg sits at $830, and even some bulls (Berenberg's Hold at $1,135) acknowledge the multiple leaves little room for error. This is not a controversial 'is the business good' debate — everyone agrees it is exceptional. It is purely a 'what should you pay' debate, which is exactly the right way to frame a leader trading near all-time highs. We side with the bulls on franchise quality while respecting that the valuation skeptics are not wrong to flag the premium.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    We would move to STRONG BUY if: (1) orforglipron is approved with a strong label and launches well, validating the oral thesis; (2) Medicare volume materially exceeds expectations; or (3) the stock pulls back toward $950-1,000 (the low-20s forward multiple), restoring a margin of safety to an elite franchise.

    We would move to HOLD if the valuation expands further without commensurate fundamental progress (e.g., toward 30x+ forward earnings), if orforglipron disappoints or is delayed, or if Novo's counter-attack visibly compresses GLP-1 pricing. We would turn more cautious still if the patent-cliff math began to look unaddressed — i.e., if the pipeline (retatrutide and beyond) stumbled while tirzepatide's LOE approached. The thesis is that an exceptional, fast-growing franchise justifies a premium as long as the growth and pipeline deliver; a stumble on either, at this multiple, would remove the support.

    14Rating & Conclusion

    Eli Lilly is, by almost any measure, one of the highest-quality businesses in the public market — the dominant leader of GLP-1/incretin therapy, the largest growth market in pharma history. It is growing revenue 25% at a ~$1T scale, with 83% gross margins, 49% operating margins, 32% ROIC, and a fortress balance sheet. It owns the world's best-selling medicine, has a clinically superior molecule, a $50B+ manufacturing moat, an oral-pill catalyst (orforglipron, Q2 decision), and a 40-million-beneficiary Medicare tailwind — all while its chief rival guides to declining sales. The franchise quality is not in question.

    The valuation is. At ~25x forward earnings, ~15x sales, and ~32x EV/EBITDA — with a DCF returning less than half the price — Lilly is priced for continued excellence and offers no margin of safety. We take that warning seriously: this is not a bargain, and a premium multiple on a stock near all-time highs leaves little room for disappointment. But for a business compounding at 25% with multiple independent catalysts and a ~1.5 PEG, the premium is defensible, and the analyst consensus sits 13% above the price with even the bears at current levels.

    BUY — conviction 70/100, target $1,260 (+13%). Own the best franchise in pharma — the clear GLP-1 leader, compounding at 25% with elite margins, a pill catalyst, and a Medicare tailwind. We moderate conviction for a demanding valuation (the DCF at ~$487 is a genuine flag, and there is no margin of safety here), so this is a 'quality at a full price' BUY, not a deep-value one. The orforglipron decision and Medicare rollout are the near-term swing factors; the patent cliff is the long-term one the pipeline must answer. For investors who can own a premium leader through volatility, Lilly remains the premier way to play the obesity/cardiometabolic supercycle — but size it knowing the price already reflects much of the greatness.
