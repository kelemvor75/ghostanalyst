The setup in one line: Broadcom is the dominant merchant supplier of custom AI accelerators (XPUs) and AI networking — the company arming the hyperscalers' custom-silicon wave — with AI revenue up 143% and Q3 guided to grow over 200% year-over-year. Paradoxically, the stock fell ~15% in early June (a "priced-for-perfection" reaction to a blowout quarter) and now trades ~21% below its highs, opening a ~26% gap to the $498 consensus. We rate it BUY — an elite franchise on sale after a sentiment-driven dip, with customer concentration the key risk.
1Investment Thesis
  • The arms dealer of the custom-silicon era. As hyperscalers race to design their own AI chips to reduce dependence on Nvidia, Broadcom is the partner that makes it possible — supplying the custom XPU design IP, SerDes, and the AI networking (Tomahawk/Jericho) that ties clusters together. AI semiconductor revenue hit $10.8B in Q2 FY26 (+143% YoY) and management guides Q3 AI revenue to grow over 200% YoY to $16.0B. Hock Tan has disclosed six core custom-silicon customers — including Google, Meta, OpenAI, and Anthropic (a $10B order) — underpinning a $100B+ AI revenue ambition.
  • A dual-engine model: hyper-growth silicon plus a recurring software annuity. Beyond chips, Broadcom's Infrastructure Software segment (~$27B, ~42% of revenue) — anchored by VMware Cloud Foundation — is a high-margin, sticky, recurring-revenue base that funds the balance sheet and smooths the semiconductor cycle. Consolidated gross margins are ~68%, and Broadcom is a free-cash-flow machine ($26.9B in FY25), an unusual combination of growth and cash generation at this scale.
  • An elite franchise on sale — if you can hold the concentration risk. The bull case: AI revenue compounding, a software annuity, and ~26% upside to the $498 consensus after the June dip. The risks are real and specific: extreme customer concentration (a handful of customers drive AI revenue; the 10-Q flags the loss of any one as material), a premium valuation (~33x FY26E EPS), $64.9B of debt and $97.8B of goodwill from the acquisition-driven history, and the "priced-for-perfection" dynamic that the ~15% post-earnings drop exposed. We rate it BUY for investors who can stomach the volatility around a concentrated, richly-valued megacap.

    4Price Action & The AI Revenue Ramp

    AVGO is up ~79% over the trailing year, but the chart's defining feature is the early-June dislocation: the stock fell ~15% after Q2 earnings (marked) — not on bad numbers, but because management didn't raise its $100B AI-chip target. That selloff rippled across the semiconductor complex. The stock has since recovered toward ~$394, still ~21% below its $495 high.

    The thesis in one picture: total revenue (navy) is climbing, but the AI-semiconductor component (green) is the engine — $8.4B → $10.8B in two quarters (+143% YoY), with Q3 guided to $16.0B (>200% YoY) inside a $29.4B total-revenue guide. This is the steepest large-cap AI-revenue ramp in the market.

    5Recent Developments & The June Selloff

    Q2 FY26 (reported June 3) was, on the numbers, exceptional. Record revenue of $22.2B (+48% YoY), AI semiconductor revenue of $10.8B (+143% YoY, above forecast), adjusted EBITDA of $15.2B (69% of revenue), and record free cash flow. Management guided Q3 to $29.4B revenue (+84% YoY) with AI revenue growing over 200% YoY to $16.0B. By almost any standard, a blowout.

    Yet the stock fell ~15% on June 5 — the "priced-for-perfection" paradox. Two things drove the drop: (1) headline revenue of $22.19B came a fraction below the $22.27B consensus, and (2) crucially, CEO Hock Tan declined to raise the full-year $100B AI-chip-sales target that bulls had hoped to see lifted. After a ~79% run, expectations were so elevated that an excellent-but-not-raised quarter triggered profit-taking. The selloff cascaded across semiconductors — it was the proximate trigger for the broader early-June drawdown (and names like Micron fell in sympathy on the "Broadcom read-through"). The episode is a case study in sentiment versus fundamentals: nothing in the quarter impaired the thesis.

    The customer roster keeps strengthening. Tan disclosed six core custom-silicon customers driving AI revenue — publicly including Google, Meta, OpenAI, and Anthropic (which placed a ~$10B order). The pipeline of prospective XPU customers continues to expand as more hyperscalers and AI labs pursue custom silicon, extending the multi-year revenue runway.

    6Business Model & Competitive Position

    Broadcom operates two complementary segments:

    SegmentWhat it does~% of Rev (FY25)
    Semiconductor SolutionsCustom AI accelerators (XPUs), AI networking (Tomahawk/Jericho switches), broadband, wireless, server storage — the AI growth engine~58%
    Infrastructure SoftwareVMware Cloud Foundation, mainframe and security software — high-margin, sticky, recurring revenue~42%

    Competitive moat — the indispensable XPU partner. Broadcom's edge in custom silicon is its combination of best-in-class SerDes/IP, packaging expertise, and deep co-design relationships with hyperscalers — a position that takes years and reference designs to replicate. Its closest custom-silicon rival is Marvell; in AI networking it competes with Nvidia's NVLink/InfiniBand ecosystem and Arista/Cisco. The strategic logic is powerful: as customers adopt XPUs, they also consume Broadcom networking, creating a compounding attach. The flip side — and the central risk — is concentration: a small number of customers drive the AI business, and Broadcom's own 10-Q explicitly warns that losing any one of its top custom-accelerator customers would have a material adverse effect. The software segment (VMware) diversifies the revenue base but came with significant debt and customer-pricing controversy.

    7Financial Model & Valuation

    Broadcom is profitable and cash-generative, so it can be valued on earnings and FCF — but the multiple embeds high growth. At ~$394 the stock trades ~61x trailing and ~34x FY26E EPS, compressing rapidly to ~20x FY27E as the Street models EPS scaling from ~$11.57 (FY26) to ~$19.40 (FY27) and ~$25.95 (FY28) on the AI ramp. The consensus $498 target implies ~26% upside — a gap that widened when the June selloff knocked the price down while estimates held.

    8Management & Capital Structure

    Hock Tan — one of the most effective capital allocators in tech. President & CEO since 2006, Tan built Broadcom through a series of large, disciplined acquisitions (Broadcom Corp, CA, Symantec, VMware), each integrated with aggressive cost discipline and a focus on durable, high-margin franchises. His credibility with investors is a genuine asset — which is also why his decision not to raise the $100B AI target moved the stock so sharply. The board has explicitly tied his go-forward (FY28–30) incentive compensation to AI revenue, aligning management with the core thesis.

Leverage is manageable given the FCF, and the dominant in-sourcing risk is mitigated by the fact that hyperscalers use Broadcom precisely to design their custom chips.

    14Price Target & Conclusion

    We initiate Broadcom at BUY with a base-case target of $498 (the Street consensus, ~26% above the post-dip price). Broadcom is the arms dealer of the custom-silicon era — the indispensable partner enabling hyperscalers to build their own AI accelerators — with AI revenue growing 143% and guided to exceed 200% next quarter, a roster of six core customers including Google, Meta, OpenAI, and Anthropic, and a $100B+ AI ambition. Layered on a high-margin VMware software annuity and a $26.9B free-cash-flow engine, it is one of the highest-quality franchises in all of technology.

    The opportunity is sharpened by the June dislocation: a blowout quarter met a ~15% selloff purely because Hock Tan didn't raise the full-year target — a "priced-for-perfection" reaction that left the stock ~21% below its high and ~26% below the consensus target while the fundamentals strengthened. That is an unusually favorable setup versus the small-caps trading above their targets. The honest caveats remain: customer concentration is the named risk in Broadcom's own filings, the valuation is full on near-term earnings, and the elevated expectations make the stock prone to sharp swings on guidance nuance. We rate it BUY as a core AI-silicon holding — an elite franchise on sale after a sentiment-driven dip, sized for the concentration and volatility.

    Bottom line: BUY, $498 target (~26% upside). The dominant custom-AI-silicon and networking franchise — AI revenue +143% (Q3 guided >200%), six marquee XPU customers, a VMware software annuity, and $26.9B FCF — available ~21% below its high after a "priced-for-perfection" June selloff that didn't impair the thesis. Customer concentration and a full valuation are the risks; the post-dip gap to a strongly-bullish $498 consensus is the opportunity.
