Investment Thesis
The NAND Flash Supercycle — SanDisk Is the Purest Play. SanDisk’s Q2 FY2026 revenue of $3.03B (+61% YoY) with 51% gross margins and non-GAAP EPS of $6.20 (beat $4.85 estimate by 28%) confirmed a historic inflection.[1] NAND contract prices surged 38% in Q1 2026 as AI data centers consumed flash storage at unprecedented rates. Global NAND capacity is effectively sold out through 2026.
From Consumer Brand to Enterprise AI Powerhouse. Since spinning off from Western Digital in February 2025, SanDisk has pivoted from consumer SD cards to enterprise SSDs for AI infrastructure. The company launched the world’s first 256TB enterprise SSD in early 2026 and is developing High-Bandwidth Flash (HBF) — a new architecture that bridges the speed gap between NAND and HBM.[2]
At 7x FY2027 Earnings With 118% EPS Growth, This Is Not Expensive. The stock has surged 425% in 6 months, which sounds expensive until you see the math: FY2027E EPS of $87.73 means the stock trades at just 7.2x forward earnings. That’s cheaper than Micron. NAND pricing power and the sold-out supply backdrop provide visibility rarely seen in memory.

1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

SNDK has risen from $28 (April 2025 low) to $635 — a 2,175% move in 12 months, making it the best-performing large-cap tech stock in 2026. The stock hit $778 before pulling back 18% amid the TurboQuant selloff on March 26, which hit all memory names (MU -20%, SNDK -7%, WDC -9%). Revenue has inflected from $1.7B to $3.0B in two quarters with Q3 guidance projected near $4B.

1B  |  THE TURBOQUANT OVERHANG

Google’s TurboQuant memory compression algorithm (March 24) hit all memory stocks including SNDK. Our view mirrors our MU thesis: the Jevons Paradox applies. More efficient memory use drives more AI adoption, expanding total storage demand. NAND capacity is sold out through 2026 regardless. The selloff created a better entry — it didn’t change the fundamentals.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Datacenter / Cloud (~50%, fastest growing): Enterprise SSDs for AI training data lakes, inference storage, and cloud infrastructure. The 256TB enterprise SSD consolidates dozens of racks into one unit. AI-driven demand growing 80%+ YoY.[2]
Edge / Client (~30%): SSDs for PCs, laptops, gaming consoles. Tailwind from on-device AI requiring 512GB–1TB storage as baseline. Smartphone storage shifting from 256GB to 1TB for local LLMs.
Consumer (~20%): SD cards, USB drives, portable SSDs. Legacy business providing cash flow. Declining as a percentage of revenue.
