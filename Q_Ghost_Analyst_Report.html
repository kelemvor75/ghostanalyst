Investment Thesis
The Consumables Monopoly Inside Every Semiconductor Fab. Qnity supplies the chemical mechanical planarization (CMP) pads, slurries, photoresists, and metallization materials that are consumed during every step of chip manufacturing. These materials are used up — every wafer processed requires fresh consumables. When TSMC, Samsung, or Intel make more chips, Qnity sells more product. Q4 FY2025 EPS of $0.82 beat estimates of $0.55 by 49%.[1]
DuPont Spinoff Unlocks Pure-Play Valuation. Spun off from DuPont in November 2025, Qnity is now a standalone semiconductor materials company with $4.75B in annual revenue, 30% EBITDA margins, and 63% gross margins. The NVIDIA collaboration announced March 18 to accelerate AI-driven materials R&D positions Qnity at the frontier of next-generation chip fabrication.[2]
Razor-and-Blade Model With 63% Gross Margins. Unlike equipment companies that sell machines once, Qnity sells consumables that must be repurchased with every wafer. This creates recurring, high-margin revenue that grows with chip production volumes. FY2026 guidance of $4.97-5.17B revenue and $3.55-3.95 adjusted EPS implies 10-12% growth.

1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

Q has rallied 64% from its post-spinoff low of $70.50 to $115, driven by the Q4 earnings beat (+49% EPS surprise), NVIDIA collaboration announcement, and Apple manufacturing program inclusion. The stock pulled back 18% from its $140.60 high amid the Iran conflict’s impact on semiconductor supply chain sentiment. Revenue growth is steady at 8-10% — not explosive, but highly predictable given the consumables model.

The Iran conflict has specifically pressured Q because of its exposure to Taiwan and South Korea fabs. If ocean shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz are disrupted, component delivery times extend. However, Qnity’s materials are critical consumables — fabs cannot operate without them — so demand is inelastic even if logistics are disrupted. The selloff is an opportunity in a supply-constrained market.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Semiconductor Technologies (~70%): CMP pads and slurries, photoresists, advanced cleans, metallization, post-etch residue removers. These are consumed during chip fabrication — every wafer requires fresh materials. Growing with AI-driven chip production volumes.
Interconnect Solutions (~30%): Laminates (Pyralux, Kapton), dry film photoresists (Riston), PCB materials, and advanced packaging materials. Growing with advanced packaging adoption (chiplets, 2.5D/3D stacking).

Qnity’s competitive advantage is the razor-and-blade model applied to semiconductor manufacturing. Once a fab qualifies Qnity’s CMP slurry or photoresist for a specific node, switching is extremely costly — requalification takes 6-12 months and risks yield loss. This creates deep customer lock-in with 63% gross margins. The consumables nature means revenue grows linearly with chip production volumes, providing natural leverage to the AI capex supercycle without the cyclicality of equipment companies.

The NVIDIA collaboration announced March 18 positions Qnity to co-develop next-generation materials for AI chip fabrication. The Taiwan R&D facility acquisition on March 6 deepens Qnity’s proximity to TSMC, the world’s most advanced foundry. Apple’s inclusion of Qnity in its American Manufacturing program on March 26 provides brand validation and potential supply chain preferencing.

9  |  WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from BUY if:

Semiconductor production volumes decline for two consecutive quarters. Consumables revenue is directly tied to wafer starts — a production downturn hits Q directly.
Gross margins fall below 58%. Would indicate pricing pressure or unfavorable product mix shift.
A major customer (TSMC, Samsung) qualifies a competing supplier for key materials. Would break the switching-cost moat.
