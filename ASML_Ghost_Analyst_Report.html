1B  |  RECENT DEVELOPMENTS

Three material events in the past week: (1) SK Hynix placed a $7.97B order for ASML EUV tools on March 24 — the largest single order ever publicly disclosed, driven by HBM capacity expansion.[2] (2) Google’s TurboQuant announcement on March 24 caused some sector pressure, but ASML is insulated — memory chips still require EUV regardless of software efficiency gains. 2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
EUV Systems (~55%): The crown jewel. Each EUV machine costs ~$400M. ASML is the sole supplier globally. Customers include TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK Hynix — every advanced chipmaker. Orders have doubled as AI drives leading-edge capacity expansion.
DUV Systems (~20%): Deep ultraviolet lithography for less advanced but still critical chips. China has been a significant DUV buyer, though this is expected to decline in 2026 due to export restrictions.
Installed Base Management (~25%): Service, upgrades, and spare parts for the thousands of ASML machines operating worldwide. Machines last decades, creating a growing recurring revenue base. Higher margin than system sales.
Why ASML Is the Ultimate Monopoly

An EUV machine contains over 100,000 parts, requires mirrors polished to sub-atomic precision, and uses a tin plasma light source that produces wavelengths of just 13.5 nanometers. No other company has successfully built one. The only Chinese competitor is a prototype from SMEE that industry experts say won’t reach production capability until 2028 at the earliest — and even then, ASML’s machines will be a generation ahead. This monopoly is reinforced by export controls: the Netherlands, U.S., and Japan have agreed to restrict advanced lithography equipment sales to China.

9  |  WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from BUY if:

Backlog declines below €30B for two consecutive quarters. Would signal demand is weakening, not just shifting between quarters.
SMEE demonstrates a production-capable EUV machine. Would break the monopoly thesis — though this is years away.
China export restrictions remove >20% of revenue with no offset. Would require significant backlog from other customers to compensate.
Gross margins fall below 48% for two quarters. Would indicate pricing pressure or unfavorable product mix.
