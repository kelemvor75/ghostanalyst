    Adeia has roughly tripled off its 52-week low of $11.61 to ~$30, as the market re-rated it from a declining Pay-TV IP licensor into a hybrid-bonding/AI-packaging royalty play. The path was catalyst-driven: the December 2025 Disney IP settlement, the March 2026 AMD hybrid-bonding license (which ended a four-month patent dispute and converted litigation into multi-year royalties), and steady semiconductor licensing momentum. The one sharp drawdown was the May 2026 -17% drop when CEO Paul Davis announced his planned departure alongside Q1 results — a reminder that leadership continuity is a real concern during this pivot.

    A note on the revenue pattern: Adeia's quarterly revenue is deliberately lumpy — it swings with the timing of license signings and catch-up payments (note Q4 2025's $182.6M spike from deal timing). This is normal for an IP-licensing model and should not be read as instability; the right lens is trailing-twelve-month and annual revenue (guided to $395-435M for 2026), plus the recurring-revenue base underneath. The lumpiness does, however, make quarter-to-quarter results hard to predict and is part of why the stock carries elevated short interest.

    5The Hybrid Bonding Royalty Thesis

    The core idea is simple and powerful: Adeia does not build chips or equipment — it owns foundational patents on how chips are bonded, and collects royalties when others use those methods. As hybrid bonding transitions from a niche high-end technique to a mainstream requirement across logic, HBM, and 3D NAND, Adeia's licensable base expands with the entire market.

    This week's Samsung news is a direct validation. Samsung published IEEE research quantifying that hybrid copper bonding (HCB) reduces thermal resistance by 20%+ and stack height by 15%+ versus conventional TCB, and confirmed it will adopt HCB starting at 16-layer HBM4E (running alongside TCB first), then fully at HBM5. Independently, Korea's KIPRO institute warned that TSMC and Adeia hold the leading hybrid-bonding patent positions — and that Korean memory makers face rising patent-dispute risk as the technology hits mass production around 2026. In other words: the industry is standardizing on the exact technology Adeia patents, and the patent leverage sits with Adeia, not the Korean memory giants.
    The 2027 renewal catalyst is the crux. Here is the most important, underappreciated detail: Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron are already licensed under pre-existing agreements — but those legacy deals carry no per-unit royalties. That dynamic is expected to shift materially when the agreements renew in 2027 and beyond. As hybrid bonding becomes the standard for high-layer HBM (exactly the volume the AI boom is driving), converting flat-fee licenses into per-unit royalties on every bonded stack would be a step-change in Adeia's semiconductor revenue. ROTH models semiconductor revenue potentially reaching $50M+ quarterly by 2027 — a 40%+ growth vector.

    The AMD deal (March 2026) is the proof of concept: Adeia enforced its hybrid-bonding patents (AMD's 3D V-Cache stacks SRAM on compute dies using the technique), litigated for four months, and converted the dispute into a multi-year license. The same playbook — enforce, litigate if needed, license — applies to the broader industry as hybrid bonding proliferates. In 3D NAND, Adeia already has ~90% of the market licensed (Kioxia, SanDisk/Western Digital are current users).

    6Business Model, Segments & Competition

    Adeia operates two licensing segments. Media (the legacy core, ~$0.4B and the majority of revenue today) licenses patents for content discovery, personalization, and multi-screen experiences to Pay-TV, OTT/streaming, and consumer-electronics companies — a base that is mature and declining ~10-15% annually in its Pay-TV portion but stabilized by OTT growth (Amazon, Disney, Microsoft deals). Semiconductor (the growth engine, smaller today but expanding 40%+) licenses hybrid-bonding, advanced-packaging, and processing patents to chipmakers and memory companies. The strategic story is the mix shift from declining media toward growing semiconductor IP — which is what re-rated the stock.

    The competitive structure is favorable: IP licensing is a high-barrier, quasi-duopolistic business where a handful of platforms control essential patents for entire ecosystems. In semiconductors, Adeia's peers are Rambus (memory IP) and, more loosely, ARM (processor IP) — but Adeia's hybrid-bonding portfolio is differentiated and, per KIPRO, among the two leading positions globally alongside TSMC (which generally practices its patents rather than licensing them broadly). In media, peers include InterDigital and Dolby. The moat is the patents themselves: essential, numerous (13,750 and growing), and validated by courts and settlements.

    7Financial Model, Valuation & The DCF Caveat

    Our $38 target implies ~25% upside and is built on ~22-24x a normalized FY2027 EPS (~$1.55-1.70) — a multiple that closes part, but not all, of the >30% discount to IP-licensing peers, crediting the hybrid-bonding growth while discounting for lumpiness and the CEO transition. Before the model, an honest note on the DCF.

    Why we do not anchor to the DCF here. A standard DCF returns ~$11 (-63%), but this is a case where DCF is the wrong tool. Adeia's cash flows are lumpy and event-driven (license signings, catch-up payments, litigation outcomes), and a DCF anchored to a trailing free-cash-flow snapshot badly understates a business whose semiconductor royalty base is set to inflect with the 2027 renewals and hybrid-bonding adoption. Unlike a stable-FCF compounder (where DCF is meaningful), an IP-licensing platform mid-transition is better valued on forward earnings, EV/EBITDA, and peer-relative multiples. We flag the DCF as a data point and a reminder that there is no deep margin of safety, but we do not treat it as the valuation anchor.

    On the appropriate lenses, the case is attractive: ~20x forward earnings and ~13x EV/EBITDA for a 100%-gross-margin, ~60%-EBITDA-margin business growing semiconductor revenue 40%+ is cheap, especially at a >30% discount to peers. The sensitivity grid frames the range.

    8Management, Capital & The CEO Transition

    The most important near-term risk is leadership. CEO Paul Davis announced in May 2026 that he will step down by Q4 2026 after four years that successfully transformed Adeia from a Pay-TV-dependent licensor into a semiconductor-IP growth story. The board has launched a search. A transition mid-pivot is a genuine concern — a new CEO could alter the semiconductor-focused strategy, and continuity matters most during a re-rating. The -17% drop on the announcement reflected this. We take it seriously, but note the strategy (enforce and license the hybrid-bonding portfolio) is well-established and patent-driven rather than dependent on a single executive.

    The balance sheet is moving the right way, and capital return signals confidence. Adeia paid down $28.1M of debt in Q1 (to ~$399M), repurchased $10M of stock against a $200M authorization, and pays a $0.05 quarterly dividend — all funded by strong cash generation (~$58M operating cash flow in Q1, ~60% EBITDA margins). The remaining ~$399M term loan is the main balance-sheet item to watch, but it is declining, was repriced lower, and is comfortably serviced. Altman Z of 5.34 confirms no solvency concern. Management buying back stock at these levels signals they view the shares as undervalued.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    We would move to STRONG BUY if: (1) the 2027 DRAM renewals are signed with per-unit royalty structures, confirming the inflection; (2) a strong CEO successor is named who reaffirms the semiconductor strategy; (3) Disney and/or Netflix are licensed, removing the media-litigation overhang; or (4) semiconductor revenue visibly accelerates toward the $50M+ quarterly target.

    We would move to HOLD if the CEO transition brings a strategic shift away from semiconductor focus, if the 2027 renewals disappoint on terms, or if hybrid-bonding adoption visibly slips (e.g., Samsung/SK Hynix push HBM4E timelines out materially). The thesis rests on hybrid bonding becoming standard and Adeia converting that into growing per-unit royalties; a stumble on either the adoption or the monetization would undermine the re-rating, though the >30% peer discount provides some downside cushion.

    14Rating & Conclusion

    Adeia is the differentiated, upstream way to own the hybrid-bonding transition that the rest of the market is playing through equipment makers (BESI, ASMPT) and memory companies (Samsung, SK Hynix). As a foundational patent holder — independently validated by Korea's KIPRO as holding one of the two leading hybrid-bonding portfolios globally — Adeia collects royalties as the technology becomes standard, regardless of which equipment or memory vendor wins. It is a 100%-gross-margin, ~60%-EBITDA-margin licensing platform, growing semiconductor revenue 40%+, with a freshly-validated thesis (Samsung's HCB research this week) and a concrete 2027 catalyst (DRAM license renewals that could convert flat fees into per-unit royalties).

    And it trades at ~20x forward earnings — a >30% discount to IP-licensing peers — with an improving balance sheet, a buyback, and a dividend. The risks are real and we do not minimize them: a CEO departure mid-pivot, inherently lumpy litigation-driven revenue, ~$399M of debt, and key media names (Disney, Netflix) still unlicensed. The DCF is unflattering (~$11) but is the wrong tool for a lumpy IP licensor; the forward-earnings and peer-relative lenses are far more favorable.

    BUY — conviction 67/100, target $38 (+25%). The royalty toll-road on hybrid bonding: Adeia collects as the technology standardizes, at a >30% discount to IP peers, with ~60% EBITDA margins and a freshly-validated thesis. The 2027 DRAM renewals are the catalyst that could convert flat licenses into per-unit royalties on every hybrid-bonded HBM stack. We moderate conviction for the CEO transition, revenue lumpiness, and debt — this is not a low-risk name — but for an investor who wants exposure to the hybrid-bonding supercycle without betting on which equipment maker or memory company wins, Adeia is the most asymmetric way to play it. It pairs naturally with the BESI/ASMPT equipment angle: they fight over who sells the tools; Adeia owns the patents either way.
