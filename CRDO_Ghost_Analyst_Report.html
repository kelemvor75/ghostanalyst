1Investment Thesis

The debate on Credo is lazy on both sides. Bulls call it a flawless AI-connectivity winner and stop there; bears call it too expensive after a 3.5x run and stop there. Both miss where the money is actually made. Credo's copper Active Electrical Cable (AEC) franchise is a genuine near-monopoly — management's phrase, "the standard for intra-rack AI connectivity," is not marketing — and it throws off ~68% gross margins and ~52% non-GAAP net margins. That dominance is real, and it is already in the price. The swing factor from here is something the bull case underweights and the bear case ignores: the optics inflection.

In the second half of fiscal 2027, Credo guides three distinct optical product lines — ZeroFlap optics, silicon photonics (bolstered by the ~$750M DustPhotonics acquisition), and optical DSPs — to each ramp past $100 million, more than $600 million of optical revenue in total. That inflection does two things at once. It extends the growth runway well beyond the AEC core, and — more importantly — it begins to cure the single most dangerous feature of this business: a concentration so extreme it is almost hard to believe. In the March quarter, the top four hyperscaler customers were ~87% of revenue (roughly 34%, 27%, 16% and 10%). The top two alone are ~61%. Any one of them pausing AI capex, or bringing connectivity in-house, would leave a crater.

So the real question is not "is AI connectivity hot." It is: can Credo replicate its AEC dominance in optics — against far larger rivals in Marvell, Broadcom and Coherent — fast enough to both sustain 80%+ growth and diversify its concentration before any one hyperscaler's digestion cycle exposes it? We think the odds favor Credo, and — as the valuation section shows — the price does not demand perfection the way the trailing multiple implies.

We initiate CRDO at BUY with a 12-month target of $300 (~15% above $259.90) — deliberately above the Street's ~$274 mean but below the most bullish targets ($325–350 from Evercore, BofA and Stifel), because the optics ramp has to land and the concentration risk is live. This is a measured BUY, not a table-pound: a best-in-class, momentum-intact compounder at a reasonable forward multiple, tempered by a concentration problem we refuse to bury and a tape that is priced to keep executing flawlessly.

  • The AEC monopoly is priced; the optics inflection is the upside. AECs are the standard for intra-rack AI connectivity at ~68% gross margin — that dominance is in the stock. The H2 FY2027 optics ramp (ZeroFlap, silicon photonics, optical DSPs; >$600M) is the underappreciated leg.
  • Concentration is the defining risk, and optics is the cure. ~87% of revenue is four hyperscalers (top two ~61%). The optics expansion both extends growth and begins to diversify that concentration — the two are linked, and that link is the thesis.
  • The model is elite and self-funding. FY2026 revenue +206% to $1.34B, ~52% non-GAAP net margin, non-GAAP net income +>5x to $662M, net cash even after the ~$750M DustPhotonics deal. Very few semis scale this fast while expanding margins.
  • Rich on trailing, reasonable on forward. ~75x trailing non-GAAP looks steep, but ~29x FY2028E for ~50% growth is a sub-1 PEG — and a discount to Astera Labs (46x sales vs 36x) with better net margins. The forward math supports the price.
  • A measured BUY for a momentum name priced for perfection. Momentum is intact (above both moving averages), but a blowout beat moved the stock just +1.3% and insiders are trimming into the rally. We buy the quality and the optics option — and size for a severe bear case (~$165).

    2The Model: Hypergrowth at Scale

    Start with what is not in dispute, because it is genuinely rare. In fiscal 2026 (ended May 2, 2026) Credo grew revenue 206% to $1.34 billion, and the March quarter alone — $437 million, up 157% year over year — exceeded the company's entire fiscal 2025 revenue. This was not growth bought with margin: non-GAAP gross margin expanded to ~68% even as the top line multiplied, non-GAAP net margin reached ~52%, and non-GAAP net income rose more than fivefold to $662 million. Full-year non-GAAP EPS of $3.46 was up 392%. The revenue line has gone up roughly sevenfold in two years while margins rose — operating leverage of a kind very few semiconductor companies ever demonstrate.

    The engine is the AEC. By pairing a low-power SerDes with a controller inside an active copper cable, Credo solved a real problem — reliable, energy-efficient, short-reach links inside the AI rack — and became the default choice as hyperscalers scaled GPU clusters. That is what drove the ramp, and it is why the gross margin is a structural ~68% rather than the 30–40% of commodity connectivity. The bull case rightly celebrates this. The discipline is to notice that the celebration is already reflected at ~75x trailing earnings, and that the sequential growth has cooled sharply — the March quarter rose just 7.4% quarter over quarter, down from +52% two quarters earlier. The parabola is maturing into a (still very fast) S-curve.

    The number that should stop a bull mid-sentence: ~87% of revenue in four customers. This is not a footnote risk — it is the central fact of the business. A single hyperscaler pausing AI capex, dual-sourcing, or in-sourcing connectivity would hit the P&L hard and fast, and the ~3.2 beta guarantees the stock would move more than the fundamentals. Every bull thesis on Credo is implicitly a bet that four specific companies keep spending. We rate the stock a BUY with that sentence in full view.

    3Revenue Architecture & the Optics Pivot

    Credo is a fabless designer of high-speed connectivity, and its strategic story in one line is a deliberate march from a single-product copper franchise into a multi-pillar copper-plus-optical platform. Reading the pillars in order shows both the opportunity and the execution bar:

    Product pillarRoleTrajectoryRead
    Active Electrical Cables (AEC)Core / monopolyThe FY26 growth engineThe standard for intra-rack AI connectivity; ~68% GM anchor
    Optical DSPsOptics pillar 1Ramping past $100M in FY27Competes with Marvell/Broadcom; the volume optics play
    Silicon Photonics (incl. DustPhotonics)Optics pillar 2Ramping (H2 FY27)~$750M acquisition; vertical integration into the optical engine
    ZeroFlap OpticsOptics pillar 3Ramping past $100M in FY27Reliability-differentiated optics; >$600M optics total in FY27
    SerDes / OmniConnect / IP / line-cardAdjacenciesGrowingOmniConnect (VSR SerDes + gearbox) adds ~$2–3K per GPU of TAM

    The logic is sound. AECs dominate inside the rack; optics is how you connect across racks and rows as clusters scale to hundreds of thousands of GPUs, and it is a far larger TAM. Owning both — with silicon photonics brought in-house via DustPhotonics — lets Credo sell a system-level solution rather than a component. The catch is that optics is not the AEC market: the incumbents (Marvell, Broadcom, Coherent, Lumentum) are larger and entrenched, and optical DSP/module margins are structurally below AEC margins, which is why management already guides long-term gross margin down to 63–65%. So the optics pillars are simultaneously the growth story, the diversification story, and the margin-normalization story. They have to work — and they have to work against tougher competition than Credo has faced in copper.

    4The Concentration Problem

    No single factor matters more to a Credo investment than customer concentration, so it earns its own section. In the fiscal fourth quarter, the top four customers accounted for ~87% of revenue, at roughly 34%, 27%, 16% and 10%. The two largest — almost certainly two of the mega-cap hyperscalers — are ~61% between them. This is the flip side of the AEC success story: Credo won the AI-rack connectivity socket at exactly the handful of companies building the largest GPU clusters, and those same companies now are the revenue.

    The risk is asymmetric and fast-acting. If one large customer digests inventory, shifts a design to a second source, or in-sources connectivity (all of which large hyperscalers have done to suppliers before), a double-digit share of revenue is exposed in a single quarter — and because the stock trades at a rich trailing multiple with a ~3.2 beta, the equity reaction would badly overshoot the fundamental hit. This is the mechanism behind our bear case, and it is not hypothetical: it is the base rate for concentrated component suppliers to hyperscalers.

    Which is why the optics inflection is the linchpin of the bull case, not just the growth case: every incremental optics dollar both grows the company and dilutes the top-four percentage. The single most important thing to watch over the next year is not the headline growth rate but whether concentration falls as optics scales. If it does, the multiple is defensible; if revenue keeps growing while concentration does not budge, the risk that is currently an abstraction becomes the story.

    10Management & Capital Structure

    Management. CEO Bill Brennan and CFO Dan Fleming have executed exceptionally — scaling revenue ~7x in two years while expanding margins and inflecting to substantial profitability is a rare operational record, and the DustPhotonics acquisition shows a management team willing to build the optics platform rather than hope for it. The one item to read carefully is insider selling: the CTO, CFO and COO have all trimmed shares through 2026. Those sales run through pre-arranged Rule 10b5-1 plans, the executives received large equity grants in May that keep them heavily aligned, and steady selling into a 3.5x rally is routine — so it is not a red flag. But it is a mild temper on the one-way-upside narrative: the people closest to the business are monetizing into the move, not adding to it.

    Capital structure is a strength. Credo ended fiscal 2026 with ~$1.44 billion of cash and short-term investments against only ~$25 million of debt, and remained in a net-cash position even after the ~$750 million DustPhotonics outlay in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. The business generates real cash at ~52% non-GAAP net margins, funds its own R&D and M&A, and pays no dividend — appropriately, given the reinvestment runway. There is no balance-sheet risk here; the entire risk is to the growth trajectory and the multiple that growth commands.

    Insiders monetizing, not adding: the 10b5-1 selling is not a warning by itself, but combined with a blowout beat that moved the stock just +1.3% and a Street already chasing to $350, it supports our measured stance — own the quality and the optics option, but respect that a lot of good news is already in the price.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    We initiate at BUY, and we hold that rating to falsifiable conditions in both directions.

    What would make us more aggressive. Evidence that the optics inflection is landing early and diversifying the base — optical DSP/silicon-photonics revenue tracking above the >$100M-per-pillar guide, and the top-four customer percentage visibly falling — would de-risk the core bear case and justify the bulls' higher multiples. We would move to BUY more aggressively on a pullback toward the 50-day that holds the uptrend, because the quality-versus-price trade-off improves with every dollar of correction while the thesis stays intact.

    What would move us to SELL. Any sign the concentration risk is materializing — a large customer guiding down, dual-sourcing, or in-sourcing; a fiscal 2027 growth trajectory that undershoots the 80% guide with no optics offset; the optics pillars slipping materially against Marvell/Broadcom/Coherent; or gross margin rolling toward the 63–65% floor faster than volume compensates. Any of those would break the "growth plus diversification" case that the valuation rests on, and with a ~75x trailing multiple and a ~3.2 beta, fair value would reset toward the low-$100s bear scenario quickly. In that event we would move to SELL rather than defend the target.

    14Conclusion & The Verdict

    Credo is one of the highest-quality growth stories in semiconductors, and the discipline is to hold that fact and its central risk in the same hand. The AEC franchise is a genuine near-monopoly throwing off ~68% gross margins; fiscal 2026 revenue tripled to $1.34 billion; the model is elite and self-funding; and the optics pivot — three pillars past $100M in the second half of fiscal 2027, silicon photonics brought in-house via DustPhotonics — is a real extension of both the growth runway and, crucially, the customer base. Against that sits an ~87% concentration in four hyperscalers, a rich trailing multiple, and a tape that already treats flawless execution as the baseline. The differentiated call is that these are the same question: the optics inflection is both the upside and the cure, and how it resolves is what an investor is underwriting.

    Analyst consensus is emphatically bullish. Of the analysts covering the name, sentiment runs roughly 13 Buy, 2 Hold, 0 Sell, and the June target hikes clustered high — Evercore initiated at $325, BofA moved to $340, Stifel to $350 — against a mean nearer $274. The Street, in short, is underwriting the optics inflection with conviction; our job is to credit it while keeping the concentration risk in full view.

    We initiate CRDO at BUY with a 12-month Price Target of $300 (~15% above $259.90, with a bull path to $400 and a bear case near $165). This is a measured BUY: a best-in-class, momentum-intact AI-connectivity compounder at a reasonable forward multiple whose optics pivot we expect to land. For a momentum-oriented investor it is a name to own with the trend and add on pullbacks that hold the 50-day, not one to chase at the high — and we have marked exactly what would move us more aggressively or to SELL.

    The verdict: BUY, 12-month target $300 (+15%). Elite growth (FY26 +206%, ~68% GM, ~52% net margin) and a reasonable forward multiple (~29x FY2028E, a discount to Astera Labs) against an ~87% customer concentration and a tape priced for perfection. Own the quality and the optics option; size for a ~3.2 beta and a severe bear case; add on pullbacks that hold the trend.
