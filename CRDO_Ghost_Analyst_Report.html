1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

CRDO has rallied from $20 to $120 over the past 12 months — a 500% move that mirrors the revenue trajectory almost exactly. The stock peaked at $90+ in January before the broader semiconductor selloff pulled it back, then recovered to $120 on the Q3 FY2026 earnings beat. Revenue growth of 193% YoY and 52% QoQ drove the re-rating.

The 78% discount to analyst consensus ($214) is the widest gap in the semiconductor sector. The stock is being re-rated from a small-cap connectivity niche player to an AI infrastructure essential.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Active Electrical Cables (AECs, ~60%): High-speed copper cables with embedded Credo DSPs that connect GPUs within AI racks. Replacing passive cables as data rates exceed 200Gbps per lane. The primary growth driver — one hyperscaler deployment can consume millions of AECs.
Optical DSPs (~25%): Digital signal processors for optical transceivers used in data center interconnects. Compatible with 800G and 1.6T transceivers from LITE, COHR, and CIEN. Growing with the same optical networking buildout that drives our photonics coverage.
SerDes IP Licensing (~15%): High-speed serializer/deserializer intellectual property licensed to other chip companies. Recurring, high-margin revenue stream. Validates Credo’s core technology leadership.

Credo’s competitive advantage is the combination of signal integrity expertise and fabless economics. As AI clusters scale to tens of thousands of GPUs, the cables connecting them become a critical bottleneck — signal degradation, power consumption, and latency all matter. Credo’s AECs embed DSP processing directly into the cable, solving signal integrity problems that passive copper cables cannot address at 200G+ speeds. This is not a commodity product; it’s a precision-engineered solution with IP barriers.

The customer concentration risk is real — one hyperscaler (widely believed to be Microsoft) drives the majority of AEC revenue. But this is also the bull case: if Microsoft’s deployment validates AECs as the standard for AI cluster connectivity, Amazon, Google, and Meta will follow. The TAM expands from one customer to four. The Q3 revenue of $410M from a standing start two years ago demonstrates the velocity of adoption once a hyperscaler commits.
