Investment Thesis
The HBM Supercycle Is Here — And Micron Just Proved It. Micron's Q2 FY2026 results (March 18, 2026) obliterated expectations: $23.9B revenue (+196% YoY, +75% QoQ), EPS of $12.20 (beat consensus of $8.60 by 42%), and 74.4% gross margins — the highest in the company's history.[1] This is not a cyclical recovery. This is a structural transformation driven by AI demand for High Bandwidth Memory.
Sold Out Through 2026 With Locked Pricing. Micron has completed price and volume agreements for its entire calendar 2026 HBM supply, including HBM4.[2] This is unprecedented in memory history — a structurally commoditized industry now has multi-year, fixed-price contracts. The HBM TAM is projected to grow from $35B in 2025 to $100B by 2028, a ~40% CAGR.[3]
HBM4 Already Shipping for NVIDIA Vera Rubin. At GTC 2026, Micron announced volume shipment of HBM4 36GB 12-Hi, designed for NVIDIA's next-generation Vera Rubin platform.[4] HBM4E is in development for calendar 2027 ramp. Micron is first-to-market with HBM4, ahead of SK Hynix and Samsung.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
DRAM (~75% of revenue): The core business, now dominated by HBM and data center demand. Data center DRAM revenue tripled YoY in Q2 FY25 and has continued accelerating.[1] HBM is the fastest-growing subcategory, with Micron crossing $1B in HBM revenue in Q2 FY25 and scaling dramatically since.
NAND (~25% of revenue): SSDs for data centers and consumer. NAND pricing has recovered from the 2023 trough but remains less exciting than DRAM. Still, NAND revenue hit records in Q2 FY26 as AI storage demand grows.
HBM (within DRAM, breakout growth): The thesis driver. HBM is required for every AI GPU — NVIDIA's H100/H200/B200 and upcoming Vera Rubin all require HBM stacks. Micron's HBM4 is shipping, HBM4E is in development for 2027.[4] The entire 2026 supply is sold out under fixed-price contracts.[2]

9  |  WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from BUY if:

DRAM ASPs decline for two consecutive quarters. Would signal the memory cycle is peaking and HBM pricing power is eroding.
Samsung qualifies HBM4 with NVIDIA and begins undercutting on price. Would break the supply scarcity dynamic that supports Micron's margins.
Hyperscaler capex guidance cuts exceed 15% in aggregate. Would indicate AI infrastructure spending is decelerating, reducing HBM demand.
Gross margins fall below 55% for two consecutive quarters. Would suggest cost inflation or pricing pressure is structural, not cyclical.
10  |  PRICE TARGET & CONCLUSION
Price Target: $700  |  Rating: STRONG BUY

Our $700 target represents 107% upside from the March 31 close of $337.84, derived from 10x FY2027E EPS of $70 in our base case scenario.

Micron is experiencing the most powerful fundamental transformation in its history. Revenue tripled in 12 months. Gross margins hit 74% — in a memory company. HBM is sold out through 2026 with locked pricing. HBM4 is already shipping for NVIDIA's next-gen platform. Q3 guidance implies single-quarter revenue exceeding every full-year revenue in the company's history through FY2024. And the stock trades at 6x forward earnings.

This is not a trade — it is a structural repricing of a company that has transitioned from commodity memory manufacturer to AI infrastructure monopoly supplier. The HBM TAM growing from $35B to $100B by 2028 provides multi-year revenue visibility that memory companies have never had. The 30% dividend increase, $6.9B quarterly FCF, and $6.1B in CHIPS Act funding provide a capital return floor even if the cycle softens.

At $338, you are buying the most explosive earnings growth story in semiconductors at a single-digit P/E. Our conviction score of 84/100 places MU in the top quintile of our quantitative model.
