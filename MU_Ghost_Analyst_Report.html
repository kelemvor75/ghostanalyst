1Investment Thesis
  • The defining beneficiary of the AI-memory supercycle — and the numbers are extraordinary. Micron is one of three global memory makers (with Samsung and SK Hynix) and the primary public proxy for high-bandwidth memory (HBM), the specialized DRAM that sits beside every AI accelerator. The AI buildout has turned a historically brutal commodity business into a pricing-power story: Q2 FY26 delivered $24B revenue at a 74.4% gross margin and $13.8B net income, and Micron has crossed a $1 trillion market cap. FY26 consensus calls for ~$112B revenue and ~$62 EPS — from a company that earned $0.70 just two years ago.
  • Sold-out HBM, structural tightness, and NVIDIA's HBM4 certification. Micron's entire 2026 HBM supply is sold out under binding contracts, and management expects DRAM and NAND to remain tight beyond calendar 2026. NVIDIA has certified Micron for HBM4 on its Vera Rubin platform (and Micron ships HBM3E for Blackwell today). The supply discipline is reinforced by physics and capital: Micron is committing $100B+ to new US megafabs (New York with Bechtel, Virginia 1α DRAM) but says they won't add meaningful capacity until FY2028 — keeping the cycle tight through the AI ramp.
  • Still cheap on forward earnings — the bull case is valuation, not just growth. The remarkable feature is that after a +1,060% run, MU trades at only ~18x FY26E and ~10x FY27E earnings (consensus EPS ~$62 FY26, ~$116 FY27). The market still applies a "memory-cycle discount," pricing in that the boom eventually busts. If Micron establishes HBM as a structural, recurring franchise rather than a classic cycle, that discount compresses and the multiple re-rates. We maintain BUY — with the June 24 print as the key test of exactly that question.

    4Price Action & The Supercycle in the Income Statement

    One of the great runs: MU is up ~1,060% over the trailing year (from ~$98 to ~$1,134), powered by the HBM/AI-memory supercycle, with the Q3 earnings date (June 24) marked. The stock sits near its all-time high (~$1,149) on ~94% volatility — extraordinary momentum, and extended into a binary print.

    The supercycle in the financials: quarterly revenue has roughly tripled (to $24B) while gross margin has expanded from ~37% to ~74% in a year as HBM became a larger share of the mix — and management guides Q3 gross margin to ~81% (dotted). That margin line is the single cleanest gauge of HBM pricing power, and it is the number to watch on June 24.

    5The June 24 Earnings Setup — "Supercycle Real or Cresting?"

    Few prints this year carry more weight for the AI trade. Micron reports fiscal Q3 2026 after the close on June 24. After a year built almost entirely on HBM, the report is the clearest test of whether the boom is a durable, structural franchise or another memory cycle that has run ahead of itself. Micron has beaten estimates 12 straight quarters, and options markets price a ~17.6% move on the result.

    The numbers that matter (and the wide disagreement): Company guidance points to roughly $33.5B revenue (±$750M), non-GAAP EPS ~$19.15, and gross margin ~81%. But the Street is higher and unusually split — revenue estimates span $33.7B to $40.9B, a >$7B gap reflecting deep uncertainty over the ramp pace (TipRanks cites a consensus near $20–$20.25 EPS on ~$35B revenue, ~960% EPS growth YoY). Key point: the revenue headline moves the stock first, but the gross margin and the FY4Q guidance decide the thesis. Because margins compress before revenue when memory supply loosens, whether the ~81% margin holds is a better signal of the cycle's health than the revenue line — and management has said it expects "higher price, lower cost, and favorable mix" to all contribute. The Q4 guide will likely move the stock more than the Q3 actuals.

    What we're watching. (1) Gross margin — does it hit/exceed ~81% (pricing power intact) or disappoint (early sign of loosening)? (2) FY4Q guidance — a strong guide pointing to still-building AI demand and rising HBM revenue is the bull trigger. (3) HBM commentary — output ramp, HBM4 progress for Vera Rubin, and whether the "sold out beyond 2026" visibility extends. (4) Pricing — sequential DRAM/NAND pricing and signs supply stays tight. A beat-and-raise extends the run; any margin or guidance wobble, against a stock up 1,060% with consensus below spot, could trigger an outsized pullback.

    6Business Model & Competitive Position

    Micron designs and manufactures memory and storage — primarily DRAM (~77% of revenue, including the crown-jewel HBM) and NAND flash (~23%) — across compute/data-center, mobile, automotive, and embedded markets. It is one of only three companies globally with the scale and technology to compete at the leading edge.

    ElementDetail
    Core productDRAM and NAND memory; the high-value growth driver is HBM (high-bandwidth memory) for AI accelerators
    The structural edgeOne of three global memory makers; the only US-based HBM supplier, with CHIPS Act backing and a $100B+ US fab buildout
    Why this cycle is differentHBM is sold out under multi-year contracts; AI demand + capital discipline keep supply tight beyond 2026 — unlike past boom/bust cycles
    CustomersNVIDIA (HBM3E/HBM4), AMD, hyperscalers — the AI-accelerator ecosystem; certified for NVIDIA Vera Rubin

    Competitive position — a disciplined oligopoly in the tightest part of the AI supply chain. Memory has historically been brutally cyclical — Micron lost $5.8B in FY23 at the trough — but the structure has improved: a rational three-player oligopoly (Micron, SK Hynix, Samsung), enormous capital barriers, and HBM's contracted, sold-out demand. Micron's position is strong: certified for NVIDIA's next-gen platform, the only US HBM supplier, and gaining DRAM/NAND share into AI. The competitive risk is real — SK Hynix is the HBM leader and launching HBM4, Samsung is investing heavily, and memory always eventually adds supply — but for the current AI ramp, Micron sits in the tightest, highest-value corner of the chain. The bull/bear axis is entirely about whether HBM is a structural franchise or a cyclical peak.

    7Financial Model & Valuation

    MU is the rare megacap that has tripled yet still looks cheap on forward earnings: ~18x FY26E and just ~10x FY27E (consensus EPS ~$62 FY26, ~$116 FY27). The low multiple reflects the market's persistent "memory-cycle discount" — the assumption that peak earnings are not durable. The investment debate reduces to that multiple: if HBM proves structural, the discount compresses; if the cycle crests, the "E" falls and the optically-low P/E was a trap.

    8Management & Capital Structure

    Steady, long-tenured leadership executing a once-in-a-generation capital cycle. CEO Sanjay Mehrotra (a co-founder of SanDisk who has led Micron since 2017) has navigated the company from the brutal FY23 trough (a $5.8B loss) to a $1T+ market cap, with a disciplined focus on HBM leadership and U.S. manufacturing. The balance sheet is solid and improving as the cycle floods cash in: $14.6B cash and short-term investments against $10.8B debt and $72B of equity. The key capital story is the enormous reinvestment — $25B+ of FY26 capex into HBM and leading-edge fabs — which suppresses near-term free cash flow but builds the capacity (and supply discipline) underpinning the cycle.

    14Rating & Conclusion

    We maintain a BUY on Micron with a base-case target of $1,092 (the analyst consensus, roughly at the current price), updating our March initiation (STRONG BUY, $700) that the stock has since far exceeded. The thesis is among the strongest in our coverage: Micron is the primary public proxy for the AI-memory supercycle, with HBM sold out through 2026, structural DRAM/NAND tightness, NVIDIA HBM4 certification, explosive growth (Q2: $24B revenue, 74% gross margin), and a $1T+ market cap — and, remarkably, it still trades at only ~18x FY26E and ~10x FY27E earnings because the market keeps applying a memory-cycle discount. If HBM proves a structural franchise rather than a classic cycle, that discount compresses and the stock re-rates higher; the contracted, sold-out, capital-disciplined dynamics increasingly favor durability.

    The reason this is a BUY rather than a back-up-the-truck STRONG BUY at this exact moment is timing and concentration risk, which we flag plainly. The stock has run +1,060% in a year and +168% in three months into a June 24 earnings print with sky-high expectations (~81% gross-margin guide), options pricing a ~17.6% move, and consensus now slightly below spot. A beat-and-raise extends the run; any margin or guidance wobble could trigger an outsized pullback in an extended, high-beta name. For a concentrated holder, the message is not to abandon a strong thesis but to size the position consciously into the binary and be mentally prepared for a ~17% move either way. The franchise warrants ownership; the entry timing around the print warrants discipline. BUY.

The dominant near-term event is Q3 earnings June 24: watch the ~81% gross-margin print and the FY4Q guide above all. Strong thesis, cheap forward multiple, historic momentum — tempered by a sky-high-expectations binary into a stock up 1,060%. Own the franchise; size the earnings risk.
