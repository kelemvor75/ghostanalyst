1Investment Thesis
  • The HBM super-cycle has turned Micron into a different company — and the multiple hasn't caught up. Micron is the U.S. leader in high-bandwidth memory (HBM), the stacked DRAM that sits beside every AI GPU. Revenue has exploded from $8.0B to $23.9B per quarter in five quarters, gross margins have expanded from 37% to 74%, and quarterly EPS has gone from $1.41 to $12.07. Despite the stock rising ~720% in a year, the forward P/E is just ~10x because earnings are compounding faster than the price. HBM4 for 2026 is sold out under binding contracts, with customers signing three-to-five-year agreements — a structural shift away from memory's historic boom-bust cyclicality.
  • Fiscal Q3 (reporting June 24) is guided to be a blowout — the question is whether it's already priced. Consensus models ~$33.5B revenue, ~81% gross margin, and $19.15 non-GAAP EPS for the quarter reporting in two days. That would annualize to a run-rate that makes the current ~$1,211 price look reasonable on forward earnings. But the stock sits at an all-time high, 202% above its 200-day moving average, with the options market pricing a ±7.9% move (±$96) on the print and IV at the 100th percentile.
  • Pristine fundamentals, but priced for a strong print at an all-time high. Altman Z-Score of 29.2 (deep Safe zone) and Piotroski F-Score of 7/9 (Strong) confirm a fundamentally exceptional balance sheet — $14.6B cash, manageable $10.8B debt. The risk is not the business; it is the entry point. With 99 insider sales and one purchase last quarter, and max pain at $1,000, the setup demands respect for a 'sell-the-news' reaction even if the quarter is excellent.

    4Price Action & The Super-Cycle

    Micron has risen approximately 720% over the past year, from a 52-week low of $103 to an all-time high of $1,211, one of the most dramatic moves in the entire market. Unlike a typical momentum stock, the rally has been underpinned by exploding fundamentals: each of the last four quarters delivered sequential acceleration in revenue, margins, and EPS as the HBM super-cycle took hold. The CDR (Canadian Depositary Receipts) already completed a 5-for-1 split in March 2026, fueling speculation that the main MU shares could follow.

    The revenue chart tells the story: a step-function from $8B/quarter to a guided $33.5B for fiscal Q3 — more than 4x in roughly six quarters. This is the AI memory build-out translating directly into Micron's income statement. The question for the June 24 print is not whether the quarter is strong (it is guided to be exceptional), but whether the market has already priced the strength at an all-time high with the stock 202% above its 200-day moving average.

    5The HBM Super-Cycle & Why It May Be Structural

    High-bandwidth memory is the enabling component for AI accelerators. Every NVIDIA GB200, every AMD MI-series GPU, requires stacks of HBM to feed the compute. Micron is one of only three HBM suppliers globally (with SK Hynix and Samsung), and the only U.S.-based one. The critical shift in 2026 is contractual: Micron confirmed its entire HBM4 supply for the year is sold out under binding contracts, and customers are now signing three-to-five-year supply agreements.

    This is the structural bull case. Memory has always been cyclical — boom-and-bust on commodity DRAM pricing. But multi-year binding HBM contracts change the earnings profile: they convert a portion of Micron’s revenue from spot-priced commodity to contracted, visible, premium-margin product. If this holds, Micron deserves a higher multiple than its historic cyclical range — which is precisely the bull argument for why a 10x forward P/E is too low.

    The bear counter is that no memory up-cycle lasts forever, and the market has been burned before by extrapolating peak DRAM margins. The 74% gross margin is extraordinary by historic Micron standards (which troughed near 0% in 2023) — and what is extraordinary can mean-revert. The truth is likely in between: HBM is structurally better than legacy DRAM, but Micron is still a memory company, and memory companies are cyclical. The multi-year contracts extend visibility; they do not repeal the cycle entirely.

    6Business Model & Competitive Position

    Micron competes in three memory markets: DRAM (including HBM), NAND flash, and emerging CXL memory. HBM is the crown jewel — highest margin, most supply-constrained, directly levered to AI. Micron's HBM3E is qualified into NVIDIA's platforms, and HBM4 (the next generation) is where the multi-year contracts are being signed. The competitive set is a rational oligopoly: three players, all capacity-constrained, none incentivized to flood the market.

    This oligopoly structure is the underappreciated strength. In commodity DRAM down-cycles, the three players historically over-produced and crushed pricing. In the HBM era, capacity is sold out years ahead and capital discipline is high. The risk is that the AI capex cycle slows, hyperscalers digest existing GPU/memory inventory, and the contracted demand softens at renewal — but that is a 2027-2028 risk, not a 2026 one.

    8Management & Capital Structure

    CEO Sanjay Mehrotra has led Micron through the trough and into the super-cycle, with a sharp focus on HBM leadership and capital discipline. The balance sheet is in excellent shape — $14.6B cash against $10.8B debt, net cash positive, funding a massive capex expansion (including U.S. fabs supported by CHIPS Act incentives) from a position of strength.

    The one caution flag: insider selling is heavy. In the most recent quarter, insiders recorded 99 disposed transactions against a single acquisition. At an all-time high after a 720% run, executives monetizing equity is not surprising and is often routine 10b5-1 scheduling — but the sheer ratio (99:1) is worth noting as a signal that those closest to the company see the current price as a reasonable place to take profits.

    11Analyst Sentiment

    Micron enjoys broad Street support. Consensus target of $1,186 sits essentially at the current price, with a high of $1,625 and a low of $400 — an unusually wide range reflecting the cyclical debate. In the past week (June 15-18), Wedbush, Rosenblatt, Stifel, Citigroup, Deutsche Bank, and TD Cowen all reiterated Buy/Outperform ratings ahead of earnings. The bulls point to the 10x forward multiple and HBM sold-out status; the lone $400 bear reflects the view that peak memory margins must mean-revert.

    Unlike the names where even the most bullish analyst sat below the price, MU's high target of $1,625 implies 34% further upside — the Street sees room to run if the super-cycle persists. But the $400 low and the wide dispersion are a reminder that memory valuation remains genuinely contested. The consensus near the current price suggests the obvious strength is mostly reflected; the upside case requires the cycle to extend further than skeptics believe.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    We would move to STRONG BUY if: (1) the June 24 print beats the $19.15 EPS guide AND management raises forward guidance, confirming the run-rate; (2) the stock sells off post-earnings toward $1,000-1,050 (the max-pain zone) on a 'sell-the-news' reaction despite strong numbers, creating a high-quality entry; or (3) HBM4 contract terms extend visibility into 2028.

    We would move to HOLD or trim if: gross margins guide down sequentially (signaling the cycle is peaking), forward EPS estimates get cut post-earnings, or the stock spikes another 20%+ pre-earnings (pure momentum detached from the already-strong fundamentals). At a $1.37T valuation, the easy money has been made; from here it is about whether the super-cycle's duration justifies the all-time-high entry.

    14Rating & Conclusion

    Micron is the best fundamental story in our coverage — a genuine HBM super-cycle with revenue up 197%, margins at 74%, EPS compounding 9x, and a forward multiple of just 10x. The balance sheet is pristine (Altman Z 29, Piotroski 7/9), HBM is sold out under multi-year contracts, and the Street sees room to run. This is not a momentum mirage; it is a real earnings explosion that the multiple has not fully caught up to.

    But the entry point demands discipline. The stock is at an all-time high, 202% above its 200-day moving average, with IV at the 100th percentile, max pain 17% below spot, and 99 insider sales last quarter. The fundamentals say BUY; the all-time-high entry two days before a ±8% binary says do not chase.
