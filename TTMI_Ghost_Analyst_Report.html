1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

TTMI has rallied from $20 to $97 over the past 12 months — a 385% move as the market discovered PCBs as a critical AI infrastructure component. Revenue has grown every quarter from $570M to $770M with margins expanding from 19.8% to 21.4%. The stock pulled back from $115 highs amid the broader tech selloff, creating a potential re-entry point.

The dual demand driver — AI networking and defense electronics — provides diversification that pure semiconductor plays lack. Defense budgets are growing regardless of the AI capex cycle, and TTM’s mil-spec PCBs for radar, electronic warfare, and satellite systems provide a stable floor under revenue growth.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Aerospace & Defense (~40%): Multi-layer PCBs for radar, electronic warfare, satellite communications, and missile systems. Long contract cycles (3-7 years) with government-funded demand. Growing with defense budget expansion.
Data Center Computing (~35%, fastest growing): Advanced technology PCBs for AI networking switches (Arista, Cisco), GPU server racks, and optical transceiver modules. Growing 40%+ as AI clusters scale.
Medical/Industrial/Auto (~25%): PCBs for medical devices, industrial equipment, automotive electronics. Stable, diversified revenue base.

TTM’s competitive position rests on manufacturing complexity. Advanced AI networking PCBs require 20-40+ layers, high-frequency laminate materials, tight impedance control, and fine-pitch traces that commodity PCB manufacturers in China cannot reliably produce. Defense PCBs additionally require ITAR compliance, security clearances, and U.S.-based manufacturing — further narrowing the competitive field. The combination of technical complexity and regulatory barriers creates a durable moat.

The PCB content per AI networking switch has been growing as data rates increase from 400G to 800G to 1.6T. Higher data rates require more layers, tighter tolerances, and better signal integrity — all of which increase TTM’s revenue per unit. This content growth multiplied by volume growth creates a powerful compounding effect that isn’t fully reflected in consensus estimates.
