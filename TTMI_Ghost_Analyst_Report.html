1Investment Thesis

In fourteen months, TTM Technologies went from a $40 printed-circuit-board stock the market had ignored for a decade, to a $221 AI-and-defense favorite, and then gave back nearly half of it. At $118.92 the debate on the tape is binary and, we think, wrong on both sides: bulls call the 47% drawdown a gift, bears call it a popped bubble. What actually changed is subtler, and it reframes the entire question.

Here is the variant view. The re-rating the bulls keep arguing for — from a single-digit "board shop" multiple to a growth multiple — has already happened. TTMI trades at roughly 24.5x FY26 and 17.2x FY27 non-GAAP EPS; that is a growth multiple, not the 8–12x the group carried for three decades. The easy money was the multiple, and the multiple has been paid. From here the stock no longer needs to be discovered; it needs to grow into the valuation it already carries. That single distinction converts TTMI from a valuation story into an execution-and-durability story, and it hangs on two things the market genuinely cannot yet know: whether a brand-new asymmetrical-PCB ("N+M") ramp and a brand-new Syracuse facility land on a compressed timeline the entire full-year guide depends on, and whether AI data-center demand — now 40% of revenue, heading to 49% — is durable or peaking.

The order book says the growth is real: book-to-bill of 1.49x and a 90-day backlog up 81% year-over-year. The chart says the AI trade is over: the stock is 47% off its June peak, 16% below its 50-day average, and sitting on its 200-day. Those two signals cannot both be right, and the reconciliation arrives in roughly sixty days at the Q3 print. Ahead of that binary, with momentum broken and the balance sheet levering into a $1.1 billion cash acquisition, we see a genuinely excellent business but no disciplined edge to chasing it here — 86% below where the sell-side's price targets still sit.

The five pillars of the thesis:

  • The re-rating already happened. At ~24.5x FY26 / 17.2x FY27 non-GAAP EPS, TTMI no longer trades at a board-shop multiple. The move from single digits to a growth multiple is behind it; the remaining return has to be earned on execution, not discovery.
  • The whole guide rests on one ramp. Management's ~$4.4B revenue / ~$5 EPS target requires ~$600M of new N+M revenue in H2 and a brand-new Syracuse Ultra-HDI plant hitting yield on schedule. Book-to-bill of 1.49x proves demand exists; it does not prove TTM can convert it on time and at margin.
  • AI concentration is the feature and the risk. Data-center & networking is 40% of revenue heading to 49%, up 91% YoY. It is the engine — and the exact exposure that de-rated the whole electronics group and halved this stock over the summer. Bull case and bear case share one variable.
  • Insiders sold the top; the CEO bought the dip. Executives distributed heavily into the June parabola at around $209–210. CEO Edwin Roks then bought $1.1M at ~$112 in late August. A real vote of confidence — but it followed a far larger wave of selling.
  • The Street is ~86% above the tape. Sell-side sits at 12 Buy / 2 Hold with a ~$221 mean target while the stock trades at $119. Someone is mispriced. We think the truth is in between, and that it gets adjudicated at Q3.

    • We initiate coverage of TTM Technologies at HOLD with a 12-month target of $128 — modest upside that reflects a business we would happily own, bought at a moment and a price that offer no momentum advantage and little margin of safety.

    3Anatomy of a 47% Drawdown

    The pre-thesis screen flags TTMI as down 47% from a June 22 peak of $221 — a mandatory "why" before any thesis. The answer matters, because a structural break argues for caution while a valuation reset can argue for opportunity. Here, the evidence points overwhelmingly to a valuation-and-sentiment reset in a high-beta name, not fundamental deterioration.

    At the June high, TTMI traded near 60x forward earnings — a multiple that left no room for error. What followed was not a company stumble but a group de-rating: through July, electronics-manufacturing peers Flex, Jabil, Sanmina and Benchmark repeatedly sold off in lockstep on the same sessions, a synchronized downdraft that says "sector repositioning," not "TTM problem." Layered on top were macro shocks that hit the whole AI-hardware complex — geopolitical fear and an oil spike, worries over new AI export curbs — plus two idiosyncratic accelerants: a Vanguard filing that appeared to trigger algorithm-driven selling, and a wave of insider distribution.

    That insider picture deserves an honest, two-sided telling. Into the June parabola, the executive team sold heavily: the CFO, CHRO, COO and multiple segment presidents disposed of shares at around $209–210 in late June — within days of the $221 top — with one IT executive selling again in the low $140s in August. Insiders monetized the top. Then, on August 25, CEO Edwin Roks bought 10,000 shares for $1.1 million at about $112, and the stock jumped 7.4% on the disclosure. The net signal is genuinely mixed with a recent bullish tilt — leadership took profits on the spike, and the CEO expressed conviction on the pullback.

    The tell that this was a reset, not a rupture: the fundamentals accelerated straight into the drawdown. TTM beat Q2 across every line and raised full-year guidance while the stock was busy halving. A reset from 60x, however, does not automatically make ~24x cheap — it makes it reasonable, and only if the estimates hold.

    So the drawdown clears the pre-thesis gate as a valuation reset, not a broken business — which is why this is a HOLD and not a SELL. But "reset" is not "bargain": the multiple fell precisely because the market began pricing the two risks the 60x tape ignored — that AI-capex demand could digest, and that the H2 ramp could slip. Both are unresolved, which is why we do not chase the bounce.

    4The Billion-Dollar Quarter

    On August 5, TTM reported the first billion-dollar quarter in its history and raised the bar for the rest of the year. Net sales of $1,004.1M rose 37% year-over-year and beat the ~$962M consensus; non-GAAP EPS of $0.99 (up 71% from $0.58) beat the $0.89 FactSet consensus and management's own $0.82–0.88 guide; adjusted EBITDA reached $166.8M, a 16.6% margin. GAAP EPS was $0.77. Gross margin expanded to ~21% and non-GAAP operating margin to 13.8% — this is not a commodity assembler's P&L.

    The guide is where the quarter turned aggressive. For Q3, TTM projects $1.10–1.14B in revenue and $1.21–1.27 in non-GAAP EPS — roughly 8% and 13% above where the Street sat — and it raised full-year 2026 to ~$4.4B revenue and EPS approaching $5, both above consensus and both explicitly excluding pending acquisitions. The revenue chart makes the inflection plain: a steady $649M-to-$774M grind through 2025 gives way to $846M, then $1,004M, with Q3 guided to ~$1.12B.

    One blemish belongs in the story, not the footnotes. Despite net income doubling, operating cash flow was roughly flat year-over-year (~$96M) and free cash flow was just $46M, as a spike in receivables from 37% growth trapped working capital. Hyper-growth is consuming cash — and management is about to layer a $1.1 billion cash acquisition on top. The income statement is inflecting up; the cash-flow statement is the pressure gauge to watch.

    The tension is structural and under-appreciated. The largest and fastest-growing slice — data center & networking, 40% of revenue heading to 49% next quarter and up 91% — is also the least visible and most cyclical: AI-infrastructure demand concentrated among a handful of hyperscale customers. The most visible slice — aerospace & defense, riding a $1.7 billion program backlog and a $7 billion qualified pipeline — is growing slowest at 14%. The market is applying one blended multiple to a book whose quality varies enormously by segment. That is precisely why a sum-of-the-parts instinct pulls the fair value below where a single growth multiple would place it.

    Geography reinforces the reshoring identity: roughly $1.6B of the base is U.S. versus about $0.3B in Taiwan — a domestically-weighted manufacturer at the exact moment Washington is paying to re-shore microelectronics. It is part of why the defense backbone deserves a premium even as the AI slice deserves a concentration discount.

    6The AI Data-Center Engine

    Underneath the 91% number sits a specific product story. TTM's advanced N+M asymmetrical PCBs and Ultra-High-Density-Interconnect (Ultra-HDI) boards are the differentiated, high-value substrates that AI accelerators and networking gear increasingly require. Management says N+M yields are running ahead of plan, and it expects roughly $600M of N+M revenue in the second half of 2026 — about one-third in Q3 and two-thirds in Q4. That single line is the bridge from ~$1.85B in first-half revenue to a ~$4.4B full year: the H2 ramp is not a rounding error, it is the guide.

    The capacity behind it is new. In June, TTM opened a 215,000-square-foot Ultra-HDI facility in Syracuse, New York — among the first in the U.S. purpose-built for this class of board — backed by a $30 million U.S. Department of War investment aimed at re-shoring microelectronics from Asia. Syracuse begins volume ramp in Q3 and is targeted to reach full run-rate by 2028. The 90-day backlog jumping 81% to $901M is the demand-side confirmation that orders are arriving faster than TTM can currently ship them.

    The unpriced question in one sentence: a 1.49x book-to-bill tells you the demand exists; it does not tell you a brand-new process and a brand-new plant can convert that demand on a compressed schedule and at margin — and new-product, new-facility ramps are historically where timelines slip. You find out in ~60 days.

    The Competitive landscape holds a nuance the simple "board shop vs. defense prime" framing misses: the entire electronics-manufacturing complex has re-rated on AI. TTM's ~24.5x forward multiple is not a wild premium — it sits mid-pack among peers who have all been swept up in the same thesis.

    Two genuine differentiators justify TTM at the top of that range. First, growth: ~51% FY26 revenue expansion dwarfs peers stuck in the mid-single digits. Second, margin: TTM's ~21% gross margin is roughly double the thin ~8–10% of the EMS assemblers, because PCBs and RF components are structurally higher-value than box-build assembly. Add a defense backlog and reshoring-funded domestic capacity that peers lack, and TTM has a real, defensible moat in advanced HDI and mission-critical RF.

    But the same table carries the warning. Because the whole group re-rated on one narrative, the whole group de-rates together when that narrative wobbles — which is exactly what the synchronized summer selloff demonstrated. And the moat has a soft edge: lucrative hyperscaler PCB demand invites Asian competitors to add advanced HDI capacity, and if they do, or if customers deliberately diversify suppliers, TTM's premium margins compress. The moat is real in defense; it is contested in data center.

    8Financial Model, Valuation & the Reverse DCF

    Start with what we will not use. A conventional discounted-cash-flow model is uninformative for TTMI today, and we discard it deliberately. Our enrichment returns a DCF fair value of roughly negative $3 — not because the business is worthless, but because trailing free cash flow is temporarily depressed: the working-capital drain from 37% growth plus the Syracuse and data-center capex ramp have pushed FCF yield slightly negative. Discounting a temporarily-negative cash stream produces a number that says nothing about intrinsic value. Leading with a precise DCF figure and then explaining it away would be performing rigor rather than exercising it, so we set it aside and value the business on forward earnings, a reverse-DCF, and scenarios.

    The reverse-DCF is more honest about what the tape is paying for. To support today's ~$13B enterprise value on a normalized ~7–8% free-cash-flow margin (i.e., after the capex peak passes), TTM must compound revenue at roughly 15%+ through the end of the decade — which is almost exactly management's stated 15–20% organic target for 2027–28. In plain terms: the market is already paying for management's plan to be delivered. There is little embedded margin of safety if the AI-capex cycle cools or the ramp slips. That, more than any single multiple, is why we land at HOLD.

    On multiples, TTMI trades at ~24.5x FY26 and ~17.2x FY27 non-GAAP EPS, and 26.2x TTM EV/EBITDA — reasonable for ~40% near-term EPS growth, but full for a business with genuine cyclical DNA (2023 was a GAAP loss year) and heavy AI concentration. Our sensitivity grid frames the outcomes:

    10Analyst Sentiment & the $221 Disconnect

    The sell-side is emphatically constructive and, we think, partly stale. The consensus stands at 12 Buy, 2 Hold, 0 Sell, and the FactSet mean price target is roughly $221 — Truist raised to $224 after the quarter, B. Riley sits at $208, Needham reiterates Buy. Against a $118.92 price, that consensus implies about 86% upside, one of the widest analyst-vs-tape gaps we have seen.

    A gap that large usually means the targets have not caught down to a changed reality. Many were set near the June peak and assume the full FY26–27 estimates land without slippage — the same numbers that embed a +66% year-over-year second half. Dispersion this wide (individual targets run from the low-$130s to $224) is itself a signal: the professionals cannot agree on what TTM is worth, which is the opposite of a settled, own-it-here setup. We sit closer to the tape than to the targets — unwilling to pay in advance for a ramp we have not yet seen convert.

    11Management & Capital Structure

    CEO Edwin Roks leads a management team that has executed the cyclical recovery well — margins up, backlog at records, the first billion-dollar quarter delivered. His $1.1 million open-market purchase at ~$112 on August 25 is the most tangible insider signal in the story and a genuine vote of confidence, even acknowledging it followed a larger wave of executive selling into the June spike.

    The capital structure is where conviction and risk meet. Management is pursuing three acquisitions at once. The headline is Epiq Solutions — a $1.1 billion all-cash deal (announced August 17) for AI-enabled software-defined radios and RF/DSP capabilities from Veritas Capital, backed by committed financing from JPMorgan, Bank of America and Barclays, immediately accretive to adjusted EBITDA margin and to non-GAAP EPS by 2028. Alongside it, Swiss Technology Group and ILFA (both all-cash, closing in Q3) add European medical and A&D capability. Critically, all three are cash-funded — there is no equity dilution — and TTM pre-positioned in June with a new $1 billion revolver and an upsized $400 million Term Loan B at SOFR+1.75%.

    The capital-allocation bet in one line: TTM is levering up to buy defense/RF capability and build domestic capacity at the exact growth inflection. Net leverage of ~0.9x today rises materially once the $1.1B Epiq cash goes out the door (against ~$508M of cash on hand), just as a working-capital drain is already pressuring free cash flow. High conviction — and a real stacking of execution and balance-sheet risk in a single window.

    This is not a distress story — Altman Z of 4.63 sits firmly in the safe zone — but it is a deliberate shift to a leveraged growth posture: a smart accelerant if the cycle stays strong and the integrations land, a constraint if the cycle softens while three deals are being absorbed at once.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    Our HOLD is a conditional judgment, not a permanent one. Two clear, pre-committed paths would change it.

    What would make us more constructive — TTMI would move to BUY. A clean Q3 that demonstrates the N+M and Syracuse ramp is converting on schedule — revenue at or above the ~$1.12B guide midpoint with margins holding — paired with the stock reclaiming its 50-day moving average (~$144), would tell us the transformation is landing and the trend is repairing. That combination removes the two things holding us back (unproven execution and broken momentum) at once. Independently, a pullback into the high-$80s to low-$90s — near the options put wall — that restores a real margin of safety against the FY26 numbers would also move us to BUY on valuation.

    What would make us more cautious — TTMI would move to SELL. Evidence that the AI-capex cycle is digesting — a hyperscaler capex cut, an N+M order push-out, or a Q3 that misses the guide — would break the estimate base the entire ~24.5x multiple rests on. So would a visible stumble in the Syracuse ramp, or a debt-funded acquisition that strains the balance sheet just as the cycle softens. Any of these turns a fairly-valued growth name into an over-valued cyclical, and we would not wait to act.
