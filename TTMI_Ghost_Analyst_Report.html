1Investment Thesis

TTM Technologies makes the advanced printed circuit boards that AI data centers and defense radars are built on — the thirty-plus-layer interconnect underneath every 800G networking switch and phased-array antenna — and it is the only U.S.-headquartered manufacturer of that class of board at scale. We initiated coverage on April 1 at $97.42 with a BUY and a $120 target. It cleared the target within the month, ran to $223.83 by late June, then gave back 32% in three weeks. This update re-underwrites at $152.84. The business got better while the stock got ahead of it: what made $97 a bargain makes $153 a full price for a company that must now build its way into its own estimates.

  • Demand stopped being the question in Q1. Revenue of $846M grew 30%, the book-to-bill printed 1.41, and management raised the capex plan from $250M to $310M mid-quarter to secure equipment. Orders are arriving 41% faster than TTM can ship boards — from here, revenue is a production schedule, not a demand signal.
  • The estimates are the CEO's construction schedule, transcribed. Dr. Edwin Roks put 'ending this year at $4 billion' on the record in May — formalized in an 8-K — and consensus FY26 sits at exactly that: $4.0B and $4.05 of EPS. With $1.60 earned across Q1 and the Q2 guide, $2.45 must land in a second half that out-earns the first by roughly 53%.
  • The balance sheet is being spent to make the schedule real. Q1 free cash flow ran negative $85M; a $400M term loan and $1.0B revolver were signed in June; two all-cash European acquisitions follow in Q3 — under a first-year CEO running the integration playbook he learned running Teledyne.
  • The market owns it for the AI beta; management is building something steadier. Data center and networking is 36% of sales and the growth engine, but roughly 40% is aerospace and defense on multi-year backlog — a Raytheon LTAMDS award worth up to $200M landed in January — and the pending acquisitions add long-cycle European medical. Ballast, not beta.

    • The structural read: a 1.41 book-to-bill is a queue, not a forecast. Demand is solved; what remains is conversion — equipment lead times, plant ramps, two integrations — and even 32% below the high, the stock pays ~29x FY27 consensus earnings for that conversion to go cleanly. The bottleneck thesis we bought in April has been proven so thoroughly that TTM itself is now the bottleneck, and the scarcity premium has migrated from the thesis into the multiple. Hence HOLD, target $160 (~30x FY27E consensus EPS of $5.32); we would add in the high-$130s, where the price stops paying in advance for a ramp that has not happened yet.

    5The Bottleneck Got Priced

    The April thesis was that advanced PCBs were the AI build-out's hidden bottleneck. Q1 proved it so thoroughly that the constraint moved inside the company. A 1.41 book-to-bill means customers ordered 41% more than TTM could ship; when demand exceeds capacity, revenue stops being a demand signal and becomes a production schedule. Management said as much with money: the capex plan went from $250M to $310M mid-quarter, in the CFO's words to secure equipment and accelerate revenue — a sentence about lead times, not orders.

    Look at what the estimates are. The CEO told a business journal in May that TTM 'will be ending this year at $4 billion' — formalized in an 8-K — and the street's $4.0B is that sentence with a spreadsheet attached. Q1 delivered $0.75 and Q2 is guided to $0.82–0.88 — call it $1.60 for the first half — so the $4.05 consensus needs $2.45 from a second half that must out-earn the first by roughly 53% — precisely when the Penang and Syracuse ramps, the deal closes, and the integration must all go right at once. Even the next quarter is pre-spent: the street's Q2 number ($0.925) sits above the top of guidance — a quarter delivered exactly as promised would print as a miss.

    The consequence is what the multiple is buying. At 37.7x FY26E and 28.7x FY27E — against a GAAP P&L flat at $72–77M of operating income for three straight quarters, negative free cash flow, and leverage rising through a term loan and two all-cash deals — the market is paying secular-compounder prices for an execution story. And the tell is in the first-year CEO's own moves — long-cycle European medical and defense, 'up the chain,' the Teledyne playbook: management is building ballast while the market owns the stock for beta. Demand is secular; conversion is a schedule. The multiple prices the first while the P&L can only deliver the second. The bottleneck got priced.

    6Revenue Architecture & Competitive Landscape
    Quarterly revenue, last ten quarters. Source: company quarterly financial statements.

    Revenue Architecture is a barbell, and the barbell is the point. Data center and networking (36% of Q1 sales) is the engine: high-layer-count boards for AI switches and accelerators — the content story that re-rated the stock. Aerospace and defense, roughly 40% (the remainder after the disclosed markets), is the anchor: multi-year program backlog, headlined by January's LTAMDS award (up to $200M). Medical, industrial and instrumentation grew to 16% from 13%; automotive shrank to 8% from 11% as low-value volume is rationalized out. The ten-quarter path ($569M to $846M) shows a business that inflected twice — defense in 2024, AI in 2025.

    The pending acquisitions extend the anchor, not the engine. Swiss Technology Group (Zurich) builds miniaturized boards for surgical robotics, hearing aids and implantables; ILFA (Hannover, 45 years old) makes thirty-plus-layer, electro-optical and embedded-component PCBs for defense and industrial customers. Both are all-cash, terms undisclosed — smaller but meaningful, modestly accretive — closing in Q3, with details promised on the Q2 call. It is TTM's first manufacturing footprint in Europe — and it is long-cycle medical and defense, not AI.

    8Management, Capital Structure & Capital Return

    Ten months of CEO transition explain most of 2026's strategic tape. Dr. Edwin Roks — chief executive of Teledyne until April 2025, where the model was decades of acquiring niche high-reliability franchises and integrating them — took over in September 2025 from Tom Edman (CEO since 2014, off the board May 7). The June sequence — $400M term loan plus $1.0B revolver, then two all-cash European deals framed as 'up the chain' — is the Teledyne playbook executed early; a first-year CEO running a program he has run before at larger scale is a real mitigant, not a full answer, to integration risk. Adding Ryan McCarthy, the 24th Secretary of the Army, to the board says which half of the barbell the company is institutionally committed to.

    The insider file reads worse than it is and still says something. That is what building through a supercycle looks like; it is not what a cheap stock looks like.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    We would upgrade to Buy on (i) a pullback into the high-$130s (~26x FY27E) with the demand picture intact; (ii) a Q2 print that both clears the above-guide consensus and puts a Q3 guide on the board consistent with the $4B year; or (iii) disclosed deal terms that keep pro-forma leverage near 2x while confirming accretion. We would downgrade to Sell on a second-half walk-back from the $4B schedule, a book-to-bill print below 1.0, an integration or leverage surprise when terms land, or a data-center order air-pocket the defense backlog cannot bridge.

    14Rating & Conclusion
    Conclusion — HOLD, 12-month Price Target: $160. The April thesis — advanced PCBs as the AI build-out's hidden bottleneck — was right, and the stock more than doubled past our target before giving a third of it back. What changed is not the story — it is who carries the risk. At a 1.41 book-to-bill demand is solved; revenue is a construction schedule, half the year's earnings sit in an unbuilt second half, and the balance sheet is being spent — $310M of capex, a new $400M term loan, two all-cash acquisitions — to make the schedule real. At ~29x FY27 consensus earnings — even 32% below the high — the multiple pays for that schedule as if already delivered. The bottleneck got priced. Own the franchise — the only U.S.-scale advanced-PCB capacity, a defense floor, a proven integrator at the wheel — but buy the execution risk at execution prices: the high-$130s, not the middle of the unwind.
