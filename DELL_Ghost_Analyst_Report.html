1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

DELL peaked at $240 in mid-2025 before pulling back to $169 — a 30% decline driven by concerns that hyperscalers are building their own servers and the broader tech selloff. Revenue tells a different story: Q2 FY2026’s $29.8B was a massive beat driven by AI server demand from enterprise customers who don’t have the engineering resources to build custom infrastructure like Google or Meta.

The pullback has created a valuation anomaly: DELL trades at 12x forward earnings with 24% EPS growth in a market where AI infrastructure companies routinely command 25-40x. The discount reflects concerns about AI server margin pressure (GPUs are a pass-through cost) and the legacy PC business drag. But the enterprise AI adoption curve is just beginning, and Dell owns the channel.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Infrastructure Solutions Group (ISG, ~50%): PowerEdge servers (including AI servers with NVIDIA GPUs), storage solutions (PowerStore, PowerScale), and networking. The AI growth engine. Revenue surged on enterprise AI deployments.
Client Solutions Group (CSG, ~40%): PCs, laptops, workstations, and displays. Mature, cyclical. Benefiting modestly from PC refresh cycle and AI PC adoption.
Services (~10%): IT consulting, deployment, managed services, and financing. High-margin, recurring revenue that grows with the installed base.

Dell’s moat in enterprise AI is distribution, not technology. The company has 40,000+ salespeople, relationships with every Fortune 500 IT department, and financing capabilities that make it trivially easy for a CIO to add AI servers to an existing Dell relationship. When an enterprise CEO says ‘we need AI infrastructure,’ the CIO calls Dell. This channel dominance is why Dell’s AI server backlog has grown every quarter despite the narrative that hyperscalers are going custom.

The margin concern is real but manageable. AI servers have lower gross margins than traditional servers because the NVIDIA GPU is a large pass-through cost. But Dell earns full margins on storage, networking, services, and financing attached to every AI deployment. The blended margin on an AI infrastructure deal is higher than the headline server margin suggests. As the attach rate for storage and services grows, blended margins should improve.

9  |  WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from BUY if:

ISG revenue growth decelerates below 10%. Would signal enterprise AI adoption is slower than expected.
AI server gross margins fall below 12%. Would indicate Dell is winning on price, not value.
Two or more hyperscalers announce major ODM server shifts. Would shrink the addressable market for branded servers.
10  |  PRICE TARGET & CONCLUSION
Price Target: $200  |  Rating: BUY

Our $200 target represents 18% upside from $169.38, derived from 14x FY2027E EPS of $14.50 — a modest re-rating from the current 12x.

Dell is the cheapest AI infrastructure play in our coverage at 12x forward earnings. The market is pricing it like a declining PC company, but the ISG business is growing 28% on enterprise AI demand. When a Fortune 500 CIO gets a mandate to deploy AI infrastructure, Dell’s 40,000-person sales force and full-stack offering (servers + storage + networking + services + financing) make it the default choice. The 30% pullback from $240 to $169 has created an entry point where even modest multiple expansion drives meaningful returns.
