Agentic AI workloads specifically require high-performance CPUs alongside GPUs — a dynamic that benefits AMD's dual CPU+GPU positioning.

1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

AMD traded from $164 in October to $250 in mid-January on the Q4 beat, then sold off to $203 as the Q1 guide of $9.8B (a sequential decline) disappointed momentum traders. The revenue chart tells the structural story: $6.8B → $7.7B → $7.4B → $7.7B → $9.3B → $10.3B — a clear inflection starting in Q3 2025 as the data center AI franchise began to scale. The sequential dip in Q1 guidance reflects normal seasonality, not demand weakness — YoY growth of 32% remains robust.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Data Center (~53% of revenue, $5.4B Q4): The thesis driver. EPYC server CPUs gain share every quarter from Intel. Instinct GPUs (MI300X, MI430X) compete with NVIDIA for AI training and inference workloads. MI450 and the Helios rack-scale platform ramp in H2 2026 with 'tens of billions' of data center AI revenue targeted by 2027.[2] Operating margin expanded to 33% in Q4, up from 30% YoY.
Client (~25%, $3.9B Q4): Ryzen desktop and mobile CPUs. Ryzen AI 400 series with NPU integration powers the AI PC wave. Revenue grew 37% YoY in Q4 driven by enterprise adoption. PC market may be down slightly in 2026, but AMD expects to grow its PC business through share gains at the premium end.
Gaming (~8%, $843M Q4): Semi-custom SoCs for PlayStation and Xbox. Revenue grew 50% YoY in Q4, but Su warned that semi-custom revenue will 'decline by a significant double-digit percentage' in 2026 as the console cycle enters year 7.[3] This is a known headwind — the data center ramp more than offsets it.
Embedded (~14%, $973M Q4): Adaptive SoCs and FPGAs from the Xilinx acquisition. Recovery underway after a deep 2024 cyclical trough. Revenue grew 19% sequentially in Q4. Not the growth driver, but no longer a drag.

The CUDA Moat — And Why It's Not Insurmountable

The bear case on AMD centers on NVIDIA's CUDA ecosystem lock-in. This is a real competitive advantage — most AI frameworks are optimized for CUDA first. However, AMD doesn't need to win the GPU war. It needs to be the credible #2 in a market growing 60%+ annually. Several dynamics work in AMD's favor: hyperscalers (Microsoft, Meta, Google) actively want a second GPU supplier to avoid single-vendor dependency; ROCm compatibility has improved dramatically, with most major ML frameworks now supporting it; and AMD's unique dual CPU+GPU offering (EPYC + Instinct in a single rack) provides a platform integration advantage NVIDIA doesn't match.

9  |  WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from BUY if:

Data center revenue growth decelerates below 40% for two consecutive quarters. Would signal the AI GPU ramp is stalling and hyperscalers are consolidating on NVIDIA.
MI450/Helios fails to win a top-5 hyperscaler deployment. Would indicate the product isn't competitive at the scale that matters.
Gross margins compress below 50% for two quarters. Would suggest pricing pressure from NVIDIA or unfavorable product mix.
Lisa Su departs. The strategy and execution are inseparable from her leadership. AMD without Su is a materially different company.
10  |  PRICE TARGET & CONCLUSION
Price Target: $269  |  Rating: BUY

Our $269 target represents 32% upside from the March 31 close of $203.43, derived from 25x FY2027E EPS of $10.77 in our base case scenario.

AMD is the only credible challenger to NVIDIA in AI GPUs. That sentence alone justifies owning the stock in a market where AI infrastructure spending is growing 60%+ annually. The data center franchise has inflected — $5.4B in Q4 with 39% YoY growth — and the MI450/Helios ramp in H2 2026 represents the next step-function. Meanwhile, EPYC server CPU gains from Intel are structural and accelerating, providing a second growth engine that most GPU-focused investors underappreciate.

At 19x FY2027 earnings with 62% EPS growth, AMD is priced as if the data center ramp might fail. Our conviction score of 78/100 places it in the top quartile — the same group that outperformed by 31% in our out-of-sample validation. The risk is real (CUDA moat, console decline, China restrictions), but the risk/reward at $203 is compelling. Consensus sees $293. We see $269 as a conservative base case with $450 upside if MI450 wins at hyperscale.
