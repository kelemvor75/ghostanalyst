1Investment Thesis

Definium Therapeutics — the company Wall Street still half-remembers as MindMed — has quietly done the two things its closest comparable could not. It has produced psychedelic efficacy that is not merely statistically clean but clinically large, and it has raised enough cash to reach the far side of approval without a dilution treadmill. It has done both while leading with generalized anxiety disorder, the one large psychiatric market that has no branded interventional incumbent and has not seen a new mechanism approved since 2007. The stock has responded — up 238% year-to-date, a seven-fold move off its 2025 low — and yet it still trades roughly 30% below the analyst mean.

Our disagreement with the market runs opposite to the one we would make on Compass. There, the good news was priced and the commercial case was thin; here, the commercial case is stronger and, after the run, is still not fully in the price. Two positive Phase 3 readouts with effect sizes roughly double the category norm, a fortress balance sheet funded into 2030, and a near-term catalyst that looks more like a replication than a fresh binary — against that, a ~$4.7B enterprise value is demanding but not stretched. The one genuinely unresolved question is commercial, and it is a hard one: an 8-to-12 hour LSD session is the most resource-intensive delivery model in a class already defined by delivery friction. We think the balance still tilts up. We initiate coverage of Definium Therapeutics at BUY with a 12-month target of $54.

    2What DT120 Is — and How It Reaches Revenue

    DT120 ODT is a single 100 µg orally-disintegrating tablet of lysergide — the tartrate salt of LSD — formulated on Catalent's Zydis fast-dissolve technology and acting as an agonist at the serotonin 5-HT2A receptor. Longtime followers know it as MM120, the asset that made MindMed; the January 2026 rebrand to Definium changed the name, not the molecule. It is being developed for the two largest psychiatric markets — generalized anxiety disorder (GAD, the lead, breakthrough-designated indication) and major depressive disorder (MDD) — with posttraumatic stress disorder flagged as future optionality. A second, earlier asset, DT402 (an R-enantiomer of MDMA), sits in Phase 1.

    DT120 is not a daily pill, and its Revenue Architecture is unusual for a drug: Definium would not sell a high-volume prescription so much as a supervised medical procedure. A single dose is administered in a monitored clinical setting, with additional doses available in the open-label extension based on symptom severity. Revenue therefore depends on three things the company does not fully control: FDA approval and subsequent DEA rescheduling (lysergide is Schedule I today), the build-out of certified administration sites with trained monitors, and payer reimbursement for the session itself. The offset — and it is a real one — is breadth: a single molecule addressing two multi-million-patient indications spreads that fixed commercial and regulatory cost across a far larger opportunity than a single-indication peer enjoys.

    Takeaway: DT120 monetizes a monitored procedure across two large indications, not a prescription. That breadth is the bull's leverage; the monitored-session dependency — approval, site build-out, reimbursement — is where the friction lives.
    3The Clinical Package: The Strongest Efficacy in the Class

    Three readouts define the asset, and all three point the same way. Emerge (MDD, June 2026) delivered a placebo-adjusted 8.1-point MADRS improvement at Week 6 (Cohen's d=0.83) that held at 7.3 points through Week 12, with no serious adverse events and no suicidality signal. Voyage (GAD, August 2026) produced a 5.4-point placebo-adjusted HAM-A separation at Week 12 (d=0.81), with onset by Day 2. Both build on the landmark 2023–24 MM120 Phase 2b, in which a single dose drove a 7.7-point HAM-A benefit and a 65% response / 48% remission rate durable to twelve weeks — the result that earned Breakthrough Therapy designation.

    Read the numbers honestly and the bull case survives the reading. The headline is the effect size: a d of ~0.81–0.83 is a large effect by the conventions of psychiatric trials, and roughly double the placebo-adjusted deltas that underpin approved esketamine and Compass's psilocybin. The bear's fair counterpoint is that the Phase 3 response and remission rates (43% and 14% in Voyage) came in below the striking Phase 2b figures (65% and 48%) — the near-universal pattern as a program scales into larger, more rigorously controlled trials with higher placebo response. What did not fade is the separation from placebo and its statistical strength. This is not a me-too signal dressed up; it is the most differentiated efficacy the psychedelic class has produced, and it is the load-bearing fact of the thesis.

    Takeaway: Two positive pivotals and a durable Phase 2b, all with large (d≈0.81–0.83) effect sizes ~2× the category norm. Absolute response rates moderated at Phase 3 scale, as expected — but the differentiation is real, and it is what the market is under-crediting.
    4The Regulatory Path and the Unblinding Question

    Every psychedelic is measured against the ghost of Lykos, whose MDMA-for-PTSD program drew a 10–1 negative advisory-committee vote in 2024 and a complete response letter, largely over functional unblinding — the objection that when a patient plainly feels a psychoactive drug, the blind breaks and expectancy inflates the measured effect. For DFTX, that objection is weaker than it first appears, and Definium has engineered the program to answer it directly.

    The evidence starts in the Phase 2b, where the company demonstrated that MM120's clinical activity was not attributable to functional unblinding. The design carries the argument forward: Panorama, the second GAD pivotal, randomizes ~250 patients 2:1:2 to 100 µg, 50 µg, or placebo, with the low-dose arm included specifically to confound patients' ability to tell which condition they are in — a complementary, active-dose structure that aligns with the FDA's own recent guidance on psychedelic development and mirrors the approach the agency has already signaled it will accept elsewhere in the class. Two pivotals per indication (Voyage and Panorama in GAD; Emerge and the ongoing Ascend in MDD) further insulate the package from any single-study critique. Add the April 2026 executive order directing the FDA to accelerate access, and a broadly supportive posture toward the category, and the probability of an eventual GAD approval is materially higher than a naive “psychedelics-get-rejected” prior implies — we carry it around 80%. The residual gates are real: the 100 µg experience remains distinguishable from placebo, an advisory committee could still be convened, and DEA rescheduling of a Schedule I substance is a discrete step before any launch.

    Takeaway: The class's defining risk — functional unblinding — is the risk DFTX has worked hardest to pre-empt, through Phase 2b evidence, Panorama's low-dose control arm, a two-pivotal design per indication, and alignment with FDA guidance. This is genuine de-risking that the delivery debate tends to overshadow.
    5Commercial Reality: A First-Mover Market and a Heavy Session

    The commercial case has a bright side the market under-weights and a hard edge it may under-price, and both deserve to be stated plainly. The bright side is the choice of lead indication. Generalized anxiety disorder afflicts ~26 million U.S. adults, has seen no new mechanism approved since 2007, and — critically — has no branded interventional incumbent. That is a categorically cleaner runway than Compass faces in treatment-resistant depression, where J&J's Spravato is entrenched, now approved as a monotherapy, and backed by five years of prescriber relationships. DT120 would enter GAD first, not fight for share.

    The hard edge is delivery. Lysergide's active experience runs 8 to 12 hours — the longest in the class, materially heavier than psilocybin's 6-to-8 hour session and far heavier than esketamine's ~2-hour administration. Every treated patient occupies a monitored chair and trained staff for the better part of a working day. That single fact constrains site throughput, complicates payer economics, and caps how quickly — and how profitably — the launch can scale. The honest read-across is Spravato's: even into an empty market, esketamine took roughly six years to grind toward ~$2B, and it did so on a fraction of DT120's chair time. A best-in-class effect size helps convert the patients who get through the door; it does not widen the door. We assume a measured, Spravato-shaped ramp rather than a blockbuster trajectory, and we think the Street's $62–$66 targets lean toward the latter.

    Takeaway: GAD-first into a market with no branded incumbent is the real structural advantage; the 8-to-12 hour LSD session is the real structural constraint. The bull owns the first fact, the bear owns the second, and a disciplined target has to hold both at once.
    6Financials, Burn, and a Fortress Balance Sheet

    Read the income statement carefully, because the headline number misleads. Definium reported a $159.0M net loss in Q2 2026 ($1.44 per share), but the majority of the gap between that figure and the $75.1M operating loss is a non-cash warrant fair-value remeasurement: the company's $4.25-strike financing warrants are revalued each quarter, and as the stock ran toward $44 that mark-to-market ballooned. It is a symptom of the rising share price, not of the business. The figure that matters is the operating loss, and it is widening — from ~$32M in early 2025 to $75M last quarter — driven by both a Phase 3 R&D ramp and a deliberate build in G&A as the company staffs for a potential launch.

    7Valuation: Why DCF Doesn't Apply, and What rNPV Says

That figure is not a bear signal; it is meaningless. Definium generates no revenue and deeply negative free cash flow by design, so a DCF simply capitalizes the burn. For a pre-launch biotech whose entire value is optionality on a binary approval, DCF is the wrong tool, and we set it aside explicitly rather than let a false $3 anchor the debate. The correct framework is a risk-adjusted, probability-weighted valuation.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    This is a BUY, but a conditional one built on a specific thesis, with clearly defined exits in both directions. We are constructive on the science and the stock; specific developments would push us further in — or out.

    We would move to BUY at a higher, Street-level target — into the $60s — on evidence that the commercial ceiling is higher than our delivery-constrained base assumes: (1) a clean, confirmatory Panorama readout in September that completes the GAD package; (2) early real-world signals — site activations, session throughput, payer coverage — suggesting the 8-to-12 hour burden scales better than the Spravato analog implies; (3) a positive Ascend readout de-risking the MDD franchise; or (4) a credible strategic approach that puts a category-consolidation premium into the price. Any of these tilts the probability mass toward the bull scenario and justifies closing the gap to the sell-side.

    We would move to SELL on evidence that approval or the commercial case is deteriorating: (1) a Panorama miss, or a readout weak enough to reopen the unblinding debate; (2) an FDA turn — an advisory committee convened on effect size or design, or a request for additional data; (3) launch-year uptake, once it exists, visibly undershooting the Spravato curve, signaling the session burden is binding; or (4) a surprise dilutive raise that contradicts the funded-to-2030 thesis. In those cases fair value migrates toward the low-$20s bear scenario and the current price becomes indefensible.

    The tell to watch first: Panorama in September. A confirmatory hit opens the path to a Street-level re-rating; a miss opens the path to the bear case. Nearly everything else in the story is downstream of that single readout.
