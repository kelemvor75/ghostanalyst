1Investment Thesis
  • A great product and a fast-growing business — wrapped in a busted IPO with a named AI predator and a supply overhang that isn't finished. Figma is the category-leading collaborative design platform, growing revenue ~41% to $1.16B with 80% gross margins and 131% net revenue retention. But the stock has collapsed ~87% from its $143 post-IPO peak to ~$19, and the decline is two forces stacked: (1) IPO hype-deflation and lockup-driven forced selling from a starting valuation of 40-50x sales, and (2) a genuine structural scare — Anthropic launched Claude Design in April 2026, a directly competing AI design tool. The first force is mechanical and largely spent; the second is a real, unresolved question about whether AI commoditizes Figma's core.
  • The valuation has reset from absurd to merely expensive — but the company is deeply unprofitable, and that matters at this stage. At ~8x EV/sales, Figma is far cheaper than its 40-50x IPO multiple, and the analyst consensus ($33) sits 71% above the price with even the low target ($25) above it. But operating margins are -41%, ROIC is -96%, and the AI-credit pricing transition cut FY2026 operating-margin guidance to 8% from 12%. Unlike a profitable franchise on sale, Figma is a cash-burning growth story whose entire value rests on future monetization — and the market has limited patience for that profile in a higher-rate, AI-disruption-fearful tape.
  • Real overhang, real activist, real competition — we see a HOLD, not a falling-knife BUY. Three specific risks keep us cautious despite the cheap-looking multiple and bullish consensus: a $6B+ low-cost-basis share unlock in August 2026 (two months away) that creates persistent supply pressure; 36.8% short interest signaling deep skepticism; and the Anthropic/Claude Design threat, which activist Findell Capital has explicitly flagged to the board. The business may well defend its moat (switching costs are real), but at an unprofitable 8x sales with a known supply cliff ahead, the risk/reward does not yet favor stepping in. We rate HOLD and would revisit after the August unlock clears.

    4Price Action & The Two-Force Decline

    Figma's chart is a textbook busted-IPO pattern. It priced at $33 in July 2025, opened at $85, closed its first day at $115.50, and ran above $140 — a valuation of 40-50x annualized sales driven by scarcity of quality software listings and AI-design excitement. It has since fallen ~87% to ~$19. Critically, this is only ~11 months of trading history, so 'all-time' ranges reflect the IPO era, not a long franchise record.

    The decline stacked two distinct forces. First, IPO mechanics: staggered lockup releases (September 2025, January 2026) and an extended lockup covering 54% of Class A shares created waves of insider supply, compounded by hype-deflation from the absurd starting multiple. Second, a structural scare: the November Q3 report showed growth decelerating (41%→33%), and in April 2026 Anthropic launched Claude Design — a directly competing AI design tool — alongside a board member's abrupt resignation. The first force is mechanical and largely spent; the second is the real debate.

    The revenue trajectory shows the business never stopped growing — quarterly revenue rose from ~$250M to $333M even as the stock collapsed. This is the crux of the bull case: the price action reflects supply mechanics and sentiment, not a business in decline. But the bear case is equally grounded — the growth is decelerating, the company is deeply unprofitable, and the AI threat is now embodied in a specific competing product from a well-funded rival.

    5The Central Debate: Defensible Moat vs. AI Commoditization

    Per our framework, a multi-phase decline demands distinguishing structural impairment from an overshoot. Figma is genuinely two-sided, and the honest answer requires weighing a real moat against a real, named threat — so we lay out both.

    The bear case (AI eats design). Anthropic's Claude Design (April 2026) is a directly competing AI tool from a well-capitalized rival — and the threat is that generative AI lets anyone produce designs and functional code from prompts, collapsing the need for a dedicated design tool. Figma is deeply unprofitable (-41% operating margin), and its shift to AI-credit pricing has already cut margin guidance (12%→8%), introducing revenue unpredictability. A $6B+ low-cost-basis share unlock in August 2026 guarantees more supply. With 36.8% short interest, the market is betting the moat erodes faster than Figma monetizes AI. An activist (Findell) is pressing the board and explicitly questioning the Anthropic relationship.
    The bull case (the moat holds, and AI expands it). Figma's moat is collaboration and workflow integration, not the drawing canvas — entire product teams (designers, PMs, engineers) live in Figma, with deep integration into the development stack and real switching costs (131% net retention proves expansion, not churn). AI is being embedded as a feature (Figma Make generates layouts and code inside the existing workflow), and the credit-based pricing converts AI usage into a new monetization meter rather than a threat. Revenue grew 41%, the company raised its forecast on AI-driven conversion, and at ~8x sales with $1.64B cash, the downside is cushioned. Consensus sees 71% upside.

    Our read: this is a closer call than ADBE, and it lands on HOLD rather than BUY for three concrete reasons. First, profitability — Figma is burning cash at -41% margins, so unlike a profitable franchise on sale, its value depends entirely on a monetization future that AI-pricing is currently making less predictable. Second, the named threat — Claude Design is not a vague 'AI could disrupt' worry; it is a specific competing product from a rival with deep AI capabilities, and even the activist wants the board to examine the Anthropic relationship. Third, the supply cliff — a known $6B unlock in August 2026 is a mechanical headwind two months away. The business may well defend its position, and the consensus may be right about the upside, but the risk/reward at an unprofitable 8x sales with these overhangs does not justify stepping in front of the August supply. We would rather pay up later with the overhang cleared and AI-monetization evidence in hand.

    6Business Model & Competitive Position

    Figma sells seat-based subscriptions to its browser-based, collaborative design platform — Figma Design (the core canvas), FigJam (whiteboarding), Dev Mode (designer-developer handoff), and Figma Make (AI generation). The model's strength is that it is the system of record for product design: entire cross-functional teams collaborate in real time inside Figma, which drives the 131% net retention and makes it sticky at the team and enterprise level. Gross margins of ~80% are excellent; the problem is operating expense — R&D over $1B and stock-based compensation of ~$1.36B in 2025 drive the deep operating losses.

    The competitive picture has two tiers. Against traditional design tools (Adobe XD, Sketch), Figma already won — collaboration was the killer feature. The new threat is AI-native design: Anthropic's Claude Design, plus AI features from other large players, and the broader question of whether prompt-to-design collapses the category. Figma's defense is that design is a team workflow, not a single-output task — the collaboration, version control, design systems, and developer handoff are hard to replicate with a prompt. The pricing transition to AI credits (enforced March 2026) is the strategic bet: monetize AI usage as a feature rather than be disrupted by it. Whether that defense holds is the entire investment question.

    8Management, Ownership & The Supply Overhang

    Figma is led by co-founder/CEO Dylan Field, who controls super-voting shares — concentrated founder control that is common for recent tech IPOs but limits governance flexibility (a point the activist raises). The balance sheet is a genuine strength: $1.64B cash against just $60M debt gives Figma years of runway to fund its losses and AI investment without needing to raise capital. That cash cushion is why Altman Z is 4.73 (Safe) despite the deep operating losses — solvency is not the risk; dilution and disruption are.

    The August 2026 unlock is the single most important near-term variable. Over $6B of low-cost-basis shares are scheduled to become sellable in August 2026 as the extended lockup (covering ~54% of Class A shares) lapses. For holders who acquired stock at single-digit or low-double-digit cost basis, ~$19 is still a large gain — so the incentive to sell into any strength is high. This is a known, dated, mechanical supply headwind roughly two months away. Combined with 36.8% short interest and ongoing insider selling (31 disposed vs 8 acquired this quarter), the supply/sentiment setup argues for patience over urgency.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    We would move to BUY if: (1) the August 2026 unlock passes without crushing the stock (supply absorbed), removing the biggest mechanical overhang; (2) a quarter or two shows AI-credit monetization driving revenue with stabilizing or improving margins, proving the pricing transition works; (3) net retention holds above ~125% despite Claude Design, demonstrating the moat is intact; or (4) the stock falls toward $15-16 (the low end of our model), where the risk/reward improves materially.

    We would move to SELL if net retention drops sharply (signaling AI competition is winning), growth decelerates below 20%, or the AI-pricing transition drives revenue volatility without margin improvement. The thesis is that Figma is a strong business facing genuine but possibly-survivable threats, fairly valued, with a known supply cliff — a fast-deteriorating fundamental picture (especially on retention) would tip it from 'wait' to 'avoid.'

    14Rating & Conclusion

    Figma is a genuinely excellent product and a fast-growing, high-gross-margin, category-leading business with a fortress balance sheet ($1.64B cash, almost no debt) and strong customer expansion (131% net retention). The ~87% decline from its IPO peak is, in large part, a mechanical unwind of an absurd 40-50x starting multiple plus lockup-driven forced selling — not evidence that the business is failing. The consensus sees 71% upside, with even the lowest target above today's price.

    But this is not a clean 'quality on sale' BUY, and we will not pretend it is. Figma is deeply unprofitable (-41% operating margin, ROIC -96%), and its AI-credit pricing transition is making near-term revenue and margins less predictable, not more. It faces a specific, named AI competitor in Anthropic's Claude Design — a threat concrete enough that an activist investor wants the board to examine the relationship. And a known $6B+ low-cost-basis share unlock arrives in August 2026, roughly two months away, atop 36.8% short interest. The business may well defend its moat and the consensus may be right — but the risk/reward at an unprofitable ~8x sales, with a supply cliff and an unresolved disruption threat, does not justify stepping in now.

    HOLD — conviction 44/100, target $22 (+14%). A strong business at a reset valuation, but too many unresolved overhangs to chase: deep unprofitability, a named AI competitor (Claude Design), 36.8% short interest, and a $6B share unlock two months out. We would rather reassess after the August unlock clears and a few quarters show whether AI-credit monetization works and the moat holds against AI-native competition. This is a 'watch closely, wait for evidence' name — the upside is real if Figma proves it's an AI winner, but the burden of proof sits squarely on the next two to three quarters. Buying before the August supply, in front of a named AI threat, at negative margins, is not a risk/reward we'd take today.
