    5TCB vs Hybrid Bonding: The Distinction the Market Is Missing

    The entire advanced-packaging investment debate hinges on a technical distinction that is frequently blurred. Both TCB (thermo-compression bonding) and hybrid bonding are methods of stacking and connecting chips — but they are at different points on the adoption curve, and ASMPT and BESI are positioned very differently across them.

    TCB is the technology of the present; hybrid bonding is the technology of the (uncertain-timing) future. TCB uses fine solder/copper micro-bumps with heat and pressure — it is in volume production today for HBM3E, HBM4, and advanced logic. Hybrid bonding eliminates bumps entirely (direct copper-to-copper) for finer pitch, but faces the yield, cost, and adoption-timing hurdles detailed in our BESI report. ASMPT is the TCB market leader (≈146% segment growth in 2025, HBM4 design wins) — meaning it leads the technology HBM makers are actually buying right now. BESI is the hybrid-bonding leader — the technology that wins later, on a contested timeline.

    This reframes the competitive picture entirely. The market treats BESI's hybrid-bonding 'monopoly' as the prize and ASMPT as the laggard. But for the next several years, TCB is where the HBM and advanced-logic bonding revenue actually is — and ASMPT dominates it. ASMPT's CEO confirmed TCB leadership with HBM4 12H orders from multiple customers and HBM4 16H development leadership. The company estimates the TCB addressable market growing from ~$760M (2025) to ~$1.6B (2028), a 30% CAGR, and targets 35-40% share.

    And ASMPT is not absent from hybrid bonding — it is the credible #2. ASMPT is shipping hybrid-bonding tools and developing a 2nd-generation platform with improved precision and throughput. So the structure is not 'BESI monopoly vs ASMPT nothing.' It is: ASMPT leads TCB (today's revenue) and is the fast-follower in hybrid bonding (tomorrow's optionality), while BESI leads hybrid bonding (tomorrow) but lacks ASMPT's TCB franchise and diversification. Owning ASMPT is owning both sides of the bonding transition; owning BESI is a concentrated bet on one side arriving on schedule.
    6Business Model & Competitive Position

    ASMPT operates two segments. SEMI Solutions (the growth engine) makes bonding and assembly equipment — TCB, die bonders, hybrid bonding, wire bonders — for logic, memory, photonics, and CPO. SMT Solutions (surface-mount technology) makes placement equipment for circuit-board assembly, including AI server boards and power packages. The SMT business adds scale and cash flow but carries lower margins and more cyclicality (currently soft in automotive/industrial), which is the primary reason ASMPT's blended gross margin (~40%) sits well below BESI's pure-play packaging margin (63-65%).

    This diversification is the crux of the bull/bear debate on ASMPT versus BESI. The bear view: the SMT drag and lower margins justify a discount, and ASMPT is a 'jack of all trades' versus BESI's focused excellence. The bull view (ours): a ~10x EV/Sales discount vastly overcompensates for a margin difference, ASMPT's TCB leadership is more valuable near-term than the market credits, and the breadth is a feature — it hedges the hybrid-bonding timing risk that a pure-play cannot. ASMPT also won the Intel Epic Supplier Award for 2026 and partners with IBM on chiplet bonding methods, validating its technical depth.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    We would move to STRONG BUY if: (1) ASMPT's hybrid-bonding Gen-2 platform wins meaningful share at a major HBM maker, proving the optionality is real and narrowing BESI's monopoly; (2) the valuation gap to BESI begins closing as the market recognizes TCB leadership; or (3) SMT recovers, lifting blended margins and removing a key bear objection.

    We would move to HOLD if hybrid bonding displaces TCB faster than expected (compressing ASMPT's core franchise before its Gen-2 platform gains share), if SMT softness deepens materially, or if the stock re-rates sharply higher without fundamental confirmation. The thesis rests on TCB durability through the HBM4 cycle plus credible hybrid-bonding optionality, all at a value multiple — a faster-than-expected hybrid-bonding transition is the primary risk to that logic, which is precisely why we hold BESI as the paired position rather than avoiding the theme entirely.
