Investment Thesis
The Irreplaceable Link in the Aerospace Supply Chain. ATI produces the titanium alloys, nickel superalloys, and specialty metals that go into every jet engine, every airframe, and every defense platform built in the Western world. Aerospace and defense now represents over 70% of revenue, with record A&D sales in every recent quarter.[1] These aren't commodity metals — they're proprietary alloys qualified on specific platforms over years, with switching costs that effectively lock customers in for decades.
Defense Spending Tailwind Is Multi-Year and Bipartisan. NATO expansion, military modernization programs, the Iran conflict backdrop, and bipartisan U.S. defense budget growth create sustained demand for ATI's materials. Every F-35, F-22, B-21, tanker, and commercial aircraft engine contains ATI alloys. The company guided FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of $975M-$1.025B, representing ~16% growth at midpoint.[2]
Margin Expansion + Capital Return = Compounder Profile. CEO Kim Fields highlighted ATI's 'differentiated capabilities, robust contractual partnerships, disciplined capital deployment, and proven ability to execute.' A new $500M multi-year share repurchase authorization (on top of $120M remaining) signals management confidence in sustainable cash generation.[3] Deutsche Bank raised its target to $185 from $150 in February.

1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

ATI has surged 75% from $83 in October 2025 to $145 today — one of the strongest moves in our defense/aerospace coverage. The stock hit an all-time high of $168 on March 2 before pulling back 15% amid broader market weakness and profit-taking. 2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
High Performance Materials & Components (HPMC): Specialty materials, precision forgings, parts and components for jet engines, airframes, landing gear, and defense systems. This is the high-margin growth engine — margin expansion driven by mix shift toward complex, higher-value titanium and nickel alloy products. HPMC serves the most demanding applications where material failure is catastrophic.
Advanced Alloys & Solutions (AA&S): Nickel-based alloys, titanium alloys in plate, sheet, and precision rolled strip. Serves aerospace, defense, and specialty energy markets. Increasingly tilted toward A&D — the company has been divesting non-core operations to concentrate on high-performance applications.
Why ATI's Moat Is Real

Aerospace OEMs qualify materials suppliers through multi-year testing and certification processes. Once a specific ATI alloy is qualified on an engine or airframe platform, it remains qualified for the life of that program — often 30-40 years. The switching costs are not financial; they're regulatory and safety-driven. Boeing and Airbus cannot simply swap in a different supplier's titanium alloy without years of re-qualification. This creates a revenue base that is among the most predictable in the industrial sector.

3  |  FINANCIAL MODEL & VALUATION

ATI's valuation appears stretched at 35x FY26E earnings for a specialty metals company growing revenue 7% YoY. However, the earnings trajectory is steeper than the revenue trajectory — EBITDA guidance of $975M-$1.025B represents 16% growth as mix shift toward higher-margin aerospace products drives operating leverage. The stock is trading on the defense/aerospace multiple, not the industrial metals multiple, and that premium is justified by the 70%+ A&D revenue mix and multi-decade customer lock-in.

9  |  WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from BUY if:

Boeing announces further 737 MAX / 787 production delays. ATI's commercial aerospace revenue is directly tied to aircraft production rates. Persistent delays compress the growth trajectory.
Nickel prices spike above $25,000/tonne for more than two quarters. Would compress gross margins faster than ATI can pass through pricing to OEM customers.
Defense budget sequestration or post-conflict spending cuts exceed 10%. Would undermine the 70%+ A&D revenue base that drives the premium valuation.
New CFO hire is poorly received by the market. Would create transition uncertainty at a critical inflection point.
10  |  PRICE TARGET & CONCLUSION
Price Target: $177  |  Rating: BUY

Our $177 target represents 22% upside from the March 31 close of $145.46, derived from 33x FY2027E EPS of $5.05 plus a premium for the aerospace supply chain lock-in and defense spending tailwind.

ATI is the quiet compounder in a loud market. While AI semiconductor stocks dominate headlines, ATI produces the physical materials that every aircraft in the sky depends on — and those materials are qualified on platforms with 30-40 year lifespans. The 70%+ A&D revenue mix, multi-year customer contracts, $620M buyback authorization, and 16% EBITDA growth guidance create a predictable compounding profile that most tech stocks can't match.

At 29x FY2027E earnings, ATI isn't cheap in absolute terms — but it's fairly valued for a company with irreplaceable supply chain positioning, expanding margins, and bipartisan defense spending tailwinds. The conviction score of 76/100 places it at the lower end of our top quartile, reflecting the slower revenue growth compared to semiconductor names. But what ATI lacks in explosive growth, it makes up in predictability and moat depth. The 75% move from $83 to $145 is the market waking up to this. Deutsche Bank's $185 target suggests there's more to come.
