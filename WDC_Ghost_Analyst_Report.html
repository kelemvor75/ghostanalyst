1B  |  TURBOQUANT & THE HDD THESIS

TurboQuant’s 6x memory compression claim primarily targets GPU-adjacent memory (HBM/DRAM), not storage HDDs. AI training data sets still need to be stored regardless of how efficiently the model processes them during inference. The data lake use case — WDC’s core market — is largely insulated from memory efficiency algorithms. The selloff was guilt-by-association with the broader memory sector.

The HDD bear case — that SSDs will fully replace hard drives — misunderstands the economics of AI data storage. Training a single frontier AI model requires storing 10-50 petabytes of raw data. At current SSD pricing of $0.08/GB, that's $800K-$4M in storage costs per model. At HDD pricing of $0.02/GB, it's $200K-$1M. For hyperscalers training dozens of models simultaneously across hundreds of petabytes, the cost differential is measured in tens of millions per year. HDDs aren't going away — they're becoming more critical as data volumes explode.

Western Digital's post-spinoff financial trajectory tells the story: revenue has grown every quarter from $2.21B to $3.02B, gross margins have expanded from 36.4% to 45.7% — a 930 basis point improvement in five quarters — and EPS has accelerated from $1.35 to $4.73. This isn't a company in secular decline; it's a company that shed its slower-growth flash business to focus on the expanding enterprise storage opportunity.
