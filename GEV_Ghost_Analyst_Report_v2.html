1Investment Thesis
  • The $12 billion of 2026 free cash flow is customer float, not earnings. GE Vernova generated $9.9B of free cash flow in H1, and the 10-Q shows where it came from: contract liabilities, mostly down payments on gas turbine orders and slot reservations, added $13.7B, while operating cash before working capital was −$1.0B. Since January the 2026 cash guide has risen $6.75B at the midpoint, to $12.0B, against a ~$0.64B rise in the EBITDA implied by the revenue and margin guide. H2 is guided “substantially” lower: $1.6B to $2.6B by subtraction.
  • What the float buys is price, and the price lands in 2029–30 earnings. H1 gas orders were priced more than 20% above 4Q'25 orders, and the CEO expects Power's 2026 gain in margin in backlog to beat 2025's low-double-digit increase. That equipment ships mostly in 2029–30, so the earnings are more contracted than the “cyclical gas-turbine manufacturer” bear case allows. The cash is more cyclical than the bulls assume: the float grows only while new contracts outrun deliveries, and new gas contracting has slowed from 24 GW in 4Q'25 to 20 GW in Q2.
  • Fairly priced until January proves the margins: HOLD, $1,075. At $987.45 GEV trades at 40.3x FY2027E and 28.5x FY2028E consensus EPS (GAAP), and a reverse DCF on consensus earnings through 2030 returns ~$1,007 at a 9% discount rate and 4% terminal growth. Our Mar 31 BUY and $900 target played out within a quarter. We move to HOLD with a $1,075 target, 31x FY2028E consensus EPS of $34.66, 9% upside; the upgrade evidence arrives on a schedule, in January's margin-in-backlog page and at a spring Capital Markets Day.

    4Price Action & The Round Trip

    Our Mar 31 initiation (BUY, $900, at $872.90) caught the first leg: GEV rose 34.6% to a $1,174.86 closing high on Jun 30, helped by a 13.8% jump on Apr 22 when Q1 results raised guidance. Then it gave the gain back. It fell 8.7% on Jul 22, when Q2 EPS missed by 22% and the plan for 30 GW of annual gas output by 2030 revived the overcapacity debate (Morgan Stanley called it a “knee-jerk reaction”). It fell 8.6% on Sep 14, when GLJ Research initiated at Sell with a $470 target, closing at $874.76, within $2 of our initiation price and 25.5% below the high. Two days later it rose 4.8% on the Vineyard Wind settlement and Scott Strazik's remark at Morgan Stanley's Laguna conference that customers had already bought 2032 slots.

    Since the Q2 print the stock has held a closing range of $874.76 to $1,079.00 (Aug 17). The $987.45 close on Oct 1 (+3.9%) was its first above the 50-day average since Aug 17; the 50-day ($965.89) is still falling, from $1,022.84 on Sep 1, while the 200-day ($915.96) keeps rising. Relative strength: +51.1% YTD against +12.0% for SPY and +8.7% for XLI, but −11.3% over three months, behind both.

    The base is the setup. For a momentum book, a close above $1,079 breaks the post-Q2 range and reopens the path to the June high; a close below $875 turns ten weeks of basing into a failed base. The Oct 28 print, with ±9.9% priced in options, is the likeliest trigger either way.
    5The $40 Billion Float

    Start with the balance sheet, where the gas-turbine boom shows up before it reaches the income statement. Contract liabilities, the cash customers pay ahead of delivery, rose from $25.77B at Dec 31 to $39.94B at Jun 30; Power's alone went from $16.53B to $27.68B. Customer money on the balance sheet is now three times GE Vernova's $13.12B of cash (total equity is almost identical), and the 10-Q names the source: “higher down payments on orders and slot reservation agreements at Power.”

    H1 cash from operations was $10.68B, including an $11.7B inflow from working capital, $13.7B of it from contract liabilities; before working capital, and after a $0.5B voluntary pension contribution, it was −$1.0B. The guidance shows the same split: since January the 2026 free cash flow guide has climbed from $5.0–5.5B to $11.5–12.5B, while the EBITDA implied by the revenue and margin guide moved from ~$5.34B to ~$5.98B.

    Management is not hiding the timing. “We expect our free cash flow in the first half of the year to be substantially higher than the second half, as many of these slot reservations convert to orders,” the CFO said in July. The guide implies $1.6B to $2.6B in H2, after H1's $9.9B. Nor has the multi-year floor moved since January: at least $24B of cumulative 2025–28 free cash flow needs only $8.3B across 2027 and 2028 once 2025's $3.7B and the 2026 midpoint are counted. A floor is not a forecast, but it is the only multi-year free cash flow number the company has put its name to.

    2026 is the cash peak, not the run rate. A deposit float grows only while new contracts outrun deliveries. H1 added 40 GW of new gas commitments against 7 GW shipped; H2 is guided at about 20 GW while the factories run at a 20 GW annualized rate, and output steps up to 24 GW in 2028. Capitalizing 2026's $12.0B of guided free cash flow, a 4.6% yield on the market cap, capitalizes a deposit cycle.

    The best rebuttal comes from the CEO: “the change in our progress balance is still less than the change in our margin in backlog i.e., the cash is less than the incremental profit that we're booking.” The float is a by-product of pricing power. H2 gas orders should land at the high end of a 10-to-20-point price premium to 4Q'25, and Strazik expects Power's 2026 change in margin in backlog to exceed 2025's “low double digits.” That equipment ships mostly in 2029–30, which helps explain why consensus EPS climbs from $24.51 in 2027 to $57.68 in 2030.

    So the stock runs on two clocks. The earnings clock is contracted: 116 GW under contract today and, at 125 GW by year-end, “mostly sold out through '30,” with more than half of 2031 sold. The cash clock ticks with new contracting, which Strazik calls “likely to prove to be conservative” at about 20 GW for H2 while warning that GE Vernova is not “going to be at a 40-gigawatt run rate every six months.”

    Value GE Vernova on the contracted earnings, not the 2026 cash. On that basis the stock is roughly fair at 28.5x FY2028E consensus EPS, and the evidence that could re-rate it arrives on dated occasions: January's results and the spring Capital Markets Day.
    6Business Model, Segments & Competition

    Power (49% of Q2 revenue) is the engine: orders of $16.73B (+134% organic), revenue of $5.48B (+14%) and segment EBITDA of $1.03B, an 18.8% margin. Gas Power signed 20 GW of new contracts in Q2 (18 GW of slot reservations, 2 GW of orders), converted 10 GW of reservations to orders and shipped 3 GW; about 20% of the gigawatts under contract are for data centers. Services are the long tail: 130 HA turbines run today with 195 more on contract, and each gigawatt of HA generates about $0.5B of services revenue over its first 20 years. In nuclear, GE Vernova Hitachi said on Sep 29 that the NRC had issued TVA a construction permit for a BWRX-300 small modular reactor.

    Electrification is the margin story: Q2 orders of $6.35B (+66% organic, book-to-bill about 1.7), revenue of $3.64B (+68%, +29% organic) and an 18.4% EBITDA margin, up 700 bps organically, on a $40.6B equipment backlog. Data center orders topped $5B in H1, more than double all of 2025, but Strazik expects them to fall from almost 40% of segment orders to “probably more like 20% of our Electrification orders in 3Q and 4Q.”

    Wind is the drag: Q2 orders fell 40% organically, revenue fell 10% to $2.03B and EBITDA was −$275M, for −$657M in H1. Holding the roughly $400M loss guide requires a positive $257M in H2 against a Q3 guide of about break-even, which loads the swing onto Q4. The Sep 16 Vineyard Wind settlement, on confidential terms, ended the litigation.

    Competition. GE Vernova, Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Power build nearly two-thirds of the gas capacity under construction worldwide, so the 2032 pricing debate is about how fast all three add capacity. On 2028 earnings GEV's 28.5x matches Quanta and sits well above Siemens Energy's 17.3x (fiscal year to Sep 2028), although GEV's faster 41.4% growth keeps its growth-adjusted multiple below Eaton's and Quanta's.

    7Financial Model & Valuation

FY2026 is unusable: H1 diluted EPS of $19.96 includes a $3.99B pre-tax gain on remeasuring the Prolec GE stake and a $330M gain on the Proficy sale, which is why consensus FY2026 EPS ($30.47) sits above FY2027's ($24.51). Our base is FY2028E consensus EPS of $34.66, the year a 12-month holder will be pricing.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    What would move us to BUY. Any two of: a January margin-in-backlog page showing Power's 2026 change well above 2025's low-double-digit gain; a 2027 outlook with EPS at or above the $24.51 consensus and free cash flow of at least ~$6B; Q3 gas contracting of 10 GW or more with deposits still rising; or a pullback toward $880 (25x FY2028E consensus). A close above $1,079 would confirm it.

    What would move us to SELL. H2 gas contracting well short of the ~20 GW guide or softer pricing on 2032 slots; contract liabilities falling in Q3 or Q4, the float reversing before deliveries peak; Wind missing break-even in Q3; or a 2027 outlook that resets EPS well below consensus. A close below $875 would confirm the failed base.

    14Rating & Conclusion

    GE Vernova is executing about as well as an industrial can: orders up 88% organically, a $176B backlog, expanding margins in Power and Electrification, and rising prices on slots five years out. Our Mar 31 BUY captured the move from $872.90 to $1,174.86. What has changed is the composition of the bull case: the headline 2026 cash flow is a customer-financed float the company itself expects to slow, and the next leg depends on margin evidence due in January.

    The case against us is strong. 2032 slots are selling at premium prices, Q3 orders are guided “very strong,” the options book is hedged and short interest is low, so a clean Q3 could carry the stock through $1,079 before January. If the 2026 margin step-up is as large as Strazik implies, consensus 2029–30 EPS is too low. We would buy that evidence, or a pullback to about $880; we would not pay the Street's 36.7x 2028 earnings in advance of it.
