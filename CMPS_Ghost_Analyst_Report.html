1Investment Thesis

Compass Pathways has spent five years answering the question the market feared most about psychedelic medicine — can you run a clean trial when the patient can feel the drug? — and across two positive Phase 3 readouts, now reinforced by the FDA's own July 2026 guidance, the answer is largely yes. The stock has obliged, tripling off its August 2025 low as regulators handed Compass a voucher that shortens the coming review to weeks. Our disagreement with the tape is not about approval, which we think is more likely than not — it is about what comes after.

COMP360 is a genuinely de-risked clinical asset attached to a genuinely unproven commercial one. Its placebo-adjusted effect is statistically pristine but clinically ordinary; it enters a market anchored by an entrenched, now-monotherapy esketamine backed by five years of prescriber trust; and it is delivered through a longer, heavier session than the incumbent. Add a runway that stops at launch, and the risk-adjusted value of the equity lands within a few dollars of where it trades. After a 3.5× move, the easy money has been made. We initiate at HOLD, 12-month target $16.

  • Unblinding — the fear that defined the class — is largely answered. COMP360's two-trial design (placebo-controlled COMP005 + no-placebo dose-ranging COMP006) is exactly the structure the FDA's July 2026 psychedelic guidance now blesses, pre-empting the objection that sank Lykos 10–1 at panel. Both pivotals hit; benefit holds to six months.
  • The market has already paid for the good news. CMPS is up ~3.5× off its 2025 low on a presidential executive order (+47% in a day), a priority voucher compressing the FDA review to 1–2 months, and the durability data. Most of the de-risking upside is in the price.
  • The unpriced question is commercial, not regulatory. COMP360's efficacy is competitive with — not superior to — an entrenched, now-monotherapy Spravato, on a heavier 6–8h monitored session. Esketamine took ~6 years to grind to $1.7B in an empty market; COMP360 must take share from it.
  • The balance sheet funds launch, not profit. $433M reaches into 2028 — through launch, not to the Street's ~2029–2030 breakeven. With shares already +41% in H1 2026, another raise before profitability is close to structural.
  • Risk/reward is balanced, and the options market agrees. Our probability-weighted fair value is ~$16 vs a Street span of $13–$25. Long-dated options price a ~±50% move into the 2027 decision — bracketing our bull ($24) and bear ($6) almost exactly. That symmetry is a HOLD.
    • The differentiated call: Consensus is asking “will it be approved?” and answering yes.     2What COMP360 Is — and How It Reaches Revenue

    COMP360 is a synthetic, proprietary formulation of psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression (TRD) — major depression that has not responded to at least two prior antidepressants, a diagnosis carried by ~4 million Americans. It is not a daily pill: it is one or two 25 mg doses, three weeks apart, each inside a 6-to-8-hour monitored session with a trained facilitator. That delivery model is the single most important fact about the company, because it defines both the moat and the bottleneck.

    COMP360's Revenue Architecture is therefore unusual: Compass does not sell a prescription, it sells a supervised procedure. Revenue depends on FDA approval and DEA rescheduling (psilocybin is Schedule I today), the build-out of certified sites with trained monitors, and payer reimbursement for the session. Management's bull case is that the hard infrastructure already exists — ~8,000 interventional-psychiatry sites, growing ~500 a quarter, equipped for multi-hour treatments by the ketamine/Spravato era — and that ~90% of interventional psychiatrists say they would prescribe within a year of launch, with 1,000+ facilitators already trained. After a ~30% workforce cut in 2025, Compass halted non-COMP360 preclinical work; this is a near-single-product story, and the product is a procedure — which is precisely why the commercial question, not the clinical one, is the crux.

    3The Clinical Package: Statistically Clean, Clinically Competitive

    Both pivotals met their primary endpoints, and that is not in dispute. COMP005 (June 2025) beat placebo by 3.6 MADRS points at Week 6; COMP006 (February 2026) showed a 3.8-point separation of 25 mg over a 1 mg comparator, with July 2026 data confirming durability to six months after just one or two doses. Rapid onset, durability, and a two-dose schedule are real advantages.

    The bull and bear cases live in the same numbers. Statistically it is pristine — the first classic psychedelic to hit two Phase 3 endpoints cleanly, with serious adverse events of ~2–6%, most events mild-to-moderate and resolving within a day, and suicidal ideation under 1%. Clinically, the magnitude is ordinary: a 3.6–3.8 point placebo-adjusted delta clears significance but sits in the same range as approved esketamine, not above it — and responder rates of 25% and 39% mean that even on active drug, most patients did not reach a clinically meaningful reduction. COMP360 works, robustly and durably, but it is not a step-change over what a psychiatrist can already prescribe.

    Takeaway: Statistically clean, clinically competitive. The efficacy is a peer of Spravato's, not a leap beyond it — which matters more for the commercial thesis than the regulatory one.
    4The Regulatory Path and the Ghost of Lykos

    The obvious bear anchor is Lykos Therapeutics: in 2024 an FDA panel voted 10–1 that its MDMA-assisted PTSD therapy's benefits did not outweigh its risks, and the agency issued a complete response letter demanding another Phase 3. The central objection was functional unblinding — when a patient plainly feels a psychoactive drug, the blind breaks and expectancy inflates the result. That is the precedent the market points to when it hesitates on Compass.

    It is a weaker analogy than it looks, and here we part with the reflexive bears. Compass engineered its program to neutralize the Lykos failure mode: a placebo-controlled study paired with a dose-ranging study that uses no true placebo, remote independent blinded raters, and firewalled dosing sessions. In July 2026 the FDA finalized its psychedelic guidance and codified exactly that complementary structure as an acceptable path — in effect writing Compass' design into the rulebook. The tailwind goes further: an April 2026 executive order directed the FDA to accelerate access and pre-positioned the DEA to reschedule on approval, and Compass holds a rolling review plus a Commissioner's National Priority Voucher carrying a 1–2 month review, with the NDA targeted for Q4 2026 and launch guided to H1 2027. Residual risk remains — the 25 mg experience is still distinguishable from 1 mg, a panel could still be convened, and DEA scheduling is a discrete step — but approval odds are materially higher than a naive “psychedelics-get-rejected” prior implies. We carry them at ~70%.

    Takeaway: The single biggest historical risk for the class — functional unblinding — has been largely defused by Compass's trial design, the FDA's own July 2026 guidance, and an expedited review. This is real de-risking, and the stock has correctly rewarded it.
    5The Commercial Reality: A Spravato Read-Across

    Here is the argument the market is not having: if approved, what is COMP360 worth commercially? The only honest benchmark is J&J's Spravato (esketamine), the first monitored-administration antidepressant and the template every psychedelic developer invokes. Spravato launched in 2019 to a “sluggish start” and only reached ~$1.7B in 2025 — six years in, with a ~$2B+ exit run-rate and consensus near $2.3B for 2026. That is the shape of a monitored-administration ramp: slow, then compounding.

    Compass' bull case is that it inherits Spravato's infrastructure — certified sites grew from ~2,800 (2024) to 7,000+ (2026) — and can ramp faster into a proven market. There is truth in that, but the same fact cuts the other way, harder: Spravato built the market as a first mover; COMP360 must now take share from it. And the incumbent is formidable — approved as monotherapy since January 2025, armed with five years of data and relationships, delivered in a shorter (~2-hour) session, and backed by a company that can out-spend Compass many times over. Against that, COMP360 offers efficacy that is a peer, not a superior, on a heavier session. A me-too-efficacy product with a heavier delivery burden, entering against an entrenched, better-capitalized incumbent, is more likely to reproduce Spravato's grind than to leapfrog it. RBC frames the opportunity at >$1.7B — roughly Spravato's current size; we think that is the right order of magnitude for an eventual peak reached over years and diluted along the way, not the blockbuster trajectory the $23 consensus quietly assumes.

    Takeaway: Spravato needed ~6 years to reach $1.7B in an empty market. COMP360 must ramp against that same incumbent on peer-level efficacy and a heavier session. Even a successful launch likely looks like a grind, not a moonshot — and the consensus prices the moonshot.

    7Valuation: Why DCF Doesn't Apply, and What rNPV Says

    Why DCF is discarded — stated as plainly as we would state a bullish one. A conventional DCF returns ~$1 for CMPS. That is not a bear signal, it is meaningless: Compass has no revenue and deeply negative free cash flow by design, so a DCF simply capitalizes the burn. For a pre-launch biotech whose entire value is optionality on a binary approval, DCF is the wrong tool, and we set it aside rather than let a false $1 anchor the debate. The right framework is risk-adjusted and probability-weighted.

    13What Would Change Our Mind

    This is a HOLD with clearly defined exits in both directions — neutral on the stock, not the science.

    We would move to BUY on evidence the ramp beats the Spravato template or the risk is removed more cheaply: (1) FDA acceptance with no advisory committee and a clean anticipated label; (2) DEA rescheduling clarity that de-risks the launch timeline; (3) early launch metrics — site activations, throughput, payer coverage — tracking ahead of Spravato's first-year curve; or (4) a pullback into the low-teens or high single digits that restores asymmetry. Any of these tilts the probability mass toward the bull case.

    We would move to SELL on evidence approval or the commercial case is deteriorating: (1) an advisory committee convened and focused on effect size or functional unblinding, echoing the Lykos pathway; (2) a complete response letter or a request for additional data; (3) launch-year uptake visibly undershooting the Spravato analog; or (4) a dilutive raise on materially worse terms than expected. In those cases fair value migrates toward the bear scenario.

    The tell to watch first: the advisory-committee decision. No panel → the path to BUY opens. A panel on effect size or unblinding → the path to SELL opens. Everything else is secondary to that single procedural fork.
