1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

RKLB surged from $24 in July 2025 to $95 in December before pulling back to $64 — a 33% decline from highs but still up 167% over 9 months. The revenue trajectory is impressive for a space company: $105M → $125M → $119M → $137M → $157M → $180M, with improving margins at every step. The pullback reflects profit-taking and macro weakness, not fundamental deterioration.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Launch Services (~40%): Electron small-lift vehicle — second-most-frequently-launched U.S. rocket. $7.5M per launch, increasing cadence toward 20+/year. Neutron medium-lift (development) will compete for larger payloads at ~$55M per launch.
Space Systems (~60%): Satellite buses (Photon platform), solar panels, reaction wheels, star trackers, separation systems, and flight software. This is the higher-margin growth engine. Customers include NASA, DoD, commercial constellation operators. Growing faster than launch.
Why Rocket Lab Is Not ‘Just a Launch Company’

Most investors see RKLB as a SpaceX competitor in launch. That’s incomplete. Rocket Lab is building a vertically integrated space company — from launch vehicle to satellite bus to on-orbit operations. The Photon platform has completed missions to the Moon (CAPSTONE) and is heading to Venus (2025). By owning both the rocket and the spacecraft, Rocket Lab can offer end-to-end solutions that pure launch providers cannot. This vertical integration is the path to sustained margins above 40%.

9  |  WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from BUY if:

Neutron first flight slips beyond 2027. Would undermine the medium-lift thesis and the path to profitability.
Revenue growth decelerates below 20% for two quarters. Would signal the market is saturating at current scale.
Cash burn forces a dilutive equity raise above 10% of outstanding shares. Would reset the per-share economics.
SpaceX offers Starship at <$30M per launch for Neutron-class payloads. Would make Neutron uncompetitive before it even flies.
