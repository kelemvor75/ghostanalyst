Investment Thesis
The Anti-SpaceX Trade That's Actually Real. Rocket Lab is the only publicly traded company with a proven orbital launch vehicle (Electron, 100% success in 2025[1]), a satellite manufacturing business generating $600M+ in revenue[2], and a medium-lift rocket (Neutron) in late-stage development. This end-to-end integration positions RKLB as the only credible second source to SpaceX.
Defense Prime Contractor Emergence. The $1.85B backlog (+73% YoY) with 37% converting within 12 months provides ~$685M in locked-in 2026 revenue.[2] The $816M SDA Tranche 3 prime contract[3] and $190M hypersonic test deal[4] demonstrate the pivot from niche launcher to defense prime — a category shift not fully priced.
Neutron Optionality Not Yet Priced. First launch targeted Q4 2026 after a stage 1 tank test failure.[5] The stock has absorbed this news. Successful qualification transforms RKLB into a medium-lift Falcon 9 competitor — a $10B+ annual addressable market.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Dual-Engine Revenue Model
Launch Services (~30% of revenue): 21 launches in 2025 with 100% mission success.[1] Electron is the second most frequently launched U.S. rocket. The HASTE suborbital vehicle extends the platform into hypersonic testing ($190M contract).[4]
Space Systems (~70% of revenue): Satellite design, manufacturing, components, and mission operations. The $816M SDA Tranche 3 prime contract is the clearest evidence RKLB has crossed from component supplier to defense prime.[3] The pending Mynaric acquisition adds laser optical comms for proliferated LEO constellations.[6]
The Neutron Question

The stage 1 tank rupture during hydrostatic testing forced a redesign and pushed first launch to Q4 2026.[5] The failure occurred during ground testing (where failures should happen), and the design modification is well-understood.

Our view: Neutron is pure optionality at the current price. The stock should be valued on Electron + Space Systems, which generates $870M+ in 2026 revenue. Neutron success would be a step-function re-rating catalyst, but the case does not require it.

9  |  WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from Buy to Hold if:

Neutron suffers another major test failure. A third significant failure would indicate systemic engineering issues.
Backlog conversion stalls. Revenue growth below 20% in any quarter without clear explanation.
Aggressive dilution without revenue. Drawing >$500M from the forward equity facility within 12 months.
SpaceX IPO at a valuation implying RKLB overvalued. If SpaceX implies RKLB should trade at 10x revenue or below.
