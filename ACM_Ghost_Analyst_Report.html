1Investment Thesis

AECOM is two businesses wearing one ticker, and in August the market repriced the whole company at the multiple of the smaller, more troubled one. A single legacy construction-management project — bid in 2019, long before today’s risk rules — absorbed a $337 million pre-tax charge that swung fiscal Q3 to a GAAP loss and forced management to cut full-year adjusted EPS guidance by roughly a third, to $3.95–$4.15 from the $5.90–$6.10 it had raised just three months earlier. The stock, already de-rating from a ~28x peak, fell to a 52-week low near $61. Underneath the wreckage sits the opposite signal: total backlog reached a record $27.8 billion, up 13% year over year, on a 1.6x book-to-burn.

That divergence is the entire debate. The professional-services core — Americas and International design, advisory, and program management — is arguably the strongest it has ever been, with net service revenue still growing, segment margins in the mid-teens, and a global win rate that keeps building visibility. The construction-management tail is the problem child, and management chose to keep it in February after a formal review, only to watch it detonate two quarters later. At $64.79, the market is valuing the combined entity at ~10.5x normalized FY27 earnings — a ~40% discount to design peers — as if the core deserves little premium over the tail.

We initiate at HOLD with a 12-month target of $72. This is a deliberately balanced call, not a hedge. The valuation reset is largely complete and the franchise is intact, which anchors downside. But for a momentum-oriented mandate there is nothing yet to pull the stock up: earnings revisions are still falling, cash conversion is broken today, the troubled project runs to mid-2027 with a live risk of a second true-up, management’s credibility took an avoidable hit at the worst possible moment, and a shareholder-litigation notice adds sentiment weight. We would rather own the recovery once it is visible than pay for it on faith.

  • Two businesses, one multiple. The market is pricing a record-backlog design franchise as if it were a construction contractor. On normalized FY27 EPS the stock trades at ~10.5x versus peers at 16–21x.
  • The charge is real; the demand signal is louder. A $337M legacy charge is a genuine execution shortfall — but backlog hit a record $27.8B (+13%) and book-to-burn ran 1.6x in the same quarter. This is a timing-and-execution problem, not a demand problem.
  • The ‘revenue decline’ is partly by design. Gross revenue fell 14% as low-margin self-perform and construction work runs off; net service revenue — the real economic top line — still grew ~4%. The optics overstate the deterioration.
  • Credibility is the binding constraint. Guidance was raised in May and cut in August; the construction unit was reviewed for sale, then retained, then blew up. The premium multiple rebuilds only after clean quarters restore trust.
  • Momentum says wait. Shares sit at 52-week lows, below the 50- and 200-day averages, with a Strong Sell downgrade freshly on the tape. Cheap and structurally sound is not a buy signal while the estimate knife is still falling.

    3Business Model

    AECOM spent more than five years deliberately becoming a different company. It sold its Management Services business, wound down self-perform at-risk civil, power, and oil-and-gas construction (now reported in discontinued operations), and repositioned around fee-based, knowledge-driven professional services: planning, consulting, architecture and engineering design, program management, and advisory. The logic was to trade the lumpy, capital-intensive, fixed-price risk of building things for the higher-return, lower-risk economics of designing and managing them — and to let the multiple re-rate toward pure-play design peers as the transformation matured.

    By late 2025 that thesis was working. At its November Investor Day the company lifted its long-term adjusted EPS growth target to 15%+ CAGR through fiscal 2029, raised its margin ambition toward a 20% exit rate by fiscal 2028, and touted proprietary AI tools driving design productivity and win rates. Management leads under CEO W. Troy Rudd and President Lara Poloni; the firm employs roughly 51,000 people and is headquartered in Dallas. Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly — a rising dividend (now $0.31 per quarter) and steady buybacks that have shrunk the share count by roughly 14% over four years.

    The economic engine. AECOM earns most of its money by billing expert hours on long-cycle public infrastructure — transportation, water, environment, buildings, and increasingly environmental remediation (PFAS) and energy transition. Roughly four-fifths of the business is now the professional-services core; the construction-management unit that caused August’s charge is the last meaningful pocket of build-risk still inside the portfolio.
    4Revenue Architecture

    AECOM reports three segments: Americas (the larger unit — U.S., Canada, and Latin America design, consulting, program and construction management), International (Europe, the Middle East, India, Africa, and Asia-Pacific design and advisory), and AECOM Capital (a small real-estate co-investment arm). The critical distinction for reading this company is between two revenue lines. Gross revenue (~$14–16B annually) includes large pass-through subcontractor costs and is deliberately shrinking as low-margin construction runs off. Net service revenue (NSR, ~$7.3B) strips those pass-throughs out and is the true measure of AECOM’s own economic activity — and NSR is still growing.

    Fiscal Q3 makes the point. Headline gross revenue fell 14% to $3.59 billion — a number that reads like decline but is substantially the intentional wind-down of self-perform and construction pass-throughs. NSR, by contrast, grew low-single digits, International NSR rose ~4% with adjusted operating margin expanding 240 bps to 14.3% on Australia and U.K. strength, and Americas — excluding the charge — still ran an ~18% margin on NSR. The construction-management unit (its Tishman and Hunt operations) sits inside the Americas segment, which is precisely why the $337M charge produced a segment-level gross loss there while International kept expanding.

    5What Happened: The Charge and the Whipsaw

    The August print was jarring because it inverted a story management had been telling with growing confidence. In May, on strong fiscal Q2 results (adjusted EPS $1.59, +27% year over year; segment margin 16.5%; a 22nd consecutive quarter of book-to-burn above 1.0x), the company raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $5.90–$6.10 and lifted its EBITDA outlook. Three months later it reported a fiscal Q3 adjusted loss of $0.50 per share against a consensus near $1.46, a GAAP loss of roughly $0.65, and negative adjusted EBITDA — then cut full-year adjusted EPS to $3.95–$4.15 and EBITDA to roughly $950 million.

    The proximate cause was concentrated and, management insists, contained: a $337 million pre-tax charge on a construction-management project bid in 2019, hit by lower subcontractor productivity, delays, and higher estimated costs to complete. The charge alone reduced EPS by about $1.99; excluding it, underlying adjusted EPS would have been near $1.49, up ~11% year over year. The company is pursuing recovery claims, but substantial completion is not expected until the second quarter of calendar 2027, and management was candid that the project “will continue to burden cash flow through the first half of 2027.” A second, smaller design-build project bid in 2020 remains a watch item.

    The self-inflicted part. In November 2025 AECOM opened a formal review of strategic alternatives for the construction-management business — including a possible sale, with Goldman Sachs advising. In February 2026 it completed that review and elected to keep and integrate the unit, folding its results into the raised guidance. Two quarters later, that same retained unit produced the charge that halved the stock. The decision to keep the risk it had flagged for divestiture is the crux of the credibility wound — and the strongest argument for revisiting a separation.
    6The Differentiated View

    Consensus reads AECOM three ways at once, and prices all three as permanent franchise impairment: a one-time charge, a “declining” top line, and a guidance cut. We think two of those three are being misread, and the third is real but bounded.

    The mispricing. Treat AECOM as a sum of its parts. The professional-services core — Americas design plus International plus program management — is a mid-single-digit NSR grower with mid-teens and rising margins, a record and growing backlog, a design pipeline up more than 20% for three straight quarters in the Americas, and secular tailwinds in water, environmental remediation, and AI-enabled design. Stand-alone, that is a franchise peers such as Jacobs, Stantec, and Tetra Tech command 16–21x forward earnings for. The construction-management tail is worth far less and carries the tail risk. At ~10.5x normalized FY27 earnings, the blended stock implies the market assigns the core almost no premium to the tail. Either a clean separation or simply a run of uneventful quarters would begin to close that gap.

    Why we still land at HOLD, not BUY. We flag the bull case and the bear case with equal force, because both are genuine. The bear case is not that demand is weak — it plainly is not — but that the very thing that justified a premium multiple, predictability, has a fresh and self-imposed crack. Guidance whipsawed by nearly $2.00 of midpoint in one quarter. Free cash flow fell ~79% year over year and the problem project is a ~$185 million cash use that bleeds into 2027. A second true-up is a live possibility on a contract that runs another year. And a shareholder-law-firm investigation — allegations only, no established wrongdoing, and a routine pattern after any sharp drop — adds a sentiment overhang that lingers regardless of merit. Multiples do not re-rate while trust is being rebuilt and cash is still leaking. The reset in valuation caps the downside; the absence of momentum, cash proof, and restored credibility caps the upside. That is the definition of a HOLD.

    The one-line reverse read. At an enterprise value near $10.9 billion, the market is implying that AECOM’s core barely grows and its margin-expansion program stalls — an outcome at odds with a record backlog, a 1.6x book-to-burn, and 240 bps of International margin gain. The bar embedded in the price is low; the catalyst to clear it is simply time and clean execution.
    7Financial Model & Valuation

    We anchor valuation on normalized earnings power rather than the charge-depressed FY26 figure, because the $337M hit is non-recurring and distorts every trailing multiple and the free-cash-flow line. Consensus places FY26 adjusted EPS near $3.98 (post-cut), rebounding to roughly $6.17 in FY27 as the charge annualizes out and delayed project starts convert. We treat ~$6.00–$6.20 as the normalized FY27 base, with the recovery’s slope — not its existence — the real uncertainty.

    A full discounted-cash-flow model is inappropriate here: current free cash flow is distorted by the project charge and working-capital drag, so any DCF would either flatter or punish the business on a temporary number. We therefore weight relative valuation. Applying ~12x — a deliberate discount to AECOM’s own five-year forward average near 20x and to today’s peer median around 18x, reflecting the credibility and execution overhang — to a normalized ~$6.00 yields our $72 target.     8Competitive Positioning

    AECOM competes in a consolidating global market for infrastructure design and advisory against Jacobs, Stantec, Tetra Tech, WSP, AtkinsRéalis, and — on the heavier construction end — Fluor. Its edge is scale and breadth: it holds top-tier positions across transportation, water, and environmental consulting (an estimated ~20% share of U.S. environmental consulting), a record backlog that dwarfs most peers, and a design franchise now augmented by proprietary AI tooling. The recent global win drumbeat — the new Lisbon Airport preliminary design, Queensland rail, Ottawa’s Alexandra Bridge, a nationwide U.S. Department of Homeland Security engineering award, and multiple U.K. framework wins — demonstrates that competitive position is intact and, if anything, widening.

    11Technical & Price Action

    The tape is unambiguous and, for a momentum mandate, disqualifying for now. Shares are ~52% below the October 2025 peak of ~$133, trading around $65 after basing off a $61 low, roughly 6% below the 50-day and 26% below the 200-day moving average. The decline has been multi-phase: an initial de-rating from a stretched ~28x peak through early 2026, followed by the guidance-cut leg in August. Momentum indicators, price-target revisions, and a fresh Strong Sell downgrade all point the same direction.

    What we want to see before the technical layer turns constructive is straightforward: a reclaim of the 200-day average (currently in the mid-$80s), a stabilization and upturn in consensus estimates, and volume confirmation on up-days rather than the elevated volume that has accompanied the declines. A base can form quickly once the estimate cuts exhaust themselves, but it has not formed yet.

    13Rating Rationale & What Would Change Our Mind

    We initiate at HOLD, price target $72. The rating reflects a genuine equilibrium: a de-rated, structurally sound professional-services franchise trading at a large discount, offset by a broken near-term cash profile, a dented reputation for predictability, an unresolved legacy project, and no momentum. We are not paid to own it here, and we are not paid to short it here.

    This is an explicitly two-sided view, and we hold it loosely. The analyst sentiment is instructive: the sell-side consensus remains constructive — 16 Buy, 8 Hold, 1 Sell, with a mean target near $85 — even as the tape prices deep skepticism. That gap is the opportunity and the warning. If the recovery estimates prove right, the stock is materially undervalued; if the charge was a symptom rather than a one-off, the Buy ratings are anchored to a number that keeps falling. We resolve the tension by waiting for evidence.

    What Would Change Our Mind. We would move to BUY on either (a) a construction-management separation that removes the tail risk and lets the core trade with pure-play peers, or (b) two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow with no new CM charge, estimates turning up, and the price reclaiming its 200-day average. We would move to SELL on a second material CM charge, a book-to-burn ratio falling below 1.0x, Americas core margins compressing beyond the “timing” explanation, or the shareholder investigation escalating into a filed complaint with credible specifics.

    Until one of those signposts is hit, the risk/reward is balanced and the honest call is to hold: let the valuation floor protect the downside and let clean execution — not faith — earn the re-rating. Our 12-month Price Target is $72.
