1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

SEDG has staged a remarkable recovery from $13 in mid-2025 to $52 — a 300% move from the trough. The stock crashed from $300 (2023) to $13 as European solar demand collapsed and channel inventory ballooned. The recovery began in H2 2025 as inventory cleared and quarterly revenue inflected from $170M to $340M. The stock now trades at a level not seen since early 2025.

The divergence between price action and analyst consensus is the most extreme in our entire coverage. At $52, SEDG is 52% above the $34 consensus — wider than LITE (49% above). Three analysts upgraded in March alone (BWG, Jefferies Hold→Hold, BofA Underperform→Neutral), but targets remain anchored below $44. Our view: the Street is anchored to trough estimates and will revise higher as the recovery confirms across Q1-Q2 2026.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Solar Inverters (~65%): String and power optimizer inverters for residential and commercial solar installations. The core product. Revenue collapsed during the 2023-2024 inventory cycle but has doubled from the trough.
Energy Storage (~20%): Battery storage systems integrated with solar inverters for residential and commercial applications. Growing as solar+storage becomes the standard installation.
EV Charging & Other (~15%): EV charging solutions and smart energy management. Small but provides diversification.

SolarEdge’s recovery is fundamentally about inventory normalization, not structural demand growth. The solar market didn’t disappear in 2023-2024 — installers and distributors over-ordered during the supply chain crisis, then spent 18 months working through excess stock. Now that channel inventory is normalized, sell-through is matching real demand again. The question is whether demand grows from here or merely stabilizes at pre-crisis levels.

The competitive landscape has shifted during the downturn. Chinese inverter manufacturers (Huawei, Sungrow) have gained share in Europe, and Enphase has maintained its position in U.S. residential. SolarEdge needs to demonstrate that its power optimizer architecture still commands a premium in a market where competitors have improved. The 22% gross margin (vs 30%+ historical) reflects both pricing pressure and under-absorption of fixed costs that should improve with volume.
