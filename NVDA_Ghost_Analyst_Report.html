1Investment Thesis
  • The indispensable platform of the AI era — and, unusually, not expensively valued. NVIDIA is the foundational supplier of AI compute, with an estimated ~81% share of AI accelerators and a full-stack moat (GPUs, CPUs, networking, and the CUDA software ecosystem). Despite being the world's largest company (~$5.0T), it trades at only ~23x forward earnings — because earnings have compounded even faster than the stock. This is the rare megacap where growth has outrun the multiple.
  • Demand visibility is extraordinary and still accelerating. Q1 FY2027 set records (revenue $81.6B, +85% YoY) and management guided Q2 to ~$91B — acceleration, not a peak. The CFO cited ~$500B of Blackwell + Rubin revenue visibility locked through end-2026, underpinned by hyperscalers committing ~$725B of 2026 capex (nearly double 2025). The next-gen Vera Rubin platform (with NVIDIA's first data-center CPU) opens a "$200B tab" in a new market.
  • The risks are real but bounded — China is already zeroed out. U.S. export restrictions have effectively eliminated China data-center revenue (a ~$4.5B charge plus lost revenue), and NVIDIA now excludes China from guidance entirely — meaning any re-entry is upside, not downside, to the model. The other risks (a documented sell-on-earnings pattern, AI-capex-cycle dependence, the Blackwell→Rubin handoff) are about timing and sentiment, not the durability of demand. The Street is overwhelmingly Buy with a $309 target (+49%).

    4Price Action & Revenue Trajectory

    NVDA is up ~84% over the trailing year — a strong but notably steadier climb than its higher-beta AI peers, with ~40% realized volatility (roughly half MRVL's). The May 20 Q1 beat and ~$91B Q2 guide are marked. The relatively contained volatility reflects NVDA's status as the AI sector's blue-chip anchor.

    The revenue trajectory is among the most remarkable in corporate history — quarterly revenue scaled from ~$39B to a record $81.6B in roughly a year, with data center ~90% of the total. Management's ~$91B Q2 guide signals the AI-infrastructure cycle is still accelerating rather than maturing.

    5Recent Developments & Catalyst Analysis

    Q1 FY2027 (reported May 20) — another blowout. Revenue of $81.6B (+85% YoY, +20% QoQ) beat consensus; non-GAAP EPS was $1.87. Crucially, management guided Q2 to ~$91B — well above expectations — signaling the AI cycle is accelerating. Non-GAAP gross margin is guided near 75% (recovered from the H20-charge-depressed FY26 level), a key sign pricing power is intact as Blackwell scales.

    The product roadmap — Blackwell to Rubin. Blackwell/Blackwell Ultra (GB300) is in full production ramp, and the next-generation Vera Rubin platform — combining Rubin GPUs with NVIDIA's first data-center CPU (Vera) — is on track for an H2 FY2027 ramp, promising up to a 10x improvement in performance-per-watt. CFO Colette Kress said the Vera CPU "opens a brand new $200 billion tab," targeting ~$20B of CPU revenue this year and positioning NVIDIA to challenge Intel and AMD in data-center CPUs. Jensen Huang expects NVIDIA to be "constrained throughout the entire life of Vera Rubin."

    The demand backstop. The hyperscalers (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta) collectively committed ~$725B of 2026 capex — nearly double 2025 — with the bulk flowing to NVIDIA. The CFO cited ~$500B of Blackwell + Rubin revenue visibility through end-2026. This is the deepest demand visibility NVIDIA has ever had.

    The China overhang — and why it's bounded. U.S. export rules (the April 2025 H20 ban) effectively zeroed NVIDIA's China data-center revenue, costing a ~$4.5B inventory charge plus lost forward revenue. An H200 partial reopening has been approved (under a 15% U.S. revenue-share arrangement) but no material China revenue has been recognized — Chinese buyers pulled back after guidance from Beijing. NVIDIA now excludes China from guidance entirely, so any re-entry is upside to the base case, not downside. This is the highest-variance variable in the model.
    6Business Model & Competitive Position

    NVIDIA designs the full stack of AI computing. Revenue is now overwhelmingly concentrated in data center, with several legacy franchises providing diversification:

    SegmentWhat it does~% of Rev
    Data CenterAI GPUs (Blackwell/Rubin), networking (NVLink, InfiniBand, Spectrum), and the new Vera CPU — the AI-compute engine for every major hyperscaler and enterprise~90%
    GamingGeForce RTX GPUs — the original franchise, still a meaningful business (~$16B/yr)~7%
    Professional Viz & AutoWorkstation graphics, Omniverse, and automotive/robotics platforms (DRIVE) — emerging optionality~3%

    Competitive moat — the deepest in tech. NVIDIA's advantage is not just silicon but the integrated platform: leading GPUs, a networking stack (from the Mellanox acquisition), increasingly its own CPU, and — most durably — the CUDA software ecosystem that has locked in the AI developer base over 15+ years. Competition is real and intensifying: AMD (MI-series GPUs), Broadcom and Marvell (custom hyperscaler ASICs like AWS Trainium), and the hyperscalers' own in-house silicon. But NVIDIA's ~81% accelerator share, annual cadence of leadership products, and software lock-in make it the default platform. The custom-ASIC threat is the most-watched competitive dynamic, but it has so far expanded the market more than displaced NVIDIA.

    7Financial Model & Valuation

    Unusually for a megacap at the center of a mania, NVIDIA screens as reasonably valued. The Street models EPS from ~$8.93 (FY27) to ~$12.48 (FY28) and ~$14.69 (FY29), putting the stock at ~23x FY27E and ~17x FY28E — multiples a fraction of those on slower-growing software names. The debate is not whether NVIDIA is cheap on near-term numbers (it is), but whether the out-year estimates — which embed an enormous, sustained AI-capex cycle — prove durable.

    8Management & Capital Structure

Co-founder, President & CEO Jensen Huang has led NVIDIA since 1993 and is widely regarded as one of the most effective technology CEOs of the era, having repeatedly positioned the company ahead of major platform shifts (graphics → accelerated computing → AI). CFO Colette Kress provides disciplined financial stewardship. Founder leadership with a multi-decade track record of reinvention is a meaningful intangible.

    13What Would Change Our Mind
    Bearish triggers: (1) hyperscalers cutting or pausing AI capex from the committed ~$725B pace — the single most important variable; (2) evidence custom ASICs (Trainium and peers) are materially displacing rather than supplementing NVIDIA GPUs; (3) a Blackwell→Rubin handoff stumble (yields, delays, order pauses); (4) gross margins breaking below the low-70s, signaling lost pricing power; (5) the first quarter of decelerating data-center growth without an offsetting new driver.
    Bullish confirmation: (1) hyperscaler capex guides rising further; (2) a smooth Rubin ramp with the demand "constraint" Huang describes; (3) China re-entry recognizing incremental revenue (pure upside to guidance); (4) Vera CPU traction in the $200B data-center-CPU market; (5) margins holding ~75% as Blackwell scales, confirming durable pricing power.
    14Price Target & Conclusion

    We initiate NVIDIA at BUY with a base-case target of $310 (+49%), in line with Street consensus. NVIDIA is the foundational platform of the AI era — ~81% accelerator share, a full-stack hardware-plus-CUDA moat, ~$725B of hyperscaler capex behind it, and ~$500B of locked revenue visibility through end-2026. The financial profile (85% growth, ~71% gross margins, ~63% net margins, net-cash balance sheet) is the strongest in the market. And, remarkably for the world's largest company at the center of an AI boom, it trades at only ~23x forward earnings — a multiple that is undemanding for the growth.

    The honest counterweight is cycle dependence. The bull case requires the extraordinary AI-capex cycle to persist, and the most credible risks — a capex digestion, custom-ASIC share gains, and the law of large numbers — are real. But the China overhang is already excluded from guidance (making it asymmetric to the upside), the balance sheet eliminates financial risk, and the valuation provides more cushion than any other AI megacap. Like its peers, NVDA tends to sell off on earnings despite beats — which, given the bullish options positioning, tends to create entry opportunities rather than signal deterioration. We view it as the highest-quality, best risk-adjusted way to own the AI-infrastructure theme, appropriate as a core holding with earnings dips as the preferred entry.

    Bottom line: BUY, $310 base-case target (+49%). The indispensable AI platform — dominant share, deepest moat, ~$725B of capex visibility, fortress balance sheet — trading at only ~23x forward earnings. The central risk is the AI-capex cycle, not the company; China is excluded from guidance and therefore upside. The highest-quality risk/reward in AI infrastructure. Own it as a core position; use earnings volatility to add.
