1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

NVIDIA peaked at $212 in October 2025, pulled back to $165 amid Iran conflict market pressure, and currently trades at $174 — down 18% from highs. The revenue chart tells the dominant story: $30B → $44B → $47B → $57B → $68B in four quarters. Q1 FY2027 estimates of ~$77B would extend this trajectory. The pullback has created the most attractive entry point since mid-2025.

1B  |  GTC 2026 & RECENT CATALYSTS

GTC 2026 (March 17) was the most significant product announcement event in AI hardware history. Jensen Huang revealed $1T in cumulative Blackwell and Vera Rubin purchase orders through 2027, doubling the prior $500B estimate.[1] Key announcements: Dynamo inference operating system entered production; Physical AI Data Factory blueprint for robotics and autonomous vehicles; Hyundai/Kia partnership for autonomous driving; $2B strategic investment in Marvell on March 31 to co-develop silicon photonics and expand NVLink Fusion.[2]

The Iran conflict has pressured broad tech sentiment, but NVIDIA has been relatively resilient compared to other semiconductor names. The TurboQuant memory efficiency scare that hit MU and SNDK was actually modestly positive for NVIDIA — more efficient memory means NVIDIA GPUs can do more per chip, expanding the addressable market.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Data Center (~91% of revenue): GPU accelerators (Blackwell, upcoming Vera Rubin), DGX/HGX systems, networking (InfiniBand, NVLink), and software (CUDA, Dynamo, AI Enterprise). This is a vertically integrated AI computing platform, not just a chip company.
Gaming (~5%): GeForce GPUs. Mature, stable. No longer the growth driver but provides cash flow.
Auto & Robotics (~2%): DRIVE platform for autonomous vehicles, Isaac for robotics. Early stage but growing 30%+ YoY.
Professional Visualization (~2%): RTX for design, simulation, and digital twins. Stable.
The CUDA Moat

CUDA is the most defensible moat in technology. Every major AI framework (PyTorch, TensorFlow, JAX) is optimized for CUDA first. Every AI researcher learns CUDA. Every hyperscaler has built their infrastructure around it. Switching costs are measured not in dollars but in years of ecosystem rebuilding. AMD’s ROCm is improving but remains years behind in library depth and developer tooling. This moat is why NVIDIA can sustain 73% gross margins in a semiconductor industry that typically operates at 50%.

9  |  WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from BUY if:

Revenue growth decelerates below 30% for two consecutive quarters. Would signal AI capex cycle is maturing faster than the $1T order book implies.
Gross margins fall below 65%. Would indicate competitive pressure from AMD or custom ASICs is structural.
Two or more hyperscalers announce majority custom ASIC deployments. Would undermine the CUDA ecosystem moat thesis.
Jensen Huang departs. NVIDIA’s strategy and vision are inseparable from its founder.
