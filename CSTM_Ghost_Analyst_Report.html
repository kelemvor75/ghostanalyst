1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

CSTM has surged 274% from its $7.32 one-year low to $27, driven by the aerospace production recovery, rising aluminum prices from the Iran conflict, and the Q4 earnings beat. Revenue grew 15% to $8.45B in FY2025 with earnings exploding 388% as the company’s operating leverage kicked in. The stock pulled back from $25.49 highs but remains in a strong uptrend, trading above both its 50-day ($21) and 200-day ($17) moving averages.

The Iran conflict has been a direct catalyst for CSTM. Aluminum prices have risen as energy costs spike and shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz are disrupted. Unlike pure commodity producers, Constellium operates on a cost-plus model where it passes aluminum input prices through and earns fixed conversion margins. Higher aluminum prices boost the top line without compressing margins — the ideal combination for an aluminum transformer.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
Packaging & Automotive Rolled Products (~50%): Aluminum sheet and coil for beverage cans, automotive body panels, and specialty packaging. High-volume, stable demand. Recycling operations provide cost advantage — using 90%+ post-consumer scrap reduces input costs and earns ESG premiums.
Aerospace & Transportation (~25%): Proprietary aluminum alloys (Airware® aluminum-lithium) for commercial aircraft, military platforms, space applications, and commercial transportation. Highest margin segment. Shipments of 207K metric tons in FY2025 (+8%). Strong order book supported by Boeing/Airbus production ramp and defense spending.[1]
Automotive Structures & Industry (~25%): Extruded aluminum structural components for automotive crash management systems, battery enclosures for EVs, and industrial applications. Growing with EV adoption and lightweighting trends. Singen plant expansion ($33M) ramping for battery foilstock.

Constellium’s competitive moat is built on three pillars: proprietary alloys, customer qualification, and recycling economics. The Airware® aluminum-lithium alloy platform took decades and hundreds of millions of dollars to develop. Once an alloy is qualified on an aircraft platform (a process that takes 5-10 years), switching is essentially impossible — the alloy’s mechanical properties are designed into the airframe’s structural analysis. This creates multi-decade customer lock-in with pricing power that commodity aluminum producers cannot achieve.

The recycling advantage is underappreciated. Constellium’s recycling operations process aluminum scrap at a fraction of the energy cost of primary smelting, producing alloys with less than 2 metric tons of CO2 per ton of aluminum (vs 10+ for primary). The CirConAl project, backed by $13M from the UK government, is developing next-generation low-carbon extrusion alloys. As OEMs face Scope 3 emissions pressure, Constellium’s recycled content becomes a competitive advantage, not just a cost benefit.

9  |  WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND

We would downgrade from BUY if:

LME aluminum prices decline 20%+ for two consecutive quarters. Would reduce top-line revenue even though conversion margins are stable.
Boeing or Airbus announce production rate cuts. Would directly reduce A&T segment shipment volumes.
D/E ratio exceeds 2.5x or refinancing costs spike. Would indicate balance sheet stress that limits strategic flexibility.
A major automotive OEM drops Constellium for a cheaper aluminum supplier. Would undermine the value-added positioning in auto structures.
10  |  PRICE TARGET & CONCLUSION
Price Target: $35  |  Rating: BUY

Our $35 target represents 28% upside from $27.32, derived from 14x FY2027E EPS of $2.39 plus a premium for proprietary aerospace alloys, the Iran conflict aluminum tailwind, and the EV battery enclosure growth vector.

Constellium is the aerospace materials play hiding in the basic materials sector. The Airware® alloy platform creates multi-decade customer lock-in with every major aircraft OEM. The recycling operations provide cost advantages and ESG positioning that commodity aluminum producers cannot match. At 11x FY27E with unanimous Buy consensus, 21% upward earnings revisions, and the Iran conflict driving aluminum prices higher, CSTM offers deep value with multiple expansion potential as the market re-rates it from commodity aluminum to specialty aerospace materials.

Conviction score of 72/100 places CSTM in the strong conviction band. The lower score relative to our semiconductor coverage reflects the cyclical nature of aluminum and the elevated leverage (D/E 2.0x). But the proprietary alloys, OEM lock-in, and the Iran-driven pricing tailwind create an asymmetric setup where the downside is bounded by long-term aerospace demand and the upside is amplified by aluminum price momentum.
