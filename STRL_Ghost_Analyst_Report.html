1  |  PRICE ACTION & REVENUE TRAJECTORY

STRL has surged from $130 to $407 over the past 9 months — a 213% move driven by the data center construction boom. Revenue acceleration from $430M to $760M in four quarters shows the E-Infrastructure segment inflecting sharply. The Q1 seasonal dip in revenue is normal for a construction company — Q2-Q4 are the heavy building seasons.

Sterling is a small-cap ($12.5B) with a tiny float (30.6M shares), which amplifies moves in both directions. The 213% run means the stock is priced for continued execution — any miss will be punished. But the backlog of data center site prep contracts provides multi-quarter visibility that most construction companies lack.

2  |  BUSINESS MODEL & COMPETITIVE POSITION
Revenue Architecture
E-Infrastructure Solutions (~45%, fastest growing): Data center site preparation, earthwork, concrete foundations, underground utilities. Every hyperscaler data center requires 6-12 months of heavy civil work before a single server is installed.
Transportation Solutions (~30%): Highway, bridge, and airport infrastructure construction. Funded by the 2021 Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act. Steady, predictable revenue.
Building Solutions (~25%): Residential foundations, primarily in Texas. Tied to housing starts. Provides cash flow diversification.

Sterling’s transformation from a traditional heavy civil contractor to a data center infrastructure specialist mirrors the broader market’s discovery of the physical construction layer beneath the AI stack. Two years ago, nobody valued site prep companies on AI multiples. Today, the market recognizes that you cannot install $500M of GPU racks without first spending $50-100M on earthwork, concrete foundations, underground utilities, and site grading.

The seasonal pattern in Sterling’s revenue is important context. Q1 is always the weakest quarter due to winter weather limiting outdoor construction activity. Q2-Q4 are the peak building seasons. The Q4 $760M figure should not be annualized — the FY2026 estimate of ~$3.0B properly accounts for Q1 seasonality. Investors should evaluate Sterling on full-year trends, not individual quarters.
